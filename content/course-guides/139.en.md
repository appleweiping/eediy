## The real learning spine is sensor → AFE → signal → system → safety

The sensor and transducer layer must go beyond a classification table. For at least 2 of temperature, force, pressure, and flow, record the measurand, transfer relation, range, sensitivity, loading, hysteresis, drift, and calibration method. The AFE layer carries a small differential signal through an instrumentation amplifier, filter, and ADC; every chain should state input common-mode range, CMRR, input impedance, noise bandwidth, isolation boundary, and saturation condition.

Only the third layer enters ECG, EEG, EMG, ERG, EOG, GSR, or PCG. Distinguish the physiological source from electrode or interface artifacts, motion interference, line contamination, and sampling artifacts rather than attaching an organ label directly to a waveform. The fourth layer reads ventilators, pacemakers, defibrillators, pulse oximeters, and hemodialysis equipment for their measurement and control roles. Its goal is understanding blocks, signals, and failure modes, not learning to make medical devices.

The fifth layer connects safety and regulation back to the previous 4. Isolation, leakage, grounding, EMI/EMC, and risk control determine whether an AFE can enter a medical context. Mention of IEC 60601 and FDA in the syllabus means the course intends to discuss them. It does not mean course completion certifies a device, explains every current regulation, or confers clinical authorization. Standards editions and jurisdictional rules change; real product work requires the current applicable documents and professional review.

Make that connection concrete as a failure path. Sensor drift can move a displayed value slowly; amplifier saturation can flatten an abnormal waveform; grounding and electromagnetic interference can create periodic components that resemble physiology; and software filtering can remove a warning feature. Every chain should state how the fault is detected, how the system enters a safe state, and who is authorized to interpret the output. Circuit performance alone does not turn a prototype into a clinical instrument.

## Four diagnostics separate “knows circuits” from “can read an instrument”

Diagnostic 1: draw a small-signal equivalent circuit for a resistive transducer and amplifier, then calculate loading error and calibration slope. Diagnostic 2: given a microvolt-level differential input, common-mode interference, CMRR, noise density, and bandwidth, estimate output signal-to-noise ratio and check saturation. Diagnostic 3: choose a sampling rate and anti-alias filter for a sampled waveform and distinguish baseline wander, line interference, and aliasing. Diagnostic 4: explain the source and common artifact differences among ECG, EEG, and EMG in non-diagnostic language without inferring disease from a single record.

A failure on item 1 calls for circuits, sensor loading, and uncertainty. A failure on item 2 calls for op-amps or instrumentation amplifiers, noise integration, and common-mode range. A failure on item 3 calls for signals and systems, sampling, and filtering. A failure on item 4 calls for basic physiology and medical terminology. These are EEDIY placement diagnostics, not an NPTEL enrollment test. If 2 of the first 3 fail, study ordinary measurement and AFE design before returning to the biomedical context.

## External practice uses only synthetic or public deidentified data

For runnable evidence, EEDIY recommends one unofficial data notebook. Use the PhysioNet [MIT-BIH Arrhythmia Database](https://physionet.org/content/mitdb/1.0.0/) or synthetic waveforms, recording the data version, license and citation, sampling metadata, and preprocessing. Implement baseline and noise inspection, filter response, event visualization, and an uncertainty discussion. This notebook is an EEDIY supplement, not an NPTEL lab, and must not be used for diagnosis, treatment advice, patient stratification, or a claim of medical-grade performance.

The default is no collection of new human data and no connection of homemade electrodes, circuits, or sensors to a person. Any human data, body-connected measurement, or subject recruitment requires the learner's institutional ethics process, informed consent, data governance, qualified supervision, and approved isolated medical equipment. “Low voltage” does not remove leakage, isolation, infection, or privacy risk. Do not build or drive a pacemaker, defibrillator, ventilator, medical laser, dialysis apparatus, or other life-support or high-energy device at home.

Public data is not condition-free material. Retain only fields needed for the study, avoid reidentification, follow dataset version and citation conditions, and put label uncertainty, cohort bias, and missing context in limitations. If provenance or deidentification cannot be established, return to a synthetic signal.

Keep source data read-only and make every transformation reproducible. Plot time and frequency views before filtering; afterward, inspect phase delay, edge effects, and amplitude distortion. Use only the dataset's own event definitions rather than assigning new medical meaning. The report should also explain why a method may fail across devices, sampling rates, or populations. That limitation is more important to instrument engineering than one polished plot.

## When to move to MIT HST.582J

MIT [HST.582J Biomedical Signal and Image Processing](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/hst-582j-biomedical-signal-and-image-processing-spring-2007/) publishes computational assignments and labs, emphasizing mathematical processing of biomedical signals and images with runnable feedback. NPTEL 102106669 gives the broader survey of transducers, AFEs, biopotential instruments, therapeutic and support systems, and safety or regulation. Choose NPTEL to build a first instrument map. Move to course ID 140 after understanding the measurement chain when filter, estimation, and image-processing practice is the goal.

The distinction is not simply “introductory versus advanced.” NPTEL has a thin public practice loop and a version conflict between 2 outlines. HST.582J is a 2007, computation-oriented archive and cannot replace modern device regulation or body-connected laboratory training. Select by the intended artifact, not institutional name or lecture count.
