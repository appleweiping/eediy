## The course page is Spring 2016, while most lab PDFs are from 2014

Cornell's [ECE 2200 Signals and Information](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-2200-signals-and-information) describes a Spring 2016 sophomore course: two weekly lectures, a fortnightly lab, three prelims, one final, and McClellan, Schafer, and Yoder's *Digital Signal Processing First*, second edition (2016). It best suits learners with basic signals knowledge who want a laboratory DSP pass. Most public lab PDFs say Spring or Fall 2014, so preserve the course, book, and lab dates instead of calling the archive a complete 2016 snapshot.

The sequence runs from discrete-time signals, LTI filtering, and spectral response through A/D, D/A, CTFT, DTFT, DFT/FFT, and the z-transform. Bring complex exponentials, calculus, elementary differential equations, and MATLAB or NumPy vectors. Before a lab, hand-convolve a short sequence, plot a sampled sinusoid, and explain normalization and aliasing.

## Assignments and feedback

The public [homework and exams page](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-2200-signals-and-information/ece-2200-homework-and-exams) has Homework 1–10 and Exam 1–3 plus the final, all with solutions. Draft each homework before opening its solution; take each prelim under time and classify errors by representation, convolution, frequency, sampling, transform property, or algebra. With no lecture notes, the text and problems carry the conceptual spine.

Use this order: discrete-time signals and convolution; Fourier representation and filtering; sampling and conversion; coordinated CT/DT transforms; DFT/FFT and z-transform. After each block, show one signal in time, frequency, and pole-zero views with units, sample index, Hz or rad/sample, and normalization.

## Labs and projects

The public [projects and labs page](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-2200-signals-and-information/ece-2200-projects-and-labs) lists Lab 1–5 without solutions. [Lab 1](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/files/2017/03/ece2200_lab1-17mpqlz.pdf) covers MATLAB scripts, vectorization, discrete signals, and echo. Lab 2 uses 44.1 kHz audio, Fourier coefficients, beats, AM, and spectrograms. [Lab 3](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/files/2017/03/ece2200_lab3-1619n2x.pdf) compares two-point average and difference filters in time and frequency and applies them to images.

Lab 4 studies a second-order notch IIR through poles, zeros, and frequency response, then a scalar Kalman filter. [Lab 5, Shazam](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/files/2017/03/ece2200_lab5-220hsqe.pdf), resamples to 8 kHz, builds a log spectrogram, peak constellation, fan-out pairs, and database, then matches a clip. Some handouts depend on Blackboard `.m` files, audio databases, or old MATLAB functions absent from OCW. Record each gap and reconstruct the objective on a small original dataset; do not claim the archived lab ran unchanged.

## Source Signals and Versions Make the Labs Reviewable

For every lab, retain code, input provenance, sample rate, raw and processed signals, unit-labeled plots, and one rerun command. A Shazam substitute needs generated or licensed signals, its feature schema, train/query split, top match, and failures—not bundled copyrighted songs. Keep Kalman and IIR equations and parameters beside empirical output.

The official Cornell homework/exams and projects/labs pages support attempts
at 10 homeworks, 4 exams, and 5 labs.
Cornell OCW supplies no lecture notes, in-person TA guidance, Blackboard,
laboratory machine, or formal grading, and its solutions cover homework and
exams rather than labs. The important caveat is the persistent version break
between the Spring 2016 course description and the mostly 2014 lab material.
