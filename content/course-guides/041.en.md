## Access and version notes

Cornell ECE 2300's [course page](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-2300-digital-logic-and-computer-organization/)
is the Spring 2016 offering. The campus course moves from transistor switches,
Boolean and combinational logic, FSMs, and timing into datapaths, pipelines,
and memory hierarchy, with 6 labs, 2 prelims, and a final. Cornell explicitly
states that homework, labs, projects, and exams are not public. Use this if you
already have exercises or a parallel lab course and want Cornell's order of
exposition; by itself it lacks the practice and feedback for a complete course.

## Course structure

The [lectures and handouts](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/ece-2300-lectures-and-handouts/)
publish 28 notes from [Lecture 1](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/files/2017/08/ECE2300_Lecture01-1pnhtjg.pdf)
to [Lecture 28](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/files/2017/08/ECE2300_Lecture28-27bycm7.pdf).
Put truth tables, minimization, timing and state, and HDL in one notebook; put
ISA, datapath/control, pipelines, memory addresses, and performance in the
other. Every processor topic should point back to muxes, registers, FSMs, and
timing constraints rather than floating above the logic design.

After each lecture, put the PDF aside and redraw its critical circuit, state, or
timing diagram from blank paper, adding the interface and one corner case.
Supplemental combinational blocks, counters and FSMs, a small datapath, and a
direct-mapped cache are useful, but they are not Cornell homework.

## Labs and projects

Feed combinational blocks into a sequential subsystem, registers and an FSM
into a datapath, and finally connect a memory model. Keep fixed lint,
simulation, synthesis, and regression commands. Beyond functional simulation,
check setup and hold, critical path, and constraints. Finally change an
instruction, reset polarity, or cache-line size deliberately; identify affected
interfaces, state, and tests before editing RTL. Handling that specification
change shows more design ability than copying all 28 slide decks.

Assess functional correctness and timing closure separately. A passing
simulation establishes intended state transitions; the synthesis report
answers the combinational-path and clock-constraint questions. For critical
registers, record launch and capture points, setup and hold assumptions, and
the slowest path. If memory latency changes, update the interface contract and
assertions first.

Use one workload for architectural comparison and inspect CPI, clock period,
stall sources, and total time. A deeper pipeline may increase frequency while
also increasing hazard penalties. Call it an improvement only when total time
and resources support that claim together. An unconstrained path, inferred
latch, or ignored constraint must send you back to RTL or the constraint file;
a higher Fmax built on an unconstrained path makes the comparison meaningless.
