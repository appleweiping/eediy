## Learn Probability and Statistics as a Connected Sequence

MIT 18.05 Spring 2022 connects probability models, conditioning, expectation,
and limit theorems to Bayesian inference, hypothesis testing, bootstrap
methods, and regression. R studios make sampling error observable. The
official [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-05-introduction-to-probability-and-statistics-spring-2022/pages/syllabus/)
requires multivariable calculus at the 18.02 level, but no prior statistics.
For independent EE study, choose it to build a complete inference chain rather
than memorize a list of tests.

The [course download](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-05-introduction-to-probability-and-statistics-spring-2022/download/)
contains both course readers, class material, R code, and studio files. There
are no public lecture recordings. The valuable structure is the sequence from
reading question to class problem to computational experiment, so an unrelated
video playlist is not a full substitute.

## Assignments and feedback

The [problem-set page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-05-introduction-to-probability-and-statistics-spring-2022/pages/problem-sets/)
publishes 11 sets with solutions, and the first 10 also have checkers. Write
the derivation, use the checker to localize a mistake, and only then read the
solution; guessing numbers bypasses the reasoning. The 10 R studios cover
simulation, Bayesian updating, intervals, tests, bootstrap methods, and
regression. Keep runnable code, a fixed seed, a figure, and a short
interpretation from each. A Python port is fine, but audit defaults such as
quantile definitions, variance degrees of freedom, and single- versus two-sided
tests instead of merely translating function names.

## Learning outcomes

The [exam archive](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-05-introduction-to-probability-and-statistics-spring-2022/pages/exams/)
contains 2 midterms, a comprehensive final, practice papers, and solutions.
Use them to test method choice. Then analyze non-sensitive repeated resistor,
voltage, or sensor measurements. State the sampling mechanism, estimand, and
model assumptions before giving intervals, effect size, and where the
conclusion does not generalize. If you give both Bayesian and frequentist
interpretations, keep their meanings distinct. A p-value alone cannot answer
the engineering questions: how large is the effect, how uncertain is it, and
would it survive another sample?
