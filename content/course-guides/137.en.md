## Course structure

Weeks 1–2 define measurand, transduction law, sensitivity, range, linearity, resolution, hysteresis, response time, and cross-sensitivity. Weeks 3–5 move from capacitive/thermal sensors to chemical, electrochemical, optical, magnetic, acoustic, and microfluidic sensing. Build a static relation and first/second-order dynamics for the selected object, then compare another principle through noise, bandwidth, drift, and packaging.

Weeks 6–7 cover microfabrication, PVD/CVD, lithography, wet/dry etching, and microscopy. Produce only a paper cross-section with critical dimensions, interfaces, residual stress, and inspection gates. Week 8 connects transduction to an instrumentation amplifier, filter, and ADC, checking signal range, source impedance, input-referred noise, gain, bandwidth, common mode, saturation, and clipping.

The same model should carry a calibration equation and uncertainty budget, referring sensitivity, offset, nonlinearity, temperature coefficient, reference error, amplifier noise, and ADC quantization back to measurand units. That common unit reveals whether electronics, quantization, or packaging drift dominates the actual error. State which terms calibration can remove and which drift with time, temperature, or packaging. A single `R²` cannot separate these sources.

## A calibration project adds practical work

The public layer has no kit, BOM, raw calibration data, code repository, build procedure, tests, or answers. The final experimental demonstration is not a reproducible lab. Use a low-voltage commercial sensor or synthetic data to build a calibration curve, separate repeatability, drift, quantization, and reference uncertainty, and inspect disconnect, saturation, and a relevant cross-sensitivity separately.

Before sampling, state the reference instrument, sampling interval, settling criterion, and repeats, and cover the operating span, rising/falling directions, and relevant environmental changes; keep fault data outside fitting. Inspect residuals for static behavior and step response/bandwidth for dynamics. A systematic residual between directions calls for discussion of hysteresis, contact, and thermal equilibrium rather than a higher-order curve that absorbs the mechanism. Synthetic data validates analysis, not real hysteresis or ageing.

The paper cross-section and readout should close on a concrete coupling. Electrode area, film thickness, and dielectric choice change parasitics/source impedance; packaging changes thermal time constant and cross-sensitivity; excitation may trade self-heating for signal. Estimate the selected coupling in the calibration residual to connect physics, fabrication, and circuits.

## Audit the Readout with Calibration and Uncertainty

Deliver the transduction equation, static/dynamic model, paper process cross-section, readout/noise budget, calibration residuals, and uncertainty. If a current cohort exposes weekly assignments, use only what the account shows; do not invent “8 solved assignments” from the public page.

Vacuum, deposition, etching, plasma, and electron microscopy from Weeks 6–7 belong in controlled facilities. Even low-voltage measurement should record reference-instrument range, calibration state, and environment. Display resolution is not sensor accuracy.

The important distinction is that display resolution, repeatability, and
sensor accuracy are never the same quantity. The calibration chain is where
they are separated.
