## Build Parameters from Fields and a Reference Sphere

The course assumes the ability to derive Hertzian-dipole far fields from a vector
potential, integrating a normalized power pattern into beam solid angle and
directivity, and fixing coordinates, polarization, feed/reference plane,
far-field region, and boundary assumptions for a dipole, slot, or aperture.
Gain, directivity, efficiency, effective aperture, and Friis must close on the
same reference sphere. Review fields and lines first if \(1/r\) fields and
\(1/r^2\) power density are being mixed.

The public lectures can be grouped into radiation/patterns, antenna parameters,
wire antennas, arrays, aperture/reflectors, generalized/MoM analysis, and
measurements. Preserve an analytic relation, dimension or power check, and
model boundary for each part. The final measurement lecture is teaching content;
the official public pages provide no lab manual, BOM, dataset, starter, or autograder.

## Use a Passive Numerical Model as the Final Exercise

The exit can be a short-dipole MoM model, linear-array synthesis, or passive
antenna/matching sweep, stating geometry, material and boundaries, frequency,
mesh convergence, pattern normalization, and an analytic cross-check,
including a failed approximation. Label it an independent numerical project, not an NPTEL
lab. [openEMS](https://github.com/thliebig/openEMS) is an active GPL-3.0
EC-FDTD solver whose repository includes Python and MATLAB tutorials for
simple patches, bent patches, and arrays. It is not an NPTEL measurement.
Change the geometry or substrate of the simple-patch model, perform mesh
refinement, and report \(S_{11}\), resonance, near-to-far pattern, and power
balance against an analytic length estimate. By default, connect no
transmitter, PA, microwave source, or antenna and do not radiate. VNA,
anechoic-chamber, or antenna-range work belongs only in an institutionally
approved laboratory under qualified RF supervision. In the numerical model,
inspect pattern sampling, integration error, and the conditioning introduced
by the grid or basis. Cross-check reciprocity, power conservation, or the
analytic short-dipole limit. If grid refinement moves the main lobe or gain,
establish convergence before discussing antenna design.

The difficult part of antenna simulation is usually not drawing a main lobe,
but showing that gain and beam shape no longer drift with mesh, domain, and
normalization. That is the method worth taking from the lectures.
