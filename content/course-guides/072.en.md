## Course fit and prerequisites

MIT [6.231](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-231-dynamic-programming-and-stochastic-control-fall-2015/) is a Fall 2015 graduate course. The [Syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-231-dynamic-programming-and-stochastic-control-fall-2015/pages/syllabus/) expects undergraduate probability—especially conditional distributions/expectations and Markov chains—and precise arguments. Each week has 2 lectures of 90 minutes and 1 recitation of 1 hour, progressing through finite horizon, imperfect information, infinite horizon, and approximate DP.

The core is not memorizing the Bellman equation. Explain why state summarizes history, when the action is selected, when randomness is revealed, and how cost accumulates. Review conditional probability if Bayes conditioning and total expectation remain formula matching.

## Assignments and feedback

[Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-231-dynamic-programming-and-stochastic-control-fall-2015/pages/assignments/) has 9 problem sets, some with solutions. Draw the decision timeline first; state the information set, state/action/noise/transition/cost, then write the recursion. Mark terminal cost for finite horizon, define a belief state under partial observation, and distinguish discounted, average, and stochastic-shortest-path assumptions for infinite horizon.

Begin with a model small enough to solve every state by hand and use solutions only for correction. Classify errors as insufficient state, conditioning, min/expectation order, boundary, or convergence conditions instead of replacing the last line. Then implement finite-horizon backward recursion, discounted value iteration, policy iteration, and rollout while retaining Bellman residuals and policy changes.

## Exams and an exact small case provide feedback

[Exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-231-dynamic-programming-and-stochastic-control-fall-2015/pages/exams/) includes the 2015 midterm and historical papers/solutions. Take the 2015 paper under time first, then revisit topics behind errors. Only after exact DP is stable should you use the [related videos](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-231-dynamic-programming-and-stochastic-control-fall-2015/pages/related-video-lectures/) for approximate material; they are not complete Fall 2015 classroom recordings.

For each approximation, separate function-class, sampling-distribution, truncation, optimization, and policy-evaluation error. Lower empirical cost in a large simulation does not prove superiority on the original model. Keep an exactly solvable small case as a regression oracle.

## Start the project at midterm

The original course used homework 30%, midterm 30%, and term paper/project 40%. [Projects](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-231-dynamic-programming-and-stochastic-control-fall-2015/pages/projects/) accepts either a critical literature study or application modeling with analytic/computational work.

Battery scheduling, queue control, or sensor selection are suitable. Define
the question, state/action/noise, baseline, and metric before comparing an
approximation and its ablations with a simulator and exact small case. Put
assumptions, seeds, raw results, mean/quantiles/runtime, constraint violations,
and failure cases in the result table. If a partially observed policy receives
the hidden state, label that run as an oracle baseline; an implementable policy
uses a belief or explicit history features.

The project's crucial argument is why the chosen variables form a sufficient
state. Try to construct histories with identical retained variables but
different optimal actions; success exposes missing information in the Markov
assumption. Under partial observation, also test belief-update normalization
and extreme observations. That counterexample often identifies the real
modeling error sooner than a larger approximator.
