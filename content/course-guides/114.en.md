## Do Not Open Lecture 1 Yet

MIT 6.622 is David Perreault’s Spring 2023 graduate course on modeling,
analysis, design, and control of power-conversion circuits. The official
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/pages/syllabus/)
names 6.2500 Nanoelectronics and Computing Systems as the prerequisite. For an
independent learner, three checks are more useful: derive the states and
volt-second/charge balance of a switching circuit; read poles, zeros, and
feedback stability from a linearized model; estimate device stress from voltage,
current, loss, and temperature. If all three are unfamiliar, I would not start
the 38-lecture sequence yet. Reviewing circuits, signals, and feedback now will
save more time than rushing through the first week.

Compared with a three-course introductory specialization, 6.622 puts
rectification, DC/DC conversion, magnetics, device loss, inverters, three-phase
systems, control, EMI, switched-capacitor conversion, soft switching,
resonance, and gate drive into one dense sequence. Use
[Power Electronics 1](115-power-electronics-1.md) first when converter
topologies are new; choose 6.622 when you want the topics in one demanding
graduate course.

## The 38 Lectures Pursue Five Design Questions

The [lecture videos](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/resources/lecture-videos/)
should not be treated as a passive playlist. Each block answers a design
question:

1. **How is power converted?** Rectification, regulation, and basic DC/DC
   topologies connect steady-state balance to ratio, ripple, and stress.
2. **How do magnetics and switches bound the design?** Inductors,
   transformers, isolation, switching loss, thermal behavior, and device
   choice replace ideal waveforms with rated components.
3. **How does a power stage become controllable?** Inverters, three-phase
   systems, small-signal models, feedback, and current-mode control must return
   to operating point, loop gain, and transient specifications.
4. **When do parasitics and compliance dominate?** EMI filtering, layout, gate
   drive, and switching transitions expose the boundary of the ideal model.
5. **How can hard-switching limits be crossed?** Switched-capacitor, soft
   switching, and resonant conversion require simultaneous energy, stress, and
   control-range analysis.

Both handwritten and typed notes are available. The typed notes are
accessibility transcriptions; the course identifies the handwritten notes as
the reference version.

## Why PS11 and Assessment 9 Need Their Own Note

Grades are Homework 40%, Assessments 50%, and Design Project 10%.
[Problem Sets](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/resources/problem-sets/)
provides PS0–PS10 with solutions. A “Problem Set 11” label appears without an
openable problem or resource. This guide therefore counts PS0–PS10 as the
complete public solved sets and does not count the isolated label as a set.

The 2023 course uses approximately weekly low-stakes assessments instead of
traditional midterms and finals. They were commonly released at 13:00 one day
and due at 23:00 the next, completed independently with a calculator and
course material but no other computing tools or references.
[Assessments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/lists/assessments/)
lists 1–10; 1–8 and 10 have solutions, while 9 explicitly does not. Take the
others under the stated time and reference limits before checking the answer.
For Assessment 9, use an independent derivation, simulation, or peer review;
do not substitute an unattributed answer found elsewhere.

## Calculations and Waveforms the Buck Report Needs

The official
[Design Project](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/pages/design-project/)
specifies a buck converter: size the power stage and input/output filters,
select power devices and thermal management, design the inductor and
capacitors, and produce a feedback controller that remains stable and meets
static and transient specifications across input and load range. Mathematical
controller design and simulation sufficient to support the conclusions are
allowed, and the project page supplies relevant data sheets.

Before accepting the report, I would look for five things:

- an input/output, ripple, transient, thermal, and protection specification;
- steady-state duty ratio, ripple, peak/RMS stress, and magnetic-component
  derivations;
- component choices with loss model, rating margin, and thermal path;
- operating point, small-signal model, compensator, gain/phase margins, and
  load-step response;
- critical waveforms, corner cases, model limits, EMI risks, and a design
  review.

All five come from the official buck assignment, not a generic converter
exercise added here. Part numbers, supply, and price change; a 2023 data sheet
explains that offering but does not replace a current procurement check.

## Do Not Turn It into a Home High-Power Lab

The public course structure does not include a hardware-build laboratory that
an outside learner can reproduce. The public material supports analysis,
design, and simulation, but not a reproducible home hardware laboratory.
Without isolation, current limiting, differential probes, thermal design,
qualified supervision, and a discharge procedure, do not build mains,
offline-rectified, high-voltage, high-current, battery-pack, or
energy-storage converters.

The [course home](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/)
is open without an institutional account; the offline package does not include
audio or video. The public material is already enough for analysis, design,
and simulation. If a physical prototype is built later, record equipment,
energy level, isolation, current limiting, and supervision with the waveforms.
Without that information, another person cannot review what happened during
power-up.
