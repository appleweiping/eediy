## ECE 3250 asks where an operation is actually defined

Cornell [ECE 3250 Mathematics of Signal and System Analysis](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3250-mathematics-of-signal-and-system-analysis) preserves David F. Delchamps's Fall 2015 junior course and names no required text. It suits learners who know ordinary signals and systems but want to ask when each operation is valid. Sets, mappings, cardinality, modular arithmetic, and public-key cryptography establish the language before convergence, signal spaces, convolution, LTI, Hilbert and Fourier analysis, wavelets, sampling, DFT/FFT, z and Laplace transforms, and SVD.

The official [syllabus](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3250-mathematics-of-signal-and-system-analysis/ece-3250-syllabus) distinguishes \(l_1,l_2,l_\infty\) from \(L_1,L_2,L_\infty\), asks when convolution exists, ties causality and BIBO stability to impulse response, and treats Fourier series as Hilbert-space expansion. Bring calculus, complex numbers, matrices and eigenvalues, routine signals work, and a willingness to prove or refute claims; an FFT call is insufficient preparation.

## Cross-Read the Forty Lectures with the Monograph

The public [lecture notes and handouts](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3250-mathematics-of-signal-and-system-analysis/ece-3250-lecture-notes-and-handouts) provide a complete [course monograph](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/files/2017/03/ece3250_monograph-2f8g8v7.pdf) and 40 videos. Lectures 1–7 cover sets, mappings, number systems, primes, modular arithmetic, Diffie–Hellman–Merkle, RSA, and Cauchy sequences; 8–16 build signal spaces, CT/DT convolution, LTI, causality, and BIBO. Lectures 17–26 treat Hilbert spaces, Fourier analysis, AM, and wavelets; 27–35 cover DTFT, sampling, DFT, and FFT; 36–40 close with z transforms, stability, SVD, least squares, and pseudoinverses.

For each lecture, record object, space, operation, existence condition, and conclusion. Establish convolution before using it for LTI behavior; state inner product and completeness before Fourier expansion; write spectral assumptions before sampling; distinguish exact solution, minimum residual, and minimum norm in SVD least squares.

## Assignments and feedback

The official [homework and exams page](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3250-mathematics-of-signal-and-system-analysis/ece-3250-homework-and-exams) lists Homework 1–11 and Exam 1–2 without solutions or labs. Check each first draft by type and dimension, limiting cases, a numerical experiment, and an independent derivation. A numerical counterexample can refute a claim; matching samples cannot prove it.

Work in four blocks: foundations and cryptography; signal spaces, convolution, and LTI; Hilbert, Fourier, and wavelets; sampling, transforms, and SVD. After each, select closed-book homework questions and compare scope with the two exams. Without keys, use peer proof review or a delayed review from theorem assumptions.

## Use computation to seek boundaries and counterexamples

A companion notebook can compare \(l_1,l_2,l_\infty\) sequences, expose convolution outside absolute integrability, contrast Fourier and wavelet behavior, and perturb singular values to observe conditioning. State the prediction, finite truncation, normalization, and evidentiary limit.

The unusually strong parts of this archive are the complete monograph, continuous lecture sequence, homework, and exams; the missing pieces are solutions, projects, labs, TA feedback, and a grader. Cryptography appears here as a mathematical example, not as training in modern secure implementation, and the historical algorithms should not be reused in a real key system.
