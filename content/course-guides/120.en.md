## Course fit

MIT [6.685 Electric Machines, Fall 2013](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-685-electric-machines-fall-2013/pages/syllabus/) is James Kirtley's graduate course and lists 6.061/6.690 Introduction to Electric Power Systems or permission as the prerequisite. Before starting, perform three-phase phasor and per-unit conversions, derive force or torque from reluctance, flux, and coenergy, and explain slip, power flow, and a torque-speed curve from an induction-machine equivalent circuit. If both three-phase power and magnetic circuits are weak, take power systems first. If steady state is sound but Park transforms and state equations are new, begin while reserving extra time for dynamics.

## Read 12 note chapters through the 24-lecture dependency

The [Course Notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-685-electric-machines-fall-2013/pages/course-notes/) have 12 chapters, but the site warns that chapter order does not necessarily match lecture order. Following the calendar, group the work into fields, energy, and materials; synchronous machines and Park transforms; induction, DC, and reluctance machines; dynamics and drives; and permanent-magnet and BLDC machines. At the start of each block, state phase versus line, peak versus RMS, mechanical versus electrical angle, and positive torque, then test an air-gap, slip, or d/q limit. Machine equations can be symbolically consistent and still fail through mixed conventions.

All Problem Sets 1–11 on [Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-685-electric-machines-fall-2013/pages/assignments/) have solutions. PS1–3 cover force, magnetic circuits, and loss. PS4–7 cover synchronous machines, windings, induction machines, and DC machines. PS8–11 move through reluctance, dynamic simulation, drive control, and PM/BLDC machines. Redraw the magnetic or equivalent circuit before calculating and check power balance and limits. The public [Exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-685-electric-machines-fall-2013/pages/exams/) page contains only two solved 1.5-hour quizzes. The 3-hour final named in the syllabus is absent, so the public exam record is incomplete.

Make at least one explicit energy check between fields and circuits. Derive force or torque by differentiating magnetic coenergy with respect to position or angle, then build an independent budget from air-gap fields and terminal power. For a synchronous machine, put electrical angle, pole pairs, and mechanical speed on one line. After the Park transform, fix the abc-to-d/q sign, scaling, and rotating-frame direction, verify constant d/q quantities in balanced steady state, and check preservation of instantaneous three-phase power.

The induction-machine equivalent circuit, torque-speed curve, and dynamic state model should share parameters. Change rotor resistance, inertia, or load torque and predict the directions of starting current, breakdown torque, slip, and transient settling before running a model. If the dynamic final point misses the steady-state curve, inspect base quantities, leakage conventions, and the mechanical equation before tuning the solver.

## Modeling workflow

The Aalto Electric Drives team's unofficial [motulator](https://github.com/Aalto-Electric-Drives/motulator) is an MIT-licensed Python motor-drive and grid-converter simulator with a DOI, documentation, tests, and induction, synchronous-reluctance, and permanent-magnet synchronous-machine examples. It is not 6.685 code or an answer key. Use it to map course equations into continuous-time machine models and discrete controllers and to compare startup, load steps, and solver settings. State parameters, abc↔d/q conventions, and power residuals independently.

## Compare Two Machine Types at Matched Ratings and Loads

The official archive publishes no lab or project. Using parameters from course problems, build steady-state and dynamic models for one synchronous and one induction machine. Plot power-angle or torque-speed behavior, one startup or load step, loss and efficiency, and parameter sensitivity. Cover no-load, rated, and overload for the synchronous machine and low, nominal, and high slip for the induction machine. Put energy or power residuals beside the figures and confirm that the transient survives a change in step size or solver.

The public route does not require energizing a three-phase motor. Physical machines, VFDs, DC buses, and rotors require rated protection, isolation and current limiting, emergency stop, mechanical guarding, and professional supervision. The released problem sets and quizzes plus a reproducible two-machine report form an honest public record; they do not replace the missing final or confer MIT credit.

Putting induction and synchronous machines under the same torque–speed, flux,
and transient conditions exposes where their models truly differ. Energized
machine skills still belong in a laboratory.
