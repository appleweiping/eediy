## The Background Exam Quantifies the Prerequisites

MIT 6.341 Fall 2005 is a graduate discrete-time signal-processing course. The
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-341-discrete-time-signal-processing-fall-2005/pages/syllabus/)
assumes LTI systems, sampling, the Fourier transform, bilateral z-transforms,
and random signals; the direct prerequisite was 6.011. Its ungraded
background exam treats a score below 80% as evidence that the foundation may
be weak. This course fits learners who can reason independently from
pole-zero plots, ROCs, aliasing, and autocorrelation and want deeper
multirate, filter-design, and spectral-analysis work. Return to signals and
systems first if `fft` or `filter` is doing the reasoning.

## Access and version notes

The [lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-341-discrete-time-signal-processing-fall-2005/pages/lecture-notes/)
list 23 positions, but Lecture 1, 14, and 23 have no notes, and there are no
public videos. Readings and problem numbers follow the 2nd edition of the
Oppenheim/Schafer/Buck text, which OCW does not provide. The
[assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-341-discrete-time-signal-processing-fall-2005/pages/assignments/)
include PS1–PS11 with solutions; the
[exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-341-discrete-time-signal-processing-fall-2005/pages/exams/)
preserve Fall 2005 and Fall 2004 background, midterm, and final papers with
solutions. Delay the key, then classify corrections by transform definition,
stability/causality, frequency scale, structural complexity, or numerical
implementation rather than changing only the final expression.

## Labs and projects

The [projects](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-341-discrete-time-signal-processing-fall-2005/pages/projects/)
retain Project I `.mat` data and 52 Project II `.wav` files. Project I.A
compares N=1 and N=50 all-pass magnitude, group delay, pole-zero behavior, and
perception. I.B compares Butterworth, Chebyshev, elliptic,
Parks–McClellan, and Kaiser designs on noisy 44.1 kHz speech by order,
stability, delay, and multiplication count. Project II adds
compressor-by-4, an 11.025 kHz output, and at most 17 multiplications per
input sample. The task is to prove that a polyphase implementation meets the
signal and compute constraints, not merely to produce cleaner audio.

## Tools and environment

Athena, `wavread`, and old MATLAB audio paths are obsolete; Python/SciPy,
Octave, or modern MATLAB are local migrations. The report still needs sample
rates, frequency normalization, IIR poles and SOS form, FIR delay, decimation
stopband, tool versions, and floating-point tolerance. The course directory
should contain independent PS1–PS11 work and corrections, timed exams, Project
I.A, I.B, and II reports, and reproducible code. Audio without a defensible
17-multiply budget misses the course's central design constraint.

The lasting judgment is concrete: within a 17-multiply budget, which
frequency bands, delays, and audible qualities must be traded away, and why?
