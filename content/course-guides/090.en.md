## See Convolution, Basis Changes, and the DFT as One Idea

EPFL's [DSP 1 course and graded assignments](https://www.coursera.org/learn/dsp1) opens a 4-course sequence.
The current page lists 4 modules, 4 graded assignments, and Python/Jupyter
work on Karplus–Strong synthesis, the Haar basis, the DFT, and telephone
keypad tones. It fits learners who know elementary signals and systems but
still treat time-domain calculation, vector projection, and spectra as
unrelated tricks. Review complex exponentials, inner products, matrix
multiplication, and array operations first if those tools are uncertain.

## The Real Work Is Fixing Conventions and Explaining Results

Discrete signals, vector spaces, and Fourier analysis should share a
convention sheet: sequence origin, forward/inverse DFT normalization,
cycles/sample versus rad/sample, and which inner-product term is conjugated.
A correct plot is insufficient. Explain delay versus pitch in
Karplus–Strong, sparsity after a Haar basis change, and how sampling rate,
observation length, and spectral leakage affect dial tones. Predict a useful
representation and spectrum for an unseen signal before
checking in a notebook, and retain a failed prediction with its correction.

## Treat the Old Repository as a Numerical Supplement

The teaching team's [DSPNumex](https://github.com/LCAV/DSPNumex) belongs to
the earlier combined MOOC and uses the old numbering for Modules 2–9. The closest
material here is [NumEx2](https://github.com/LCAV/DSPNumex/tree/master/NumEx2)
and [NumEx4](https://github.com/LCAV/DSPNumex/tree/master/NumEx4). They help
check discrete-signal and Fourier examples but are not a complete mirror of
the current guided labs. Keep Coursera labs, old NumEx material, and personal
notebooks distinct; grading, feedback, and downloads still depend on the
current account plan.

The real test is whether a change from a short sequence to a long signal, or
from steady behavior to a transient, changes the choice among convolution,
basis change, and the DFT for a reason you can explain.
