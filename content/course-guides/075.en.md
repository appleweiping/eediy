## Course fit

MIT 6.832 Underactuated Robotics, Spring 2022 studies systems with fewer actuators than degrees of freedom or with contact and dynamical constraints. Through the acrobot, cart-pole, quadrotor, and legged robots it repeatedly asks how models limit controllability, what region a Lyapunov claim covers, and whether an optimized trajectory remains closed-loop robust to disturbances. The [FAQ](https://underactuated.mit.edu/Spring2022/faqs.html) expects basic linear algebra, differential equations, and Python, but no robotics background.

A pendulum makes the prerequisites concrete: derive its state equation, compare energy drift at 2 step sizes, and plot a two-dimensional phase portrait. Difficulty here points to mathematics or NumPy testing; no robot is needed.

## Course structure

The [Schedule](https://underactuated.mit.edu/Spring2022/schedule.html) moves in Lectures 1–6 from nonlinear dynamics to dynamic programming; 7–10 to Lyapunov and sum-of-squares; 11–18 to trajectory optimization, stabilization, policy search, walking, hybrid systems, and contact; and 19–25 to planning, stochastic dynamics, robust control, and output feedback.

For the same continuing system, update assumptions about state observability, model error, noise, input bounds, and the protected region. Preserve boundary conditions, time discretization, objective, dynamics defect, constraint residual, and disturbed replay for every optimized trajectory. A solver candidate is not a stability proof.

A phase portrait shows behavior; also inspect dynamics residual and disturbed recovery.

## Assignments and feedback

[Assignments](https://underactuated.mit.edu/Spring2022/assignments.html) publishes 7 sets: Nonlinear Dynamics, Dynamic Programming, DP/Acrobots, Continuous-Time Value Iteration, Lyapunov, Trajectory Optimization, and Legged Robots. Map formulas to functions, shapes, and units, then test limiting cases, random initial conditions, physical invariants, and failure causes.

[Course Information](https://underactuated.mit.edu/Spring2022/index.html) says the original class used Deepnote, Gradescope, and staff review; public learners have neither hidden tests nor official grades. The [code repository](https://github.com/RussTedrake/underactuated) continues to change and differs from the 2022 runtime. Record a commit, Python/Drake versions, and notebook paths, and keep compatibility patches separate from solutions.

## Final project

The [Project](https://underactuated.mit.edu/Spring2022/project.html) allows an individual or 2–3 people over about 4–5 weeks, including paper reproduction. The proposal is at most 500 words, individual/team videos are 3/5 minutes, and an IEEE-style report of 3–8 pages is suggested. A good question is “does this swing-up controller beat the baseline over a specified disturbance range?”, not “build a quadrotor system.”

Define the plant, input, metric, baseline, and a falsifying experiment before
adding complexity. Put the model, derivation, solver/version, disturbed
trajectories, failure case, and the command that generates the key figure in
the project. If grasping, geometry, and perception stacks matter more, switch
to [6.4210](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-4210-robotic-manipulation-fall-2022/).
This course's useful question is where the controller beats the baseline over
the disturbance range, and simulation can answer it.
