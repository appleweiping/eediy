## Assignments and feedback

Cornell [ECE 5330 Semiconductor Optoelectronics](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-5330-semiconductor-optoelectronics/) is preserved from **Fall 2014**. The page says both **Undergraduate (senior level)** and **comprehensive graduate-level course**; the useful signal is its prerequisite strength: quantum mechanics, electromagnetic radiation (ECE 3030), and either semiconductor device physics (ECE 4570) or solid-state physics (ECE 4070). The provider advises students not to take it when any area is missing.

Original grading was homework 35%, midterm 25%, final 35%, and instructor discretion 5%, with no required textbook. The public layer has notes, homework, and solutions, but no video, complete code repository, project, or lab.

Before Handout 1, use diagnostics in each prerequisite area. For quantum mechanics, an infinite well checks wavefunction normalization, parity, and matrix elements. For electromagnetics, derive reflection/transmission from tangential boundary conditions and verify flux with the Poynting vector. For devices, draw equilibrium and forward-bias band diagrams for a heterojunction or PIN and connect Fermi/quasi-Fermi levels, doping, generation, recombination, and current density. Review any area that survives only as memorized results.

## Course structure

[Lecture Notes](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-5330-semiconductor-optoelectronics/ece-5330-lectures-notes-and-handouts/) has Handouts 1–16. Handouts 1–4 move through semiconductors, heterostructures/quantum wells, Fermi’s golden rule, gain/spontaneous emission, and III–V bandstructure. Handouts 5–7 cover photodetectors, solar cells, dielectric response, LEDs, and recombination. Handouts 8–11 connect waveguides, SOAs, quantum-well transitions, and laser rate equations. Handouts 12–16 add cavities, S/T matrices, DBR/DFB/VCSEL structures, chirp/linewidth, tunable lasers, and plasmonics.

For every device, trace `carrier state → transition/material gain → optical mode/confinement/loss → terminal power/efficiency/threshold/bandwidth/noise`. Material gain is not modal gain, and modal gain is not net terminal gain. State normalization, loss, and carrier assumptions at each boundary.

## Let 8 solved homework sets set the pace

[Homework and Exams](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-5330-semiconductor-optoelectronics/ece-5330-homework-and-exams/) publishes HW1–8 with solutions for all 8. HW1–5 cover quantum/semiconductor review, heterojunctions, gain, spontaneous emission, and solar-cell thermodynamics. HW6 covers SOA/waveguides, HW7 an InGaAsP laser/rate equations, and HW8 DBR, VCSEL, S/T matrices, and DFB; its prompt says it runs for 2 weeks and counts 2 times an ordinary homework.

Pace assignments by conceptual closure: first connect bands/carriers to transitions, then photodetectors, solar cells, and LEDs to observables, and only afterward modes, SOAs, lasers, and cavities. Preserve an unprompted first attempt, then locate the first physical divergence: material parameter, occupation, matrix element, mode normalization, units, or numerical method. After correction, recompute with a different material or cavity parameter instead of copying the solution line by line. Numerical figures should retain equations, initial conditions, tolerances, units, raw arrays, and a limiting case.

The public midterm is open book but its solution is unavailable, and no final exam is public. After HW1–5, a learner can attempt the midterm without searching for answers, but cannot create a Cornell self-score from it.

## Access and version notes

HW6, HW7, and official [HW8 Problem 8.4](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/files/2017/03/ece5330_homework8-2fz4yrw.pdf) refer to `ece533solver`, whose executable and source are absent from public navigation. A similarly named external file is not thereby a Cornell release, and official output cannot be claimed.

An independent scalar 1D eigenmode solver can check effective index/cutoff against a dielectric slab, then test grid, domain, boundary conditions, mode normalization, and failure range while distinguishing bound modes, leaky modes, and numerical artifacts. Preserve the mode trajectory as the domain expands and the grid is refined; a single attractive field profile does not establish correct mode selection. For S/T matrices, check zero-index-contrast reflection, lossless energy flux, the DBR stopband trend, and S/T conversion. This is numerical practice, not a replacement for official questions blocked by the missing tool.

## Finish at a terminal observable

[MIT 6.977, Fall 2002](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-977-semiconductor-optoelectronics-theory-and-design-fall-2002/) more explicitly emphasizes theory/design, device noise, and fiber communication, making it a useful extension after Cornell. For the exit, choose a photodetector, SOA, laser, or VCSEL and deliver the full carrier/gain, mode/loss, and terminal-observable model while retaining unresolved solver and midterm items.

Cornell explicitly marks Projects and Labs unavailable. The home route is derivation and offline computation only; [OSHA laser hazards](https://www.osha.gov/laser-hazards) also records eye and skin exposure risks. A numerical project is not energization, alignment, detector calibration, or a Cornell grade.
