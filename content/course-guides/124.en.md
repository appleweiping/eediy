## Recognize the Course Shape: It Moves Between Material, Bands, and Devices

The public Cornell ECE 4070, *Physics of Semiconductors and Nanostructures*, offering is Spring 2012. The [official course details](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-4070-course-details) identify a senior undergraduate course with 3 lectures and 1 section each week, 2 prelims and a final, and no required text. It begins with free electrons and lattices, passes through bands, statistics, phonons, and transport, and reaches heterostructures, ballistic transport, optics, and thermoelectrics. MOSFET and amplifier material appears as an insertion in the theoretical sequence rather than waiting until every solid-state topic is complete.

This is not a fabrication, cleanroom, TCAD, or device-characterization laboratory course. The official page explicitly marks projects/labs unavailable and lists no lecture video, course code, grader, or section critique. Its public strength is written feedback through handouts and solutions; its gaps are classroom transitions, experiments, and a programming environment. This guide is R0 desk research and does not equate solving public papers with a Cornell grade.

### Check the Entry Point with Wavefunctions, State Counting, and Reciprocal Lattices

1. Normalize a 1D infinite-well wavefunction, derive its allowed energy, and explain how a real-space boundary condition restricts the k-space state.
2. Write the energy dependence of the 1D, 2D, and 3D density of states, multiply by the Fermi occupation, and check units; explain why dimensionality changes the band-edge singularity.
3. For a simple lattice, construct reciprocal vectors and the first Brillouin zone, derive effective mass from the curvature of `E(k)`, and distinguish Bloch wavevector, crystal momentum, and free-particle momentum.

If task 1 fails, review quantum wavefunctions, operators, and boundary conditions. If task 2 produces memorized DOS plots but no state count, review delta functions and Fermi–Dirac statistics. If task 3 mixes real and reciprocal lattices, review Fourier series and linear algebra. This gate is not a generic prerequisite list; it is the shortest test of whether a material model can later connect to a device equation.

## Draw Physical Dependencies Across 32 Numbered Handouts

The [official lectures, notes, and handouts index](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-4070-course-details/ece-4070-lectures-notes-handouts-2/) lists Handout 1–32 plus 3 items titled Advanced Topic: Symmetries, Berry's Phase, and Polaritons. Handout 1–3 covers Drude, Sommerfeld, and the 3D/2D/1D free Fermi gas. Handout 4–8 moves through lattices, reciprocal lattices, Bloch states, nearly free electrons, and LCAO. Handout 9 inserts MOSFET regimes, I–V behavior, backgate effects, velocity saturation, and breakdown. Handout 10–12 uses tight binding for polyacetylene, Ge, graphene, Si, and GaAs. Handout 13 inserts FET amplifiers, active loads, and cascodes.

Handout 14–16 returns to statistics, effective mass, electrons and holes, and semiclassical dynamics. Handout 17–21 treats lattice vibrations, phonon quantization and statistics, and heat capacity. Handout 22–24 covers Boltzmann transport, drift-diffusion, the effective-mass equation, and dopants. Handout 25–28 develops heterostructures, quantum wells/wires/dots, ballistic transport, and Landauer theory. Handout 29–32 closes with optical transitions, dielectric response, nanotubes, and thermoelectrics. The 3 advanced handouts belong after the core, not before Bloch states, band curvature, and quantum statistics.

## Put Solved Assessments at the Seams Between Theory Layers

The [official homework and exams page](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-4070-course-details/homework-and-exams/) provides HW 1–11 with solutions and publishes Exam 1 and Exam 2 with their solutions. The Final Exam is public, but its solution is unavailable. That creates a useful feedback gradient: homework supports local correction, Exam 1/2 calibrates timed proof and calculation, and the Final remains a transfer test without an official answer. Opening solutions first erases the most useful error diagnosis in the archive.

Attempt each HW closed and state units, boundary conditions, degeneracy, and approximations. During comparison, record only the first step that caused the later divergence; then close the answer and restart from a blank page. For Exam 1 and Exam 2, use a timed original attempt, an official-solution rubric, and a retake after 7 days. Do not invent a “standard solution” for the Final. Write a complete answer, then build a peer/self rubric from dimensional analysis, known limits, a second derivation, or independent numerical calculation, leaving genuinely disputed items marked unresolved.

Assignments and exams do not make the material itself linear. Establish Handout 1–8, take the MOSFET detour at Handout 9, and finish Handout 10–12. Treat Handout 13 as a circuit insertion, return to statistics, phonons, and transport in Handout 14–24, and close with Handout 25–32. This leave-and-return route is faithful to the archive; pretending it is a linearly ordered textbook is not.

At each layer transition, audit conservation and scale: trace dispersion to density of states to carrier count to current, and device parameters back to the band model. For each layer, record input/output dimensions, normalization reference, and allowed error; cross-check discrete sums against continuum limits. Stop when effective mass, temperature, or degeneracy loses provenance, or grid refinement fails to converge. Preserve raw failures—interpolation or smoothing cannot replace them. Record every symbol's physical object, units, and domain so band, transport, and circuit sections cannot silently reuse it.

## Public Completion Evidence and the Experimental Safety Boundary

A defensible package contains a dependency map for Handout 1–32, a reading note for the 3 advanced handouts, blind and corrected attempts for HW 1–11, timed results and error classifications for Exam 1/2, an unresolved-item list for the Final, and one numerical artifact runnable from a clean environment. For every handout equation, preserve symbol definitions, units, spin or valley degeneracy, temperature, boundary conditions, and approximation range. A final plot alone cannot show whether 1D/2D/3D normalization was mixed.

Because the course has no official lab, do not add home experiments involving high-voltage breakdown, cleanroom processing, chemical etching, vacuum, high-field transport, or nanofabrication. EEDIY's default practice boundary is analytic derivation, open numerical tools, and synthetic or public data. Any later institutional experiment follows that facility's SOP, training, and supervision; an ECE 4070 handout is not operating authorization. List the missing lecture video, section feedback, grader, code, projects/labs, and Final solution individually in the completion statement.

## MIT 6.701 Offers a Different “Quantum Box to Current” Entry

The [MIT 6.701 Spring 2010 syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-701-introduction-to-nanoelectronics-spring-2010/pages/syllabus/) begins with quantum mechanics, particles in boxes, dots and wires, and FETs before connecting material band structures and computation limits. Its [Open Textbook page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-701-introduction-to-nanoelectronics-spring-2010/pages/open-textbook/) lists Parts 1–8. [Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-701-introduction-to-nanoelectronics-spring-2010/pages/assignments/) lists 12 HW groups and 2 MATLAB simulation-project parts, while the separate [Exams page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-701-introduction-to-nanoelectronics-spring-2010/pages/exams/) exposes the 2010 midterm, 2010 final, and 2011 final papers without solution links. Prefer Cornell for broad solid-state, phonon, transport, and optics coverage with 11 solved HW sets. Start with MIT for a gentler route from quantum boxes to current and ready-made simulation projects.

### The Numerical Artifact Tests Cross-Layer Conservation, Not an Official Lab

The EEDIY supplement is a “dimensionality and ballistic-transport audit,” not an official Cornell project. Implement either a 1D tight-binding chain or a quantum-well subband model, calculate 1D/2D/3D DOS, and use the Landauer relation to generate conductance steps. Check analytic limits, grid convergence, a state-count sum rule, and units. Deliver equations and assumptions, executable source, pinned parameters, raw arrays, a convergence sweep, failed plots, and documentation. Acceptance requires boundary-condition or grid-size changes to expose mistakes, an explanation of the numerical singularity near a band edge, and an independent script that rebuilds every plot from raw arrays.
