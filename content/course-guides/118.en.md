## This course analyzes network states; it does not train you to handle high-voltage equipment

IIT Kharagpur's **Power System Analysis**, taught by Debapriya Das, is aimed at third-year electrical-engineering undergraduates. On 2026-07-29, the official [course page](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/117105140) identified the active run as Jul–Oct 2026: enrollment ran from 2026-05-22 through 2026-07-27, exam registration through 2026-08-14, and the proctored exam was listed for 2026-10-25. The course moves from transmission-line parameters, per-unit quantities, and component models to load flow, optimal operation, three-phase and unbalanced faults, symmetrical components, and basic stability. It uses generator and network models; it is not a course in electrical-machine construction, protection commissioning, or high-voltage experiments.

Public learning and certification are separate. The certification notice says enrollment and learning are free, while a certificate requires separate registration, a Rs 1000 fee, and an in-person proctored exam at a designated center. The review date is after the enrollment deadline, although the lecture archive remains public. That does not show that a late visitor can submit active assignments, enter the discussion forum, or sit the exam. Dates, centers, and fees can change, so certificate candidates must recheck them in an NPTEL account.

## Three entry problems expose three forms of “calculation without modeling”

Problem 1: choose a common MVA/kV base for a small network with a transformer, convert impedances across voltage levels into per unit, and explain why the ideal turns ratio disappears from the equivalent network. Problem 2: form \(Y_{bus}\) from 3-bus data, name the unknowns and mismatch equations for PQ, PV, and slack buses, and hand-compute one Newton–Raphson update. Problem 3: transform an unbalanced phasor set into positive, negative, and zero sequence and connect a fault network; then state signs and units for inertia, mechanical input, and electrical output in a minimal swing equation.

Complete at least Problems 1 and 2 independently before Week 1. If base voltages do not follow transformer ratios consistently, \(Y_{bus}\) off-diagonal signs vary by accident, or phase sequence is confused with chronological order, review three-phase circuits, linear algebra, and numerical methods. These are EEDIY placement diagnostics, not NPTEL enrollment prerequisites.

The lecture sequence crosses week boundaries: Lectures 1–14 cover structure and line parameters, 15–20 components and per unit, 21–25 line performance, 26–36 load flow, 37–44 optimal operation, 45–54 faults and symmetrical components, and 55–60 stability.

Map every lecture to a network artifact: a one-line diagram, base table, parameter model, \(Y_{bus}\), power-flow residual, cost curve, sequence network, fault current, or swing trajectory. This keeps the course from becoming 60 substitutions into formulas. The video API marks its resources public but lists no code starter, numerical test-case bundle, lab manual, or autograder.

## Load flow, faults, and stability each need a different independent check

A load-flow submission must report more than “converged.” Record bus types, bases, initialization, iteration history, active and reactive mismatch norms, PV reactive-limit treatment, and line-loss balance, and compare against a 2-bus closed form or an independent implementation. A fault study must preserve pre-fault assumptions, sequence-network connections, base conversions, and reconstruction error on returning to phase quantities. A stability calculation should report integration step, an energy or equal-area check, clearing-time perturbations, and units for every state.

Return to Lecture 26 if, after Lecture 30, the meaning of Newton–Raphson Jacobian blocks remains unclear or residuals decline only because updates are arbitrarily damped. Repeat the fault block if sequence-network connections cannot be drawn after Lecture 50. Do not exit if the swing trajectory remains highly time-step-sensitive after Lecture 58 while a critical clearing time is still reported. Numerical convergence is not sufficient evidence of a correct physical model.

## EEDIY adds reproducible cases, not an official lab

The EEDIY supplement is a simulation dossier: a 3–5 bus per-unit and \(Y_{bus}\) builder, a Gauss–Seidel or Newton–Raphson load flow, a three-phase and one unsymmetrical fault, and a single-machine infinite-bus swing simulation. Every notebook needs a hand-checkable case, units and base table, residuals, independent reference, parameter perturbation, and invalid input. These are EEDIY supplements, not official NPTEL assignments, labs, exams, or certificate evidence.

Do not use confidential utility models, station topology, relay settings, switching procedures, personal data, or unauthorized SCADA or PMU exports. Use a teaching case with an explicit public license or a clearly labeled synthetic network, recording source, license, and changes. Results from a small case must not become advice about the stability margin, fault level, or operating decision of a real grid.

## The high-voltage boundary comes before any physical idea

This route is simulation-only by default. Do not connect mains, three-phase sources, transformers, motors, generators, switchgear, capacitor banks, relay panels, or high-voltage equipment; open a CT secondary; touch exposed buswork; inject a short circuit or fault; or use an ordinary oscilloscope probe on floating or high-voltage nodes. Fault current and stability in this course are model objects, not experiment instructions.

Physical power experiments belong only in an institutionally approved high-voltage or machines laboratory under a qualified supervisor designated by that institution, with formal risk assessment, lockout/tagout, de-energization verification, grounding, arc-flash boundaries, rated probes and PPE, barriers or interlocks, and an emergency plan. A learner must not translate simulator breaker actions directly into field operations. This page grants no permission for power-up, wiring, protection settings, or fault testing.
