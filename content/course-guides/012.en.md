## 8.03SC connects oscillators, waves, and optics

MIT [8.03SC](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-03sc-physics-iii-vibrations-and-waves-fall-2016/) is Yen-Jie Lee's Fall 2016 Scholar edition. Its 24 lectures build normal modes from simple, damped, driven, and coupled oscillators; take the discrete system toward wave equations, sound, electromagnetic waves, dispersion, and group velocity; and finish with polarization, interference, diffraction, and resolution. It best serves learners who already have mechanics, electromagnetism, and differential equations and want one phase-based intuition connecting RLC circuits, transmission lines, resonators, antennas, and optics. Treating mechanics, EM, and optics as separate lists misses the course's design.

## One driven oscillator tests readiness

The official [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-03sc-physics-iii-vibrations-and-waves-fall-2016/pages/syllabus/) lists 18.02 and 8.02 and expects familiarity with 8.01, 18.01, and some 18.06. Before starting, derive the steady-state complex amplitude of a driven damped oscillator, explain its phase as the drive crosses resonance, and map mass, damping, and stiffness to a series RLC. If \(\omega_0=\sqrt{k/m}\) is familiar but transient and steady response cannot be separated, review second-order ODEs and complex phasors first.

The [Resource Index](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-03sc-physics-iii-vibrations-and-waves-fall-2016/pages/resource-index/) aligns video, typed and handwritten notes, Georgi's openly available text, 10 problem sets, help videos, and demonstrations. Read the derivation and fill in omitted steps, use video to inspect nodes, phase, and envelopes, then attempt the problem. Classroom demonstrations have no bill of materials or report requirements and should not be presented as reproducible off-campus labs.

Keep the same “phase account” whenever the physical object changes: drive-to-response phase, spatial propagation constant, reflection phase at a boundary, group delay, and whether the observed amplitude is peak, RMS, or complex. For two coupled oscillators, predict normal modes from symmetry and then derive eigenfrequencies from the matrix. For a continuous string or line, put endpoint conditions into the allowed \(k\). Continue tracing path and phase differences in interference and diffraction so optics does not become a disconnected formula list.

Dispersion deserves one dedicated figure. Mark the carrier, envelope, phase velocity, and group velocity on the same pulse. After changing the dispersion relation, compare packet spreading and energy-flow direction and state which losses the model omits. That exercise connects mechanical chains, transmission lines, and optics and prepares the same reasoning for filter or communications group delay.

## Assignments and feedback

The 10 problem sets do not have a complete solution set. [Problem Solving Help Videos](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-03sc-physics-iii-vibrations-and-waves-fall-2016/pages/problem-solving-help-videos/) demonstrate representative problems across 10 topics. Complete your own derivation before watching so the failure can be located in the equation, boundary condition, phase convention, or physical interpretation. The public [final exam](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-03sc-physics-iii-vibrations-and-waves-fall-2016/pages/final-exam/) supplies a formula sheet and 3 practice finals but still not a complete answer chain. State the phasor and Fourier conventions and cross-check dimensions and limiting cases.

A suitable synthesis begins with a driven oscillator's magnitude, phase, and \(Q\); extends two coupled oscillators to a periodic chain; compares discrete eigenfrequencies with a continuous dispersion relation; and finally adds a finite boundary to explain standing waves. Keep parameters, units, analytic predictions, and numerical error beside the plots. Understanding is visible when the same model survives the move from mechanical to circuit and propagation problems.
