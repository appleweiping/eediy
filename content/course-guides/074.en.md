## Course fit

MIT [6.4210 Robotic Manipulation](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-4210-robotic-manipulation-fall-2022/) puts perception, planning, dynamics/control, and learning inside the single problem of manipulation in clutter. It fits learners who want to move from individual algorithms to a complete robotic system. OCW archives Fall 2022, while the [online textbook](https://manipulation.csail.mit.edu/) is a living set of notes, so today's chapters, notebooks, and `pydrake` APIs may differ.

[Course Information](https://manipulation.csail.mit.edu/Fall2022/index.html) requires basic linear algebra and lists probability, algorithms, basic neural networks, and Python/Jupyter. Robotics background is optional, but simultaneous difficulty with NumPy shapes, least squares, and coordinate transforms will make the opening assignments slow.

## Coursework

The [Schedule](https://manipulation.csail.mit.edu/Fall2022/schedule.html) lists Problem Set 1–10. PS1–PS3 cover simulation, kinematics, frames, poses, Jacobians, and optimization; PS4–PS5 introduce geometry, point clouds, registration, and grasping; PS6–PS8 connect behavior, deep perception, and planning; PS9–PS10 move to control, the project check-in, and reinforcement learning.

PS4–PS8 are the course's most distinctive sequence. The same final animation can hide either a perception or planning failure: once the point cloud or candidate grasp is wrong, a better planner cannot recover; when the geometry is sound but a collision query or solver fails, the problem finally belongs to planning. Comparing those intermediate quantities under the same scene, object pose, and random seed explains much more than success rate alone.

## Tools and environment

The official repository at [commit cdb8c90f402c9201999bb90a27a034cf2e5d166c](https://github.com/RussTedrake/manipulation/commit/cdb8c90f402c9201999bb90a27a034cf2e5d166c) is a useful modern entry point. Its [pyproject.toml](https://github.com/RussTedrake/manipulation/blob/cdb8c90f402c9201999bb90a27a034cf2e5d166c/pyproject.toml) reports `2025.10.20` and declares `python>=3.10,<4.0` with `drake>=1.45.0`. This is not the original Fall 2022 notebook environment, so API, geometry, or solver differences in old problems are unsurprising. Starting with a small notebook is the easiest way to learn current Drake.

The complete course can be studied in simulation without a robot arm. Feeding simulation ground-truth ports directly to a controller creates a misleading success rate. Work on a physical arm also requires on-site supervision, speed and force limits, emergency stops, and collision protection; the public materials do not provide those site conditions.

## Final project

The official [Project](https://manipulation.csail.mit.edu/Fall2022/project.html) runs 4–5 weeks and supports teams of 2–3. The preproposal is about 500–750 words, the proposal about 1000 words, individual/pair/trio videos are 3/4/5 minutes, and the suggested report is 1500–4500 words. Clutter clearing, peg-in-hole, and a planner benchmark are all sufficient topics; they bring occlusion, contact, and search complexity to the foreground in different ways.

The most valuable habit to carry into the project is decomposing the source of failure rather than adding a more complicated model. Pose and occlusion affect perception, mass and friction affect contact, and collision margin, termination, and runtime expose planning tradeoffs. Explaining changes in success rate along those dimensions is what finally joins the course's perception and planning halves.
