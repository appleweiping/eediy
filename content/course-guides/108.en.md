## The challenge in 6.013 is connecting integral laws to real devices

MIT 6.013 **Electromagnetics and Applications, Spring 2009** is David Staelin's undergraduate course. The official [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-013-electromagnetics-and-applications-spring-2009/pages/syllabus/) expects preparation equivalent to 18.01, 18.02, 8.01, 8.02, 6.002, and 6.00, specifically including complex numbers, vector operators, simple matrix operations, calculus, RLC circuits, Maxwell equations, and Fourier transforms. This is not only a collection of electromagnetic boundary-value problems. It connects fields and waves to motors and generators, MEMS, transmission lines, resonators, waveguides, antennas, wireless links, fibers, lasers, and acoustics.

Keep the version explicit: this is the OCW archive of the Spring 2009 campus class. That class scheduled 2 one-hour lectures, 2 one-hour recitations, and 1 one-hour tutorial each week. The public archive retains notes, a textbook, problems, answers, and demonstrations, but not the 2009 tutorial, TA grading, participation score, or an MIT credential. Its grading formula, 2 quizzes, and final explain the historical assessment; a local self-score is not an MIT grade.

## Use three sheets of paper for placement

Sheet 1: move from the integral Faraday, Ampère–Maxwell, and Gauss laws to differential form with explicit orientation and boundary assumptions, then verify the units of \(E/H\), phase, and the Poynting vector for a uniform plane wave. Sheet 2: for a step-driven transmission line, write the traveling waves, reflection coefficient, and initial and final values while distinguishing line, load, and Thevenin-source impedance. Sheet 3: derive the relation between \(Q\) and bandwidth from energy exchange in an RLC resonator, and explain how additional loss changes poles, linewidth, and stored energy.

Complete at least 2 sheets without an answer key before Lecture 1. If vector calculus is only component-by-component symbol manipulation, or phasors and time-domain quantities are mixed without warning, review multivariable calculus, signals, and circuits first. Each sheet must expose reference directions, planes, parameter sources, dimensions, and one limiting case. These 3 sheets are an EEDIY diagnostic, not an MIT placement exam. Their purpose is to expose a mismatch between mathematical notation and physical direction before it spreads.

## The 24 lectures form one fields-to-energy-to-ports chain

The official [calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-013-electromagnetics-and-applications-spring-2009/pages/calendar/) and [lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-013-electromagnetics-and-applications-spring-2009/pages/lecture-notes/) align with Lectures 1–24. Lectures 1–4 establish Maxwell equations, media and boundaries, uniform plane waves, and the Poynting theorem. Lectures 5–7 turn field energy into force, motors, generators, MEMS, and Laplace boundary problems. Lectures 8–10 treat anisotropic, lossy, and plasma media, Snell's law, TE/TM waves, and Brewster angle. Lectures 11–16 cover delay, transients, transmission lines, the Smith chart, resonators, and waveguides. Lectures 17–20 reach antennas, links, radiation, and diffraction; 21–24 close with fibers, lasers, acoustics, and speech generation.

Maintain one audit sheet throughout: control volume or contour, state variables, constitutive relation, boundary conditions, power or energy balance, and measurable terminal quantity. It prevents a common split in which field derivations and network calculations are both familiar but cannot be connected. The provider also supplies a 5 MB [open textbook](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-013-electromagnetics-and-applications-spring-2009/resources/mit6_013s09_notes/) for complete derivations. Use the 24 lecture notes for sequence and the textbook for depth rather than reading both as duplicate courses.

## The 12 problem sets do not have uniform answer coverage

The official [assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-013-electromagnetics-and-applications-spring-2009/pages/assignments/) page lists Problem Sets 1–12. Public solutions exist for Sets 2–7, 9, 11, and 12; Sets 1, 8, and 10 have no public solution. Problem Set 12 was issued with its answer as an uncollected learning exercise in the original class. The attempt-first rule should not change with answer availability: fix the diagram, knowns and unknowns, governing law, boundary, and units before calculating.

For solved sets, classify corrections as orientation or sign, constitutive model, boundary matching, complex algebra, power balance, or limiting case. For unsolved sets, require at least 2 independent checks—for example, energy conservation plus a limiting case, or a closed-form special case plus numerical convergence. Exit to Lectures 1–4 if normal and tangential components are still routinely reversed by Problem Set 3. Pause if force cannot be explained through an energy derivative by Problem Set 6.

## Quizzes are not formula recall; the final crosses three representations

The official [exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-013-electromagnetics-and-applications-spring-2009/pages/exams/) page publishes Quiz 1, Quiz 2, and the Final Exam, each with a solution and equation sheet. Sit each paper under timed conditions using only the corresponding sheet, then correct every answer under 3 columns: field picture, mathematical boundary, and network or output quantity. Matching a final number is insufficient; the correction must also locate where power enters or leaves, where energy is stored, and where power is dissipated.

Place Quiz 1 after Lecture 10, Quiz 2 around the wireless section, and reserve the final until Lecture 24. Do not study a solution before timing the paper. The campus class also had recitations, tutorials, and participation context that OCW learners do not receive. Label any local rubric as EEDIY self-review, not a reconstruction of an MIT grade.

## Demonstrations explain; they do not authorize home reproduction

The official [movie demonstrations](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-013-electromagnetics-and-applications-spring-2009/pages/movie-demonstrations/) page contains 15 MATLAB-generated movies on free-space, lossy, and plasma waves, polarization, array patterns, apertures, quarter-wave transformers, resonators, acoustic waves, and terminated lines, with scripts for some entries. They are computational demonstrations. They do not authorize rebuilding physical demonstrations involving lasers, radar, MEMS, motors, or high-power RF.

The EEDIY supplement is a simulation-only “energy ledger.” Choose a transmission-line transient, resonator, or antenna link and plot a field or network quantity, instantaneous or cycle-averaged power, instantaneous stored energy, and cumulative dissipated energy; run at 2 grid or step sizes to check convergence. It is an EEDIY supplement, not an official MIT 6.013 lab, Problem Set, or grader task.

By default, do not connect mains, motors or generators, lasers, RF power amplifiers, waveguide sources, or unknown antennas, and do not recreate arc, plasma, or radiation demonstrations. Any physical work requires an institutionally approved laboratory and qualified supervision, with isolation and energy limiting, rated cables, terminations and probes, guards, interlocks, an emergency stop, and applicable RF, laser, and electrical procedures. This page grants no bench-work authorization.

## Completion means moving both ways among field, energy, and port

The dossier should include all 3 placement sheets, an audit table for Lectures 1–24, first attempts and check status for Problem Sets 1–12, timed Quiz 1, Quiz 2, and final records, one EEDIY simulation energy ledger, and an explicit unreproduced list: tutorials, TA feedback, campus grading, and physical demonstrations. For a transmission line, resonator, or antenna problem, the learner should move from Maxwell equations and boundaries to a terminal quantity, then audit the result through Poynting or energy balance.

Operating a Smith chart without explaining reflected-wave energy, or quoting the Poynting theorem without relating it to circuit power, misses the central 6.013 threshold.
