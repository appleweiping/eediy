## 8.05 Is the Formal Advanced Continuation of 8.04

MIT 8.05 Fall 2013 does not reteach one-dimensional wave mechanics. Its
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-05-quantum-physics-ii-fall-2013/pages/syllabus/)
requires at least a C in 8.04 and moves directly to Hilbert spaces, bras and
kets, operators, tensor products, and symmetry, then applies them to
two-state systems, spin, angular momentum, and identical particles. Review
linear algebra first if complex vector spaces, eigenbases, and unitary and
Hermitian matrices are not comfortable; otherwise Dirac notation becomes an
elegant-looking screen over missing structure.

## Pair Every Abstract Claim with a Small Matrix

Follow the 26 [video lectures](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-05-quantum-physics-ii-fall-2013/video_galleries/video-lectures)
in order. Express the same state as a coordinate wavefunction, a column vector
in a chosen basis, and Dirac notation, then verify that normalization and
observable predictions survive a basis change. For a tensor product, state
the subsystem dimensions and basis ordering. For angular momentum, eliminate
impossible outcomes by symmetry before consulting coefficient tables.

The [assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-05-quantum-physics-ii-fall-2013/pages/assignments/)
publish 11 sets without official solutions. Small matrix programs can check
Hermiticity, unitarity, commutators, and time evolution, but should not replace
analytic arguments. When two references give coefficient signs that differ,
compare phase conventions before treating the difference as physical.

A two-state system is the best miniature of the course. Calculate one
evolution in both the energy and measurement bases, draw the Bloch sphere or
an equivalent geometry, and identify which phase information is observable.
For multiparticle work, write the tensor-product basis ordering before every
calculation; this prevents many invisible indexing errors.

## A Basis Change Must Leave the Physics Intact

The [exam archive](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-05-quantum-physics-ii-fall-2013/pages/exams/)
contains tests, finals, and formula sheets from 2011–2013, also without
solutions. For a final exercise, choose one spin-\(1/2\) observable and compute
its eigenstates, time evolution, and measurement probabilities in two bases,
then show that the predictions agree. A complete two-level-system analysis is
another strong exercise. Connecting state space and symmetry to an observable result
is the point of this formal advanced continuation. State the phase convention used by
the change-of-basis matrix alongside the calculation.
