## Course fit

MIT [18.01SC](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/) suits a first systematic pass through calculus when complete self-assessment matters. The Fall 2010 Scholar edition reorganizes David Jerison's teaching, recitations, examples, problem sets, and exams into an independent-study sequence. Its advantage is the useful trail of examples, solutions, and exams after a wrong answer. The official [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/pages/syllabus/) asks only for high-school algebra and trigonometry and estimates roughly 150 hours for full participation. If derivatives, integrals, and series are already fluent and proof is the real goal, take the more theoretical 18.014 instead.

## Course structure

The course moves from limits, derivatives, and linear approximation through definite integration, the fundamental theorem, and integration techniques, then ends with sequences, series, Taylor expansion, and error. For an EE learner, the recurring questions are how a quantity changes near an operating point, how local change accumulates into charge or energy, and when a finite-order approximation ceases to be credible. Optimization requires its endpoints, while a Taylor approximation needs its expansion point and remainder. Those conditions matter more than another page of integration formulas.

Make the graph, formula, and explanation agree. In related rates, the real work is often stating the constraint and the independent variable before differentiating. For an improper integral or infinite series, separate existence, requested approximation, and error control. A natural EE extension is a dimensioned \(i\!-\!v\) curve: compare its first-order approximation with the exact value near an operating point and watch the error grow with the interval. This is an optional connection, not an additional MIT assignment.

## Solve Before Opening the Answer

Each Scholar part contains session problems, problem sets, worked examples, and solutions. The [Exam 1](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/pages/1.-differentiation/exam-1/) page likewise places review material, the paper, and its solution together. When a problem set stalls, a worked example is more useful than copying the answer. Changing a parameter after reading a solution usually separates a conceptual gap from an algebra mistake.

The course has 4 unit exams lasting 1 hour each and a [final exam](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/pages/final-exam/) lasting 3 hours. If an old Java Mathlet no longer runs, use a hand sketch or local numerical plot rather than weakening browser security. Lectures, problems, solutions, and full exams all remain aligned to the same offering, which is why 18.01SC works better for self-study than a loose collection of calculus videos.
