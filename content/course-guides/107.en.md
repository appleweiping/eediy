## Access and version notes

The [Fall 2026 Cornell roster](https://classes.cornell.edu/browse/roster/FA26/class/ECE/3030)
lists PHYS 2213, MATH 2930, and either ECE 2100 or ENGRD 2100 as
prerequisites for the 2026–2027 catalogue course; the anonymously usable
material is Farhan Rana's
[Fall 2007 archive](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3030-electromagnetic-fields-and-waves-2/).
It moves from static Maxwell/Poisson/Laplace problems, materials, and waves
into transmission lines, waveguides, antennas, scattering, and apertures. It
fits learners with vector calculus, ODEs, phasors, complex power, and basic
transmission lines who want a continuous electromagnetics path with solved
work. Keep the Fall 2026 prerequisite statement separate from the 2007 teaching
archive, and choose another entry when a modern lab or project is required.

## The 36 Note Sets Form a Physical Route

The [lecture notes](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3030-electromagnetic-fields-and-waves-2/ece-3030-lectures-notes-and-handouts-2/)
provide 36 sets that can be read as static fields, waves/materials,
transmission-line matching, guided modes, radiation/antennas, and
scattering/apertures. Begin each part with coordinates, Maxwell equations, and
boundary conditions, then check dimensions, energy/power, or a limiting case.
Using a Smith chart, TE/TM cutoff, or an antenna formula without reference
directions and material assumptions leaves the parts disconnected.

## The 12 Solved Sets Provide the Main Feedback

The [homework and exams](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3030-electromagnetic-fields-and-waves-2/ece-3030-homework-and-exams-2/)
provide 12 homework sets with solutions. Delay the key until after an
independent derivation and classify corrections by coordinate choice, phasor
sign, impedance normalization, or boundary condition. The same page lists
Exam 1, 2, 3, and Final, while the home page says 2 prelims plus final. Because
the archive is internally inconsistent, use papers under their file titles
and years rather than reconstructing an unsupported grading structure.

## Finish with a Cross-Scale Maxwell Field Map

The public pages say labs/projects are unavailable; empty placeholder titles
do not establish a lab package. Select representative work from static fields,
wave interfaces, line matching, waveguides, and antennas, preserving coordinate
choice, boundary conditions, derivation, and an energy check. Then map how the
same Maxwell equations change approximation and observable across scales. A
Smith-chart or waveguide script can be labeled an independent numerical check,
not a Cornell lab. Pair another openly documented laboratory course when
hands-on RF measurement is the goal.

[Meep](https://github.com/NanoComp/meep) is an active GPL-2.0 FDTD
electromagnetics solver with straight and bent waveguide, cavity, and
far-field examples. It is not a Cornell lab. Adapt a minimal straight
waveguide model, stating cell, resolution, PML, source, and reference plane,
then perform mesh refinement and compare propagation constant, cutoff, or
power flux with a hand-derived limit. If those values still drift after
refinement, an attractive field image cannot answer whether the waveguide has
converged.
