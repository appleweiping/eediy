## Prerequisites and the Missing Lecture Layer

MIT 6.301 Fall 2010 is a BJT-heavy graduate Solid-State Circuits course. Its
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-301-solid-state-circuits-fall-2010/pages/syllabus/)
requires 6.012 and assumes the Bode plots, Laplace transforms, transfer
functions, and complex impedance of 6.003. The primary notes are not on OCW
and there are no lecture videos. Choose it only as advanced reinforcement,
not as a first transistor course. The public list spans
[Recitations](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-301-solid-state-circuits-fall-2010/pages/recitations/)
1–26, but Recitation 17 has only the listed topic “Lab 2 wrap-up, op-amps
continued”; its notes are not available.

## A Whole Amplifier Exposes Prerequisite Gaps

Analyze a two-transistor amplifier: find DC bias and region, draw its
small-signal model, and estimate midband gain, input/output resistance, and
poles and zeros. If that is not yet possible, review devices, circuits, and
feedback first. For each recitation topic, keep the operating point,
incremental model, dominant approximation, error boundary, and a SPICE check.
Leave the Recitation 17 notes visibly unavailable rather than labeling an external
replacement as MIT material.

The [assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-301-solid-state-circuits-fall-2010/pages/assignments/)
have 9 sets without solutions. The [labs](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-301-solid-state-circuits-fall-2010/pages/labs/)
provide Lab 1, Lab 2, and a Design Problem; campus work included staff
sign-off. An independent adaptation needs low-voltage current limiting, a
substitution BOM, prelab predictions, raw waveforms, and a fault log. The
design should also compare topology, headroom, gain-bandwidth, and
compensation.

## How to Use the Older Exams

The [exam archive](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-301-solid-state-circuits-fall-2010/pages/exams/)
mainly contains 2006–2008 papers, not Fall 2010 exams, and lacks a uniform
solution set. Use them as timed method-selection checks while preserving each
year.

The 741, TTL, and classical BJT circuits expose charge storage, parasitic
poles, saturation recovery, and feedback stability. On moving to CMOS IC
design, rebuild the device, capacitance, and noise models and carry forward
only the workflow: establish bias, linearize, estimate poles, then measure.
Explaining why an approximation succeeds or fails is more valuable than
memorizing historical device parameters.

As a design exercise, take an unfamiliar supply, load, and gain specification
and choose a topology and compensation independently, naming swing, stability,
and any unanalyzed noise risk. A method that works only with the recitation's
original numbers has not yet become design judgment.
