## Begin by admitting that this is a graduate transport course

The official [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/10-626-electrochemical-energy-systems-spring-2014/pages/syllabus/) for MIT **10.626 Electrochemical Energy Systems, Spring 2014** lists 10.50 Analysis of Transport Phenomena as a prerequisite and schedules 1-hour meetings 3 times per week. This is not battery trivia or a DIY battery guide. It analyzes batteries, fuel cells, supercapacitors, and electrokinetic systems through equivalent circuits, thermodynamics, reaction kinetics, transport, electrostatics, porous media, and phase transformations. If a learner cannot derive a flux balance from a conservation law, review transport before trying to compensate with more videos.

The official grading structure also reveals the course's center: problem sets are 40%, the take-home midterm is 30%, and the open-book final is 30%. These 3 numbers describe assessment inside the 2014 offering; they supply no outside grade, grader, or instructor feedback. Commercial textbooks and some research articles must be obtained separately and lawfully. Publication of an OCW page does not place every assigned reading under one freely redistributable license.

## Use 3 threshold problems to decide whether Session 1 is viable

Problem 1: derive a conservation equation for 1-dimensional diffusion or migration, state boundary conditions, and use nondimensionalization to identify the dominant time scale. Problem 2: use electrochemical potential or the Nernst relation to explain how equilibrium voltage varies with concentration or temperature, checking sign and units. Problem 3: write the complex impedance of a resistor–capacitor equivalent circuit and interpret the high-frequency intercept and characteristic time on a Nyquist plot. If any answer is only formula substitution, review transport, thermodynamics, or linear systems as appropriate.

These 3 problems are EEDIY placement diagnostics, not an MIT prerequisite waiver. Passing means being able to state assumptions: whether electroneutrality holds, whether activity is replaced by concentration, whether isothermal operation is defensible, and whether an interface has been lumped. A correct number without intelligible assumptions is not enough to start porous-electrode modeling.

Mathematical notation must also return to physical quantities. Fix flux direction, interface normal, and current sign on a diagram before writing reaction rates and boundary conditions. If concentration is substituted for activity, state the regime in which that approximation is defensible. Check units before defining the control volume and surface. This exposes mistakes before a numerical solver hides them behind a smooth curve.

## Three introductory sessions precede 5 editorial model layers

The official [calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/10-626-electrochemical-energy-systems-spring-2014/pages/calendar/) itemizes 38 sessions. The first 3 are not a generic block of “applications and mathematical background”: Session 1 is syllabus and overview; Session 2 is basic physics of galvanic cells and electrochemical energy conversion; Session 3 is electrochemical energy storage. These are the 3 official introductory sessions.

Only from Session 4 does this guide apply an explicitly editorial EEDIY grouping; it does not claim that MIT names 5 layers. Sessions 4–7 form the circuit and impedance layer; 8–15 thermodynamics; 16–24 reaction kinetics; 25–34 transport and reaction–diffusion; and 35–38 double layers, supercapacitors, porous media, scaling, and porous-electrode synthesis. Follow these 5 coupled layers while retaining the provider's original 38-session numbering in the reading index.

The first layer produces a circuit-identification notebook: fit a minimal equivalent circuit to a voltage or current transient and discuss parameter identifiability. The second produces a thermodynamic ledger aligning derivations of chemical potential, open-circuit voltage, stability, and phase behavior. The third puts limiting cases, Tafel behavior, and exchange-current assumptions for Butler–Volmer-type kinetics on one page. The fourth moves from diffusion and migration conservation to dimensionless groups. Only the fifth couples double layers, interfacial kinetics, and transport in a porous-electrode model, with checks for mesh, time step, mass balance, and energy consistency.

Do not put all 5 layers into one black box at once. Before adding a physical mechanism, preserve the previous layer's baseline output, conservation residual, parameter table, and limiting case. If the new model changes an old result, identify whether the cause is the added mechanism, parameter re-identification, or numerical discretization. Effective transport coefficients and interfacial area in porous media are not arbitrary fitting knobs; their source, units, and valid regime belong in the parameter ledger.

The [Lecture Notes index](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/10-626-electrochemical-energy-systems-spring-2014/pages/lecture-notes/) is not a set of 38 same-version instructor notes. The official index mixes 2014 student scribe notes, older 2011/2012 notes, textbook chapters, and research readings, and not every session has a current note. A study record must identify source year and document type. It must not relabel old material, student notes, and formal 2014 lectures as “38 matching lecture notes.”

## The 5 problem sets have no solutions, so feedback must be designed

The official [Assignments page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/10-626-electrochemical-energy-systems-spring-2014/pages/assignments/) lists Problem Sets 1–5 and explicitly says that solutions are unavailable. Attempt each problem before seeking calibration and preserve the governing equation, boundary conditions, units, limiting cases, and numerical residual. Corrections can use a conservation residual, an analytical special case, 2 independent numerical discretizations, or peer derivation that does not exchange final answers. Do not fill the gap from an unverified solution archive.

Feedback should become stricter as models couple. An equivalent circuit can be checked against a closed-form response. Thermodynamics can be checked for Maxwell-style consistency and equilibrium limits. Kinetics can be checked in small- and large-overpotential limits. Transport requires global mass balance and mesh or time-step convergence. A porous electrode additionally requires parameter perturbation. A plausible discharge curve without residual and convergence evidence is not completion of that layer.

These problems train derivation but provide no independent grade. Record each problem as one of 4 states: attempted, independently checked, peer-reviewed, or unresolved. Do not let a vague “done” hide the uncertainty created by the missing solutions.

Peer review also needs a boundary. A reviewer may inspect the control volume, signs, dimensions, convergence, and physical interpretation, but should not send a complete answer that replaces the first attempt. When 2 derivations disagree, compare their state variables, reference potentials, and boundary conditions and locate the first divergence instead of voting on the result. A preserved disagreement record is stronger evidence of modeling judgment than a cleaned-up answer alone.

## The midterm closes a loop; the final is an open-book transfer test

The official [Exams page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/10-626-electrochemical-energy-systems-spring-2014/pages/exams/) publishes the 2014 midterm and its solution. It also publishes the final without a final solution. Complete and lock a first midterm attempt under take-home conditions, then use the official solution to classify errors as model choice, sign or units, algebra, boundary conditions, or physical interpretation. On the repeat, alter a parameter or boundary condition so that memorizing steps cannot masquerade as correction.

Use the final as an open-book transfer test. Define allowed references beforehand, record model-selection time and unresolved assumptions for each problem, and then review with dimensional analysis, conservation, special cases, and a peer rubric. In the absence of an official solution, a self-score is not an MIT score. If the final exposes a porous-transport gap, return to Sessions 25–38 rather than accumulating more application papers.

## Outside practice defaults to simulation and public data

EEDIY recommends 1 “model-to-data studio.” Data must come from a public electrochemical dataset with a clear license, a clearly labeled synthetic trace, or authorized non-confidential industrial electrochemical data. Retain 1 runnable notebook each for equivalent circuits, a Nernst or thermodynamic relation, Butler–Volmer kinetics, diffusion, and porous coupling, then test parameter transfer on a held-out trace. This is an EEDIY supplement, not an official MIT 10.626 lab or assignment. The public pages reviewed here do not supply a hardware lab, BOM, code grader, or reproducible battery-cycling setup.

The physical boundary must be stricter than the model boundary. Do not assemble homemade cells, disassemble batteries, cycle unknown, damaged, swollen, or salvaged batteries, or perform overcharge, short-circuit, thermal-abuse, or gas-generation tests. A course model of a battery or fuel cell is not a home chemistry or high-energy experiment instruction. If an institution has approved a low-energy electrochemical measurement, it still requires qualified supervision, an approved chemistry, isolated and current-limited instrumentation, SDS/PPE, ventilation, and a waste process. This guide supplies no authorization.

A defensible model-to-data report separates model adequacy, parameter identifiability, measurement uncertainty, and safety scope. Public or authorized data needs a license or written authorization, provenance, temperature, cycling protocol, and missing-metadata record; synthetic data must be marked synthetic. Do not use proprietary, NDA-bound, or otherwise unauthorized laboratory or industrial data, and do not mistake removing a filename for publication permission. A good curve fit does not prove a unique mechanism and cannot support a diagnosis of real battery health or safety.

Parameter identification must be separated from prediction. If the same trace selects the model, fits its parameters, and supports the accuracy claim, there is no independent evidence of transfer. Predeclare fitting and held-out intervals, report residual structure rather than only mean error, and discuss whether multiple parameter combinations produce nearly the same output. When parameters are not identifiable, narrow the conclusion to “describes these data over this input range” rather than promoting it to a material property or degradation mechanism.

Choose the course ID 122 PV route to explain conversion from photons to electrical power. Choose 10.626 to model the thermodynamics–kinetics–transport chain inside batteries or fuel cells. Both concern energy, but their introductions cannot be swapped on the premise that electrochemistry and semiconductors merely “deal with energy.”
