## Course fit and prerequisites

The [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/10-626-electrochemical-energy-systems-spring-2014/pages/syllabus/) for MIT **10.626 Electrochemical Energy Systems, Spring 2014** lists **10.50 Analysis of Transport Phenomena** as a prerequisite and schedules 1-hour meetings 3 times per week. The course analyzes batteries, fuel cells, supercapacitors, and electrokinetic systems through equivalent circuits, thermodynamics, reaction kinetics, transport, electrostatics, porous media, and phase transformations. It is not battery trivia. Review transport first if you cannot derive a flux balance from a conservation law.

The campus grading split was problem sets 40%, take-home midterm 30%, and open-book final 30%. Those numbers describe the 2014 class, not an outside grader. Commercial textbooks and research readings do not become open merely because the course has an OCW page.

A compact entry set should span a diffusion problem derived from conservation with explicit boundaries; equilibrium voltage versus concentration and temperature through electrochemical potential or the Nernst relation; and complex impedance plus the high-frequency Nyquist intercept of a resistor–capacitor network. Each answer should state its electroneutrality, activity-versus-concentration, isothermal, and lumped-interface assumptions. A correct value without those conditions is not enough preparation for a porous-electrode model.

## Course structure

The [Calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/10-626-electrochemical-energy-systems-spring-2014/pages/calendar/) lists 38 sessions. Session 1 is syllabus and overview, Session 2 covers galvanic-cell physics, and Session 3 introduces electrochemical storage. A useful study regrouping then puts circuit/impedance in Session 4–7, thermodynamics in 8–15, kinetics in 16–24, transport/reaction–diffusion in 25–34, and double layers, supercapacitors, porous media, and porous electrodes in 35–38.

When moving to a more complex model, keep the preceding limiting case, conservation residual, parameter table, and baseline. Ask whether equivalent-circuit parameters are identifiable; check Butler–Volmer behavior at small and large overpotential; test transport for mesh, time-step, and mass balance; and retain sources and units for porous-media effective coefficients and interface area.

The [Lecture Notes index](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/10-626-electrochemical-energy-systems-spring-2014/pages/lecture-notes/) mixes 2014 student scribe notes, older 2011/2012 notes, textbook chapters, and papers. It is not a set of 38 same-version instructor notes, so record source year and document type.

## After 5 unsolved problem sets, the midterm is the one complete feedback point

[Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/10-626-electrochemical-energy-systems-spring-2014/pages/assignments/) provides Problem Set 1–5 and states that solutions are unavailable. Preserve governing equations, boundary conditions, units, limiting cases, and numerical residuals in the first attempt. Cross-check with an analytical special case, conservation, or 2 discretizations rather than an unverified answer archive.

[Exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/10-626-electrochemical-energy-systems-spring-2014/pages/exams/) publishes the 2014 midterm with a solution and the final without a solution. Attempt the midterm independently, then classify corrections by model choice, sign/units, algebra, boundary conditions, and physical interpretation. Use the final as an open-book transfer test. A porous-transport gap points back to Session 25–38; a self-score is not an MIT score.

## Test fitted parameters away from the trace that produced them

A useful independent study implements equivalent circuits, a Nernst/thermodynamic relation, Butler–Volmer kinetics, diffusion, and porous coupling, then tests fitted parameters on held-out current, temperature, or concentration ranges. Explain residual structure, conservation, and parameter identifiability. Reusing one trace for model selection, fitting, and acceptance supplies no transfer evidence. Public data needs a license, temperature, cycling protocol, and missing-metadata note; synthetic data must be labeled.

This is not an MIT hardware lab. The public pages supply no BOM, code grader, or battery-cycling setup, and homemade cells, battery disassembly, overcharge, short-circuit, and thermal-abuse tests are outside this route. For conversion from photons to electrical power, choose TU Delft [Photovoltaic Energy Conversion](https://ocw.tudelft.nl/courses/solar-energy-engineering-photovoltaic-energy-conversion/). Stay with 10.626 for coupled thermodynamics, kinetics, and transport inside a battery or fuel cell. A model-to-data report that names its valid range and failure point is a fitting result.
