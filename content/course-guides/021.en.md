## The best part of 6.002 is that the algebra cannot hide from the bench

MIT [6.002](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/) is often described as an introductory circuits course, which is only half the story. It begins with the lumped-circuit abstraction, KCL and KVL, and Thévenin and Norton equivalents, then threads MOS switches, bias and small-signal models, dynamics, sinusoidal steady state, filters, op-amp feedback, and CMOS through laboratory work. The official [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/pages/syllabus/) names measurement, construction, model comparison, and an audio playback system as outcomes. The residential shape was two one-hour lectures and one one-hour recitation each week, with 11 homework sets, Labs 1–4, two quizzes, and a final; that describes the course, not a universal self-study workload. The readings schedule is open, but the assigned Agarwal–Lang textbook remains commercial; a chapter list is not an open full text. Watching the lectures means hearing the course; waveforms and faults are where the course actually happens.

The page says Spring 2007, while much of the video and some teaching material came from Fall 2000, and Lecture 24 is incomplete. Following topics is more useful than forcing those editions into a false weekly calendar. Linear Circuits 1/2 is shorter if DC and AC analysis are the only gaps, and 6.091 is more direct if soldering and instrument habits are the goal. The reason to choose 6.002 is that it makes the circuit in the equations and the circuit under a probe become the same object before analog electronics, signals, or IC design.

## Three questions can save a bad start

The course page lists 8.02 and 18.03 as formal prerequisites. A better readiness test is practical: write KCL for a dependent-source network and explain the signs in its power balance; derive an RC or RL time constant from the differential equation, initial value, and final value; explain phasor impedance and phase with complex numbers. If all three feel ordinary, begin. If one does not, revisit that topic rather than replaying an entire calculus sequence.

The irritating errors later in the course are rarely algebra alone. A node direction changes, bias and incremental quantities get mixed, an initial value disappears, or an oscilloscope input becomes part of the load. Writing those conditions beside the circuit is worth more than another page of nearly identical exercises.

## Slow down for Labs 2–4, for three different reasons

The laboratory sequence moves from instruments and equivalent networks through logic gates, [Lab 2's MOSFET inverting amplifier and first-order circuits](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/resources/lab2/), [Lab 3's second-order networks](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/resources/lab3/), and the [Lab 4 audio playback system](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/resources/lab4/) prepared by Homework 11. Without the original bench, the topology can be rebuilt in current SPICE software and measured on isolated, low-voltage, current-limited hardware. That is a useful port, not a reproduction of MIT's setup.

Lab 2 is the right place to compare three views carefully: hand-calculated DC bias and incremental gain, the simulator's operating point, and the clipping boundary on an oscilloscope. In Lab 3, the same component values should explain poles and zeros, the Bode plot, and the step response. Lab 4 then integrates the clock, DAC, low-pass filter, and volume control into a demonstrable system. When they disagree, inspect meter loading, device range, and probe connection before blaming “component tolerance.” One failed waveform and its diagnosis usually reveals more about the lumped model than the final clean trace.

Steven J. Frank completed the first 14-week MITx 6.002x run from March through June 2012. His [IEEE Spectrum account](https://spectrum.ieee.org/review-mitxs-online-circuit-design-and-analysis-course) discusses videos, practice, problem sets, online laboratories, the midterm and final, and the debugging friction of the virtual circuit sandbox. It is first-person evidence about that early online course. His 2012 6.002x certificate is not evidence of completing the Spring 2007 residential Labs 0–4.

## Assignments and feedback

The [Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/pages/assignments/) page has Homework 1–11. The [Exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/pages/exams/) page has two quizzes, a final, and older practice papers, but there is no single complete solution trail. Dimensions, KCL and KVL residuals, limiting cases, SPICE, and measurement can check one another; an unresolved problem is better left marked than patched with a guess.

The old WebSim is unreliable in current browsers; without a bench, the [executable RC low-pass exercise (not an official MIT lab)](https://github.com/appleweiping/eediy/tree/main/examples/rc-lowpass) offers an analytic reference and an ngspice result, but no measured data. Then put hand analysis, simulation, and raw waveforms for one MOS amplifier or filter side by side, and identify the amplitude, frequency, or load at which the approximation stops working.
