## Course fit

The Berkeley [EECS 151 catalog](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Courses/EECS151/)
lecture covers Verilog, combinational and sequential logic, FSMs,
memory/cache/FIFO structures, pipelines, and power and reliability, but
students must also take at least 1 lab. An anonymous learner can see outcomes,
not the complete CalNet-gated slides, starters, servers, autograder, and live
feedback. Use the course as a digital-IC benchmark; the exercises below are
not 151LA or 151LB coursework.

## Tools and environment

[EECS 151LA](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Courses/EECS151LA/) is the ASIC
route, moving from HDL and testing through synthesis, floorplanning, placement
and routing, timing and power, and chip assembly.
[EECS 151LB](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Courses/EECS151LB/) maps a
processor, cache, and peripherals to an FPGA. Both use RTL, testbenches,
synthesis, and timing. ASIC work needs a legitimate standard-cell library or
PDK and physical closure; FPGA work needs a board, constraints, bitstream, and
host interface.

First implement a pipelined RTL block with a specification, latency and
clock/reset/CDC policy, reference model, assertions, and regression. Explain
critical path, resources, and warnings after synthesis, then hand-trace
hazards in a 3-stage RISC-V subset. If that is not manageable, review digital
logic and architecture.

## Assignments and feedback

The HKN [EECS 151 exam archive](https://hkn.eecs.berkeley.edu/exams/course/EECS/151)
has selected midterms, finals, and solutions from several years. They test bit
width, Verilog semantics, timing, pipelines, and memory; the project below
supplies the EDA flow or board demonstration. Keep the exam year beside each
attempt and use the score only for self-checking.

For engineering work, build a small RISC-V core or streaming accelerator. An
ASIC-like route can use a clearly licensed educational flow for synthesis,
placement and routing, STA, and DRC/LVS where supported. An FPGA-like route
can add implementation, timing, hardware counters, and a fixed-input demo.
Compare at least 2 design points under one workload using clock, cycles,
area/resources, a power proxy, and verification cost.

Fix tool version, constraints, input, and correctness oracle. Explain how
pipeline depth, cache size, or parallel lanes affect Fmax, cycles, area, and
verification effort. Put one
rejected design back in the table and name the timing, area, or correctness
constraint that eliminated it. That comparison is the point of EECS 151: the
same RTL decision often meets a different bottleneck in an ASIC flow than in
an FPGA flow.
