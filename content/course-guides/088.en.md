## Course materials

MIT [RES.6-008](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-6-008-digital-signal-processing-spring-2011/) is labeled Spring 2011, while its description says CAES produced the material in 1987 for working engineers. The chalkboard, image quality, and device examples belong to that period. Difference equations, the z-transform, the DFT, filter structures, and finite word length do not. It remains a remarkably compact first systematic route through discrete-time DSP. If continuous-time LTI systems, convolution, and Laplace and Fourier analysis are not fluent, take 6.003 first.

The course's virtue is its pace. Oppenheim carries one object from sequence to transform to structure without a slide deck chopping up the derivation. Its limitation is equally plain: the required text is the 1975 Oppenheim and Schafer book, and OCW gives section mappings rather than the full book.

## Course structure

The [Video Lectures](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-6-008-digital-signal-processing-spring-2011/video_galleries/video-lectures/) page contains Lectures 1–20 plus 2 sampling and aliasing demonstrations. They are not Lectures 21 and 22. Official [Study Materials](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/res-6-008-digital-signal-processing-spring-2011/pages/study-materials/) supplies 20 homework groups and 19 solution packets for Lectures 2–20.

Four blocks make a clean replay. Lectures 1–7 cover difference equations, convolution, the DTFT, and the z-transform; 8–10 cover DFS, DFT, and circular convolution; 11–17 cover structures, quantization, and IIR/FIR design; 18–20 finish with the FFT. In each block, make one small system agree in time, transform, block-diagram, and implementation forms. Causality, stability, ROC, index convention, and finite precision belong beside the result, not in an afterthought.

## One short sequence exposes most DFT confusion

Hand-compute the linear convolution of two short sequences. Choose padding and reproduce it by DFT multiplication, then remove the padding deliberately and watch time aliasing appear. Label a spectrum in bins, hertz, and normalized angular frequency, including window length. Increasing FFT size makes samples denser; it does not by itself improve physical resolution. If that distinction cannot be explained on the same short sequence, the DFT is not yet secure.

For filters, make FIR and IIR designs meet the same passband, stopband, and delay requirements, then compare multiply-accumulate cost and coefficient sensitivity. After changing an IIR from direct form to second-order sections, inspect impulse response, pole radius, internal-state range, overflow, and group delay as well as magnitude. The 1987 hardware can be skimmed; these numerical structures still govern fixed-point DSP, FPGA, and MCU work.

## A high-order IIR connects the old course to current tools

Sascha Spors's unofficial [Digital Signal Processing notebooks](https://github.com/spatialaudio/digital-signal-processing-lecture) are University of Rostock master's-course material under CC BY 4.0. They cover spectral analysis, random signals, quantization, filter realization and design, exercises, and CI. They provide a current Jupyter environment, not RES.6-008 answers.

A fitting finish is one stable high-order IIR: derive its difference equation, system function, ROC, and pole-zero plot; implement direct form and SOS; sweep coefficient word length. Follow output error as poles, internal state, and scaling change. The official archive has no exam, code lab, or project, so this is an explicitly unofficial extension. The 1987 material has reached today's tools when “identical ideal \(H(z)\)” no longer gets mistaken for identical finite-precision behavior.
