## Labs and projects

MIT 6.091 January IAP 2008 is an in-person laboratory boot camp, not a
semester of circuit theory. Its official
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-091-hands-on-introduction-to-electrical-engineering-lab-skills-january-iap-2008/pages/syllabus/)
says “three-week-long” in one place while the meeting table spans 4 weeks; the
reliable description is 5 lecture/lab pairs. Choose it if you already
understand voltage, current, and power and want real practice with a DMM,
oscilloscope, breadboard, and soldering.
The public OCW pages cannot supply the original equipment kit or live
feedback, and old parts need checks against available substitutes.

## Move from Component Recognition to a Mixed System

The [labs](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-091-hands-on-introduction-to-electrical-engineering-lab-skills-january-iap-2008/pages/labs/)
are the course spine. [Lab 1](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-091-hands-on-introduction-to-electrical-engineering-lab-skills-january-iap-2008/resources/lab1/)
starts with parts, meters, oscilloscopes, and soldering. The middle introduces
LED devices, BJT stages, MOSFET switches, op-amp circuits, 555 timers,
regulators, TTL, counters, and a DAC.
[Lab 5](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-091-hands-on-introduction-to-electrical-engineering-lab-skills-january-iap-2008/resources/lab5/)
connects motors, ADC/DAC work, and digital design. Historical parts can be
substituted only after rechecking pinout, logic levels, supply range, drive
current, and decoupling.

Predict first, then keep wiring, instrument settings, raw waveforms, and one
real debugging episode. Write the fault log as observation → smallest
hypothesis → one changed variable → new evidence. Check supply, ground, clock,
reset, and pinout before blaming a device. Preserve both truth tables and
timing for digital work, and operating point plus input/output waveforms for
analog work.

## Safety

Use only isolated, low-voltage, current-limited power; rewire with power off
and check shorts and polarity before turn-on. Add a suitable flyback path for
a motor and verify oscilloscope ground reference. Mains, exposed high voltage,
high-energy batteries, and large capacitor discharge do not belong in
personal practice. Simulation cannot complete a lab-skills course when no DMM,
oscilloscope, or safe supply is available.

The most revealing exercise is to rebuild one experiment from an empty bench.
The README should state pin orientation, interfaces, instrument settings,
expected readings, and common faults. If you can rebuild and debug it without
the original wiring photo, the bench habits have begun to stick.
