## Reorder 84 Lectures into Five Instrument Families, Not a Watchlist

IIT Kharagpur / NPTEL's
[Electrical Measurement and Electronic Instruments](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/108105153)
is taught by Prof. Avishek Chatterjee. The official course page preserves
Lectures 1–84 plus a dynamic live-session group: 84 numbered lectures, not 84
reproducible experiments. Numbered Lectures 3, 6, and 29 are themselves
demonstrations. The public theory spine has board derivations and equipment
demonstrations, but no home-lab BOM, raw data, or code repository.

The official [syllabus PDF](https://archive.nptel.ac.in/content/syllabus_pdf/108105153.pdf)
sets a 12-week plan, requiring circuit theory plus basic digital and analog
electronics, and divides the course into Electrical Measurements and
Electronic Instruments. EEDIY regroups it into 5 mechanism families:

1. Lectures 1–12: PMMC, electrodynamic, moving-iron, and electrostatic
   instruments; deflecting torque, damping, moving-coil dynamics, and the
   ballistic galvanometer.
2. Lectures 13–27: ammeters, voltmeters, ohmmeters, rectifier meters,
   4-terminal resistance, bridges, high resistance, and wattmeters.
3. Lectures 28–46: energy meters, DC and AC potentiometers, AC bridges, CT/PT,
   and magnetic measurement.
4. Lectures 47–70: counters, op-amps, Schmitt triggers, digital frequency
   meters, ramp, dual-slope, and successive-approximation voltmeters, ADCs,
   and DACs.
5. Lectures 71–84: electronic instruments, instrumentation amplifiers,
   function and pulse generators, the 555 timer, oscilloscopes,
   emitter-follower voltmeters, ohmmeters, and ramp generators.

This is an EEDIY map, not another NPTEL syllabus. Pause if first-order meter
dynamics, Thevenin loading, or the distinctions among accuracy, resolution,
sensitivity, and precision are missing; review transients, analog electronics,
and error propagation before 84 formulas become disconnected names.

## Validate Every Family along the Same Measurement Chain

Use measurand → conversion → loading/conditioning → indication → uncertainty,
not merely analog-to-digital chronology. For PMMC and moving-iron in Lectures
3 and 6, derive torque/damping and the connected meter's loading. At Lecture
23, join absolute, relative, and limiting error. CT/PT spans Lectures 42–44;
keep ratio error, phase error, and burden together rather than citing Lecture
42 alone.

For Lecture 58's frequency meter, account for gate time, quantization, and
input conditioning. Derive dual-slope DVM rejection/resolution in Lectures
64–65 from integrator, reference, and clock. Separate probe loading, vertical
path, time base, and trigger in Lectures 80–81. Each group card records range,
transfer relation, dominant errors, check method, and hazard boundary; missing
the last 3 is not measurement judgment.

Use 3 diagnostics: propagate input limiting errors through an indirect
measurement into absolute, relative, and worst-case error; derive Wheatstone
bridge sensitivity for a small resistance change and distinguish accuracy
from resolution; explain how dual-slope integration time changes line
rejection and conversion time. Return to Lecture 23, Lecture 24, or Lectures
64–65 after the 1st, 2nd, or 3rd failure. These are not NPTEL certificate
questions.

## Public Videos, Downloaded Assignments, and Live Feedback Are Different Access

Lectures 1–84 are publicly browsable, while live-session entries remain
dynamic. The official
[downloads API](https://nptel.ac.in/api/downloads/108105153) currently lists
84 numbered lectures plus one `Live 2` download, but its `assignments` array
is empty. Current assignments, discussion, scores, and the certificate exam
still vary with the NPTEL/SWAYAM session, login, and deadlines. The reviewed
[Week 1 archived assignment](https://archive.nptel.ac.in/content/storage2/courses/downloads_new/108105153/Week_01_Assignment_1a.pdf)
is an old portal-style PDF with multiple-choice items, accepted answers, and
historical metadata. The
[Week 6 archived assignment](https://archive.nptel.ac.in/content/storage2/courses/downloads_new/108105153/Week_06_Assignment_6.pdf)
preserves CT/PT questions and answers. They support partial self-checking, not
a current stepwise autograder, reproducible lab data, or code tests.

Hide accepted answers and preserve the first response. Old due dates, username
UI, and scores are not current registration. Report only assignments,
discussion, and exam information visible to the account; do not project the
PDF onto a 2026 session. Recheck rolling dates at enrollment.

University of Colorado Boulder's
[Sensors and Sensor Circuit Design](https://www.coursera.org/learn/sensors-circuit-interface)
is a possible practice complement. It lists 5 modules, 5 assignments, a
thermistor lab, and a course project, but materials and assignments belong to
the paid experience; its physical route needs a PSoC 5LP, components, LCD,
oscilloscope, and Windows toolchain. NPTEL is open theory; Coursera
adds practice only if paid access and legacy hardware are acceptable. Together
they still are not one fully open course with hardware feedback.

## Observe Mains, Three-Phase, and Instrument-Transformer Demonstrations; Do Not Rebuild Them

The official inventory includes single-phase meters, three-phase
wattmeter/energy-meter connections, CT/PT, magnetic measurement, and
oscilloscope demonstrations. This is classroom scope, not home authorization.
Do not reproduce energized work; never open a CT secondary with an energized
primary or attach an earth-referenced scope ground to unknown potential.
Shock, arc, overcurrent, ground-fault, and stored-energy hazards require rated
equipment, protection, procedures, and on-site supervision.

EEDIY supplements are analysis/simulation, not NPTEL labs: model voltmeter
loading; run resistor-tolerance Monte Carlo for Wheatstone/Kelvin bridges;
implement a discrete-time dual-slope DVM; sweep gate time versus frequency
quantization. Preserve schematic/equation, domain, units, seed, solver, and
raw output; check an analytic limit or archived assignment. An ideal SPICE
waveform is not calibrated measurement.

## A Completion Directory Should Expose What Was Never Measured

A record can contain 5 measurement-chain card sets, a Lecture 1–84 index,
first archived-assignment attempts with answers hidden, 3 simulations, and an
uncertainty ledger. Separate resolution, loading, reference tolerance,
quantization, and model-form error; label problem data, assumptions, and
unmeasured quantities. Keep the absent home lab, code grader, and current
certificate exam visible.

For a course change, report lecture/assignment number, session, access date,
and login state. For simulation, attach parameters, versions, and one rerun
command; never upload restricted questions or certificate-exam content. This
can advance R0 desk research without making an old export a permanent access
promise.
