## Why a Course Covers Both Motors and Lasers

MIT 6.007 Spring 2011 uses fields, energy, and ports to connect macroscopic
energy conversion to quantum devices. The first half treats actuators,
generators, and motors; the second reaches tunneling, light and matter, solar
cells, displays, and lasers. The official
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-007-electromagnetic-energy-from-motors-to-lasers-spring-2011/pages/syllabus/)
requires 8.02 or 6.01. Review circuits first if KVL/KCL, first-order circuits,
and signed power are unstable. Choose this course to understand how a physical
mechanism becomes terminal behavior, not as a replacement for a deep machines
or semiconductor course.

## Draw an Energy Flow for Every Topic

The [lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-007-electromagnetic-energy-from-motors-to-lasers-spring-2011/pages/lecture-notes/)
are the instructional source; there are no lecture videos. For each system,
draw input ports, stored energy, coupling, losses, and outputs, then work the
[10 problem sets](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-007-electromagnetic-energy-from-motors-to-lasers-spring-2011/pages/assignments/).
No solutions are public, so check conservation, steady-state limits, units,
and order of magnitude. At the quantum-device end, distinguish a microscopic
transition rate from an external I–V curve: density of states, occupation,
contacts, and parasitic elements still intervene.

## The Lab Choice Determines the Value

The campus course required 5 labs. [Lab 1: DC Motors](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-007-electromagnetic-energy-from-motors-to-lasers-spring-2011/resources/lab-1-dc-motors/)
connects electrical input, back EMF, and mechanical motion. The
[lab videos](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-007-electromagnetic-energy-from-motors-to-lasers-spring-2011/video_galleries/lab-videos)
are preparation, not measurement. Outside campus, favor isolated,
current-limited low-voltage equivalents and keep wiring, instrument settings,
raw data, and energy-closure error. Keep mains, high-energy capacitors,
unknown high voltage, and powerful lasers off a personal bench.

Finish by drawing a layered model of a converter or optoelectronic
device: physical mechanism, lumped equivalent, and measurable terminals.
Check it against safe measurements or datasheet data. Explaining the same loss
or discrepancy across every layer captures why the course travels from
motors to lasers. Keep the energy boundary and power sign convention
consistent across all three layers.
