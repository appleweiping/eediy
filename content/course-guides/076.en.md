## Who this course fits

MIT [6.270](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-270-autonomous-robot-design-competition-january-iap-2005/) preserves January IAP 2005: 4 weeks and 13 lectures of 3 hours, with teams building fully autonomous contest robots. The [Syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-270-autonomous-robot-design-competition-january-iap-2005/pages/syllabus/) records a kit worth about 1500 dollars per team, a long-hours lab, contest tables, and staff. The public site supplies none of those conditions.

There is no hard prerequisite, but the course draws on programming, circuits, digital systems, and mechanical design from 6.001, 6.002, 6.004, and 2.007. Current-limited motor control, a sensor decision with hysteresis, a nonblocking state machine, and a load/traction estimate test those abilities separately. An unmeasurable subsystem leaves no debugging basis for whole-robot integration.

## Assignments and feedback

The original syllabus says the first 2 weeks had 7 assignments; the current [Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-270-autonomous-robot-design-competition-january-iap-2005/pages/assignments/) page publishes only 6 numbered PDFs. Complete what is visible and do not invent assignment 7. Retain measurements, schematic/code, failure causes, and a demo for each so sensors, motors, controllers, and strategy connect progressively.

[Lecture Notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-270-autonomous-robot-design-competition-january-iap-2005/pages/lecture-notes/) and [Study Materials](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-270-autonomous-robot-design-competition-january-iap-2005/pages/study-materials/) describe the Handy Board and old battery/sensor/software environment. Build a substitution table for processor, driver, motor, encoder, detector, and mechanics, including voltage, current, sample rate, latency, and geometry. Replace unsafe old binaries with a modern MCU or simulator.

## Course structure

In week 1, establish power, emergency stop, download path, motor direction, and one sensor calibration. In week 2, settle chassis geometry and test straight motion, turns, relocalization, and manipulation. In week 3, encode rules as a state machine and test scoring actions, blocked paths, and sensor-timeout recovery. In week 4, keep core mechanisms unchanged and concentrate on qualifying runs, repairs, and the project page.

Define success and maximum duration before every full run. Save battery voltage, initial pose, commit, sensor log, state transitions, and outcome, with at least 3 repeats. Test hardware watchdog, software timeout, and de-energized stop independently. [Videos](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-270-autonomous-robot-design-competition-january-iap-2005/pages/videos/) show contest cadence, not the invisible debugging process.

## Access and version notes

Original recognition depended on timely assignments, a qualifying robot, and a public team page; winning was not the only condition. A public rebuild should deliver a 6-assignment archive, one complete qualifying demo that may fail, a module diagram, key tradeoffs, and successful and failed clips—without inventing MIT credit or rank.

[Related Resources](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-270-autonomous-robot-design-competition-january-iap-2005/pages/related-resources/) still cannot supply the kit, staff, or arena. Physical work needs a controlled battery/motor workspace, fusing/current limiting, mechanical guards, and an emergency stop. Otherwise use a low-power fixture or simulator and mark traction, collision, noise, and manipulation as unvalidated.

The old-kit substitution table is itself part of the result: it separates performance due to algorithms from performance due to components and the arena.
