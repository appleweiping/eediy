## Use One CMOS Gate to Decide Whether This 2003 Course Fits

MIT 6.374 is a Fall 2003 graduate course. The official
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-374-analysis-and-design-of-digital-integrated-circuits-fall-2003/pages/syllabus/)
sets a narrow but substantial prerequisite: 6.012 or equivalent device
physics, and 6.004 or equivalent logic design. Diagnose readiness with one
CMOS inverter: mark VTC noise margins; estimate rise/fall delay from devices
and load; account for transition energy; explain the layout-parasitic error.
If Verilog is stronger than sizing and capacitance, review MOS large- and
small-signal models plus RC. If devices are stronger than logic, review
latches, registers, and timing constraints. This is EEDIY routing, not an MIT
entrance test.

That distinction also locates the course relative to Berkeley EECS 151. The
current [EECS 151 catalog description](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Courses/EECS151/)
starts with Verilog, finite-state machines, arithmetic units, and memories,
then branches into FPGA and ASIC laboratories. 6.374 pushes down to delay,
energy, and layout at the CMOS gate, interconnect, clock, and memory-cell
levels. Choose that top-down route first; choose 6.374 after writing,
simulating, and verifying RTL, when the question becomes why its gates are
slow or energy-hungry. The separate, student-run EECS151 Tapeout course assumes EECS
151 and ASIC-lab background and uses Chisel, Chipyard, SKY130, and SoC
tapeout. It does not make MIT's 2003 flow current.

## Make the 24 Meetings Converge on One Delay–Energy–Layout Chain

The official [calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-374-analysis-and-design-of-digital-integrated-circuits-fall-2003/pages/calendar/)
lists 24 meetings. Lectures 1–4 cover short-channel MOS models, inverter VTC,
delay, capacitance, and switching, short-circuit, and leakage power. Lectures
5–9 compare static, ratioed, pass-transistor, and dynamic logic, then logical
effort, adders, multipliers, and shifters. Lectures 12–17 join latches,
registers, metastability, interconnect, skew/jitter, and clock distribution; 18–20 cover
ROM, PLA, SRAM, DRAM, and sense amplifiers. Lectures 21–22 treat voltage
scaling, activity reduction, and VLSI test; 23–24 are project presentations.

There is no public lecture-video series. “Lecture Notes” is mainly a
topic-to-textbook map pointing to slides for the 2nd edition of Rabaey,
Chandrakasan, and Nikolic. The slide site states Prentice Hall/Pearson
copyright, limited instructor use with the book, and no republication; the
text is separate. The executable core is the calendar, 5 problem sets, tool
documentation, quizzes, and project format, not a redistributable slide
archive. Keep each design quantity in one account:

- In the inverter block, keep the DC transfer curve, hand \(t_p\), SPICE
  transient, and extracted capacitance together instead of reporting one
  waveform.
- In the logic block, put topology, transistor count, worst-case path,
  activity factor, and energy together; “faster” is not every objective.
- In the sequential/interconnect block, state setup/hold, skew/jitter
  assumptions, wire RC, and buffer chain before interpreting timing numbers.
- In the memory/test block, separate cell, sense path, array organization, and
  fault model. A working SRAM schematic is not a measured read/write margin.

## The Five Sets Have Strong Feedback; the Two Quizzes Do Not

The [assignments page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-374-analysis-and-design-of-digital-integrated-circuits-fall-2003/pages/assignments/)
publishes Problem Sets 1–5, all solved, with FAQs for PS1–PS4; PS3 and PS5
reference 0.25 µm CMOS parameters. Record model, approximations, and units
before reading a solution, then classify discrepancies as device region,
missing capacitance, critical path, or energy/power confusion. Read the FAQ
after the first attempt so it can still diagnose the modeling gap.

The [exams page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-374-analysis-and-design-of-digital-integrated-circuits-fall-2003/pages/exams/)
lists only the Quiz 1 and Quiz 2 papers. Although the course index carries an
“Exam Solutions” resource label, neither the reviewed exams page nor the two
downloaded files lists a separate solution. Do not turn that metadata label
into a claim that these are 2 solved exams. Quiz 1 covers the inverter and
combinational logic; Quiz 2 covers arithmetic, interconnect, sequential
circuits, and memory. Complete each closed-book, preserve the first script,
and cross-check analogous models against solved sets. Put unresolved items in
an errata list rather than use an unknown answer copy.

The official weights reinforce where the feedback lies: 5 problem sets count
for 20%, the 2 quizzes for 25% each, and the final design project for 30%.
The direct [project-guidelines PDF](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-374-analysis-and-design-of-digital-integrated-circuits-fall-2003/3fd5ad1346a5389abc54bd158606a17a_guidelines.pdf)
sets a 2-page limit and requires worked/failed ideas, 3 innovative ideas, and
a simulation path; a separate 1-page technical-summary form also exists.
Neither supplies a common reproducible specification or sample result. These
are reporting rules, not a published circuit assignment.

## Keep Magic, HSPICE, and 0.25 µm in a Historical Lane

The official [tools page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-374-analysis-and-design-of-digital-integrated-circuits-fall-2003/pages/tools/)
names Magic, HSPICE, and NanoSim; the syllabus also mentions IRSIM. The page
publishes Magic/HSPICE documentation and external 0.18/0.25 µm model links,
but does not itself grant present-day HSPICE/NanoSim use or establish that old
models remain lawfully available and compatible. Mark commercial-tool access
as learner-verified, not publicly downloadable by inference. Substituting a
modern open PDK into the PS3 0.25 µm parameters and claiming the official
solution is invalid; process, model card, supply, temperature, or extraction
changes create another experiment.

Keep 2 records. In the historical lane, use stated parameters and mark
simulation without a lawful tool/model as not executed. The modern lane is an
EEDIY supplement, not an MIT lab. In one licensed, version-pinned PDK/EDA
flow, choose an inverter and logic path; submit hand RC/energy, pre-layout
SPICE, DRC-clean layout, extraction, and post-layout comparison. Predict each
slew, load, supply, and size sweep, then explain differences without imposing
one threshold across PDKs.

## Completion Evidence Must Locate the Layer Where a Model Failed

A credible record contains corrected PS1–PS5 attempts, timed first attempts
for 2 quizzes, an assumption ledger, and a delay–energy–layout audit. Preserve
schematic/netlist, model and tool versions, sweeps, raw output, layout,
DRC/extraction, and the cause of pre/post-layout differences. Without lawful
HSPICE/NanoSim, process files, or layout flow, stop at solved sets and
analysis; a missing tool does not waive its license.

The course can still anchor transistor-level digital IC study, but it is not
a modern RTL-to-GDS installation manual or proof of tapeout readiness. A
course report should state whether it used the official 0.25 µm problem data
or which modern PDK, the exact tool versions, and whether work ended before or
after layout extraction. Include a minimal reproduction path that contains no
restricted model. That evidence lets the next learner distinguish a concept
error from an age or license boundary without presenting off-campus
simulation as a complete reproduction of MIT 6.374.
