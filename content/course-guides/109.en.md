## Access and version notes

MIT [6.630 Fall 2006](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-630-electromagnetics-fall-2006/)
uses its [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-630-electromagnetics-fall-2006/pages/syllabus/)
to divide 14 weeks among Maxwell equations and polarization, transmission
lines, boundaries/waveguides/materials, and lumped/loaded line structures. It
fits learners who know vector calculus, phasors, ODEs, RLC circuits, and the
telegrapher equation and want to connect field boundaries to guided
structures. Review fields and circuits first if deriving a plane wave from
phasor Maxwell equations, explaining reflection, or writing tangential/normal
boundary conditions is still difficult.

## Use One Continuous Text to Bridge Gaps between the Notes

The [study materials](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-630-electromagnetics-fall-2006/pages/study-materials/)
are reading entries and partial files, not a self-contained text. Fix
time/frequency domain, sources, loss, material, and reference direction, then
check dimensions, energy flow, and limiting cases. A Hertzian dipole,
terminated line, and rectangular waveguide can persist throughout, connecting
radiation scaling, standing waves/input impedance, and TE/TM cutoff rather
than becoming a 14-week list of PDF summaries.

## Assignments and feedback

The [assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-630-electromagnetics-fall-2006/pages/assignments/)
publish 10 problem sets without solutions. The
[exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-630-electromagnetics-fall-2006/pages/exams/)
provide only 2 quiz prompts, without rubric. Calibrate work through units,
limits, power conservation, or an independent sweep, retaining unverifiable
claims as unresolved. Use quizzes as closed-book diagnostics and classify
corrections by assumption, boundary condition, phasor sign, or algebra rather
than inferring correctness from a smooth curve.

## Use MATLAB Only to Test an Approximation

The [tools](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-630-electromagnetics-fall-2006/pages/tools/)
retain dipole, media, resonance, reflection-coefficient, and array scripts,
while Parallel Plate Waveguide movies/scripts are marked no longer available.
These are neither classroom videos nor hardware labs. Finish with a terminated
line or rectangular waveguide report containing analytic boundaries, power
flow, a numerical sweep, and approximation breakdown. Record units,
normalization, and parameters, alter a single physical quantity, and predict
the trend. Treat a missing movie as a gap rather than imagining its result.
Keep the analytic solution, discrete sampling, and plotting window distinct so
a discrepancy can be traced to a physical approximation or to the display.
For a transmission line, compare power flow under open, short, and matched
loads; for a waveguide, check propagation constant and energy direction on
both sides of cutoff.

The habit 6.630 should leave is asking whether a smooth curve comes from the
boundary condition, an approximation, or the plotting window. Transmission
lines and waveguides both demand that distinction.
