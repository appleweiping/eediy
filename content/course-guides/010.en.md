## Draw the System Boundary Before Choosing \(F=ma\) or Conservation

The MIT [8.01SC course home](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/)
identifies the Fall 2016 Classical Mechanics Scholar edition. The public
course adapts the original into 12 weeks: a Vectors Review and Lesson 0,
followed by 37 numbered lessons on kinematics, Newton's laws, circular
motion, drag/constraints, momentum, continuous mass transfer, work/energy,
collisions, rotation, angular momentum, and rolling. Deep Dives treat
friction, the center-of-mass frame, and gyroscopes. It is not a
chapter-by-chapter reading of a textbook. It repeatedly asks what belongs to the system, how the
environment acts, and which quantity can cross-check the model.

For an EE learner, that question connects concretely to torque and energy in
motor shafts and flywheels, angular momentum in rotors and gyroscopes,
constraints and drag in actuators, and the distinction between an
accelerometer's specific-force reading and acceleration expressed in a chosen
reference frame. 8.01SC does not teach electromagnetic motor
torque, sensor circuits, or feedback control. Connecting mechanics to those
objects is an EEDIY transfer interpretation, not an MIT mechatronics lab.

## Let Twelve Weeks Merge Two Ledgers

The first is a force–momentum ledger: choose a system boundary, draw a
free-body diagram, list external forces and constraints, then predict motion
from \(\sum\mathbf F=d\mathbf p/dt\). The second is a work–energy ledger:
state conservative and non-conservative work, then compare kinetic,
potential, and dissipated energy. Weeks 1–4 establish kinematics, forces,
circular motion, drag, and constraints. Weeks 5–9 add momentum, mass
transfer, work/energy, and collision as independent checks. Weeks 10–12 map
translation into moment of inertia, torque, angular momentum, and rolling.

Do not compress this into “a block of translation formulas followed by a
block of rotation formulas.” State the system and positive axes first, then try the
dynamics and conservation ledgers separately. When they disagree, search for
a missing external force, friction work, mass flow, collision type, or
rolling constraint. The hard step is often not differentiation but deciding
where the boundary passes, whether friction is external or internal, and
whether mechanical energy is conserved at all.

## Diagnose Readiness with Three No-Equipment Reconstructions

The official [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/syllabus/)
lists no prerequisite and makes 18.01SC a corequisite. The original class met
for 2 lectures of 2 hours and 1 problem-solving session of 1 hour each week.
The absence of a formal prerequisite does not eliminate the need for algebra,
trigonometry, vectors, and derivatives. Before Week 1, perform three EEDIY
diagnostics; they are not official MIT exams:

1. From an \(x(t)\) plot, draw \(v(t)\) and \(a(t)\), including signs, units,
   and non-differentiable points. If curve height is confused with velocity,
   review how functions and derivatives are represented graphically.
2. Draw only external forces for a block on an incline, choose 2 different
   coordinate systems, and obtain the same acceleration. If normal force,
   weight components, and positive direction merge, practice free-body
   diagrams first.
3. For a one-dimensional collision of 2 particles, state the system and
   collision type before deciding whether momentum and kinetic energy are
   conserved. If “conserved” has no boundary and condition, postpone Week 5.

None of these checks needs a real incline, launcher, or rotating apparatus.
Keep practice simulation-only; do not construct high-speed rotating,
projectile, or collision experiments for “verification.”

## A Public Worked Example Is Not a Problem-Set Answer

The official [Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/assignments/)
index lists problem PDFs for Problem Sets 1–12 and, beside most weeks,
related worked examples. A worked example demonstrates a nearby modeling
process but is not a line-by-line solution to PS1–PS12. The reviewed public
assignment table has no solution links, the navigation has no exams page,
and the course home lists only Lecture Videos and Problem Sets as resource
types. This negative finding is limited to the current public OCW indexes; it
does not claim that the original MIT staff had no keys or exams.

The syllabus describes the 2016 classroom grading: 3 midterms total 45%, the
final 25%, problem sets 10%, and participation 20%. Those weights show that
the campus class once depended on exams and classroom feedback; they do not
make those exams public today. An independent learner should attempt the PS,
then use a nearby worked example to check boundary and method. Ask a peer or
discussion partner to inspect only the free-body diagram, conservation
conditions, and units before revealing a final number. Keep problems without a reliable
answer as unresolved rather than filling the gap from an unknown key.

Use a three-color protocol: black preserves the first solution, blue records
corrections supported by the worked example, and red lists assumptions still
unverified. Each week, choose at least 1 problem that can be checked by 2
valid methods using \(\sum\mathbf F\) and energy/momentum. If only one method
applies, state precisely why the other does not.

## Thirty-One Textbook Chapters Are Not Transcripts of Thirty-Seven Lessons

The official [Online Textbook](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/online-textbook/)
page says the text incorporated reader corrections in 2022 and lists 31
numbered chapters plus physical constants and astronomical data. Its scope
continues into simple harmonic motion, fluids, Navier–Stokes, and
non-inertial frames, beyond the public 12-week video/PS spine ending at
rolling and gyroscopes. Use Weeks 1–12 as the required spine and the matching
text chapters as a second explanation. Do not claim all 31 chapters are
required, and do not turn a 2022 correction update into a claim that the
course was re-recorded in 2022.

The official main path requires no dedicated software. A trajectory plotted
with Python or another simulator is an EEDIY check and must record
integrator, step size, parameters, units, and energy/momentum drift. A
software plot cannot replace a free-body diagram, and numerical stability is
not physical stability.

## 8.012 Is a Faster, Deeper Branch—not the Next Step

The Fall 2008 [8.012 syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-012-physics-i-classical-mechanics-fall-2008/pages/syllabus/)
calls it the advanced version of first-year mechanics: the same material as
8.01/8.01L at a faster pace and somewhat greater depth, for students with
mastery of single-variable calculus and vector algebra plus an interest in
physics. The original course used Kleppner and Kolenkow and had weekly
assignments, 3 midterms, 1 comprehensive final, and a project. Choose 8.01SC
when segmented explanations, worked examples, and a 12-week modeling cadence
are needed. Move to 8.012 after non-inertial frames, rigid-body reasoning, and
longer derivations are independently manageable. The public resource sets
differ; an 8.012 solution is not an official 8.01SC answer.

## Finish with Auditable Mechanics Ledgers, Not a Hazardous Demonstration

Using the official [Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-01sc-classical-mechanics-fall-2016/pages/assignments/)
spine, completion evidence should contain first attempts for all 12 problem
sets, 1 three-color correction per week, a question index over 37 lessons, and a
translation–rotation map. Its rows should connect mass/\(m\), force/\(F\),
momentum/\(p\), and kinetic energy with moment of inertia/\(I\),
torque/\(\tau\), angular momentum/\(L\), and rotational energy, including
conditions of use.

An optional **EEDIY digital experiment—an EEDIY supplement, not an official
assignment**, may use an existing low-risk motion clip from the course or
synthetic data; construct no physical apparatus. Extract \(x(t)\) or
\(\theta(t)\) for one translational and one rolling model, predict each with
the dynamics and energy/momentum ledgers, sweep time step, and report
residual and conservation drift. Submit the original or synthetic data
source, coordinate calibration, equations, code environment, raw output, and
one rerun command.

This is R0 desk research over public materials and makes no first-hand
8.01SC completion claim. Feedback should name Week/Lesson/PS, system boundary,
signs and units, whether a solution/exam link appeared on the public page,
and a minimal reproduction. Do not report an unverifiable answer as a course
error or upload a video that invites hazardous physical replication.
