## Course fit

MIT graduate [3.46 Photonic Materials and Devices, Spring 2006](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-46-photonic-materials-and-devices-spring-2006/) met 2 times per week for 1.5 hours. Its [Syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-46-photonic-materials-and-devices-spring-2006/pages/syllabus/) uses the commercial Saleh and Teich text as the primary reading; it is not in the free package. Enter with electromagnetic wave/phasor analysis, basic quantum/semiconductor physics, linear algebra, and the ability to read slab-waveguide, transfer-matrix, or coupled-mode models.

Material data, devices, and manufacturing context reflect 2006. The mode, resonance, carrier, and nonlinear relationships remain valuable, but state-of-the-art comparisons require modern primary literature.

## Course structure

[Lecture Notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-46-photonic-materials-and-devices-spring-2006/pages/lecture-notes/) moves from optical material, waveguide, WDM filter, ring resonator, and photonic crystal through the semiconductor electron/photon model, LED and laser behavior, crystal growth and defect/strain, and then nonlinear optics, modulators, III–V processing, detectors, and micro/nanophotonics.

Connect all 21 lectures with `material → geometry → mode/carrier → device metric → system tradeoff`. In the first block, check boundaries, normalization, dispersion, loss, and coupling. In the middle, put band structure, carrier distribution, recombination, gain, and threshold in one budget. In the final block, compare speed, efficiency, noise, insertion loss, and fabrication tolerance. Keep wavelength, temperature, and source with old material parameters; recalculate when replacing them.

## Assignments and feedback

[Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-46-photonic-materials-and-devices-spring-2006/pages/assignments/) has 7 homework sets without solutions. State the model and approximation before checking dimensions, limits, and order of magnitude. Use independent sweeps for waveguides/resonators and particle or power balance for carrier/laser work.

[Exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-46-photonic-materials-and-devices-spring-2006/pages/exams/) provides 7 weekly quizzes and a final, also without solutions. Take each quiz in 20–30 minutes and reserve the final until after the first device-design iteration. Mark results without an independent check uncalibrated, and do not label third-party work as an MIT answer.

## Use 4 device-design iterations to connect the course

[Projects](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/3-46-photonic-materials-and-devices-spring-2006/pages/projects/) has 4 staged design checkpoints. Original teams usually paired 2–3 students at each of MIT and Singapore, gave an approximately 20-minute presentation in 5–6 slides, then submitted corrected slides and a 2-page report. Topics include waveguides/solar/Bragg/fiber, an erbium ring laser, a detector, and a real-time design task.

The public package has no fabrication, bench protocol, measurement data, code repository, or tests. These are design exercises, not labs. An independent version can deliver requirements, an analytical model, parameter sweep, tolerance/noise budget, 5–6 slides, and a revised 2-page report, with MATLAB or other code labeled learner-written.

Across the 4 iterations, tighten the device chain: first freeze optical function, geometry, and material; then audit gain/loss or source budget; next inspect detector noise, responsivity, bandwidth, and readout interfaces; finally defend system tradeoffs. For an erbium ring laser, check threshold and loss. For a detector, compare dark current, area, and bandwidth. For waveguide/Bragg work, check cutoff, reflection bandwidth, and fabrication bias. Show a hand-derived limit, numerical sweep, and rejected branch for every important choice.

A sound exit is 7 homework sets, 7 quizzes, the final, at least 2 project iterations, and a 1-page 2006/current comparison. Let 1 design connect material, confinement, gain/loss, source/detector noise, readout, and fabrication tolerance. Without measurement, the conclusion stops at “works under the stated model and assumptions.”

When substituting modern material data, recalculate confinement, loss, threshold, and thermal budget together, and list the old value, conditions, and source in the comparison. Replacing only an attractive refractive-index or efficiency number breaks the design evidence.

Take the hardest question from each presentation into the next iteration and answer it with a hand calculation, sweep, or tolerance case. That progression explains why the design holds more clearly than a polished final curve.
