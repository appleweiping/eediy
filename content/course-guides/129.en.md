## Course fit

MIT **6.777J / 2.372J Design and Fabrication of Microelectromechanical Devices, Spring 2007** was taught by Carol Livermore and Joel Voldman. Its syllabus describes 2 meetings per week of 1.5 hours and says prior microfabrication experience is desirable. The public spine is device design, lumped modeling, transduction, readout, packaging, and tradeoffs. It supplies no PDK, recipe, mask submission, fabrication, or test service.

A learner entering directly should be able to derive energy, force, and
stability for a parallel-plate actuator; estimate cantilever stiffness, first
resonance, and scaling; infer deposition, lithography, etch,
sacrificial-layer, and release constraints from a final cross-section; and
connect a capacitive/resistive sensor to a small-signal readout with noise
referred to its input.

## Use One Device to Check Mechanics, Electronics, and Process

The [Calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-777j-design-and-fabrication-of-microelectromechanical-devices-spring-2007/pages/calendar/) and [Lecture Notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-777j-design-and-fabrication-of-microelectromechanical-devices-spring-2007/pages/lecture-notes/) cover 25 lectures. Maintain `specification → process cross-section → transducer model → electronics/noise/feedback → package/test`.

There is no need to invent a separate “deliverable” for every lecture. A capacitive accelerometer makes a useful thread through the course: materials and process in Lectures 1–5 determine its beams, proof mass, gap, and release; Lectures 6–18 explain its response through stiffness, resonance, damping, capacitance change, and readout noise; Lectures 19–25 bring packaging, tradeoffs, and the accelerometer itself back together.

Gap is a revealing tradeoff to follow. A smaller gap improves capacitive sensitivity and electrostatic force, but makes pull-in, stiction, process variation, and package parasitics harder; beam thickness, proof-mass area, and cavity pressure change bandwidth and noise at the same time. Keeping those effects on one device teaches more design judgment than several unrelated finite-element plots.

Begin with a lumped model simple enough to calculate by hand. A higher-fidelity model earns its place only when fringing fields, nonlinear displacement, or package damping could change the decision; it should answer a question the first-order model cannot, rather than bury boundary conditions and the linearization point in software defaults.

## Assignments and feedback

[Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-777j-design-and-fabrication-of-microelectromechanical-devices-spring-2007/pages/assignments/) has Problem Set 1–7. Solutions exist for Problem Set 1–4, 6, and 7, but not Problem Set 5. The page also has the Treasure Hunt and `prob6_10.m` and `prob6_11.m` for Problem Set 4. Attempt work independently, then classify corrections by assumption, equation, unit, order of magnitude, and numerical implementation. For old `.m` files, record the MATLAB/Octave version and smallest compatibility diff.

The original [Syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-777j-design-and-fabrication-of-microelectromechanical-devices-spring-2007/pages/syllabus/) assigns 15% to 7 homework sets, 35% to an unpublished take-home design problem, and 50% to the final project. An independent design review can restore practice, but cannot create an MIT grade.

## Reproduce the team project’s stage reviews

The [Project](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-777j-design-and-fabrication-of-microelectromechanical-devices-spring-2007/pages/projects/) uses teams of 4–5. At Lecture 17 it asks for a 1–2-page preliminary report; at Lecture 22, a 2–5-page interim report; 5 days after Lecture 25, a 30-minute presentation; and finally a 25–30-page report. Examples include a DEP sorter above 1000 cells/s, a piezoresistive biomolecular sensor, an RF-IF filter, and a micro flex-tester.

Without facilities, the project can become a design study of one device, but it should not pretend that fabrication occurred. Follow the accelerometer above or one of the official examples, choose one decision-driving question—release gap, package pressure, or input-referred noise—and use a cross-section, estimates, and a numerical model to show which choice is more credible and what still requires physical measurement.

## Access and version notes

Use the [Laboratory Safety guide](../../guides/safety.md) for general fabrication stop conditions. Outside MIT, a learner can still reason through a device specification, cross-section, models, readout/noise, and package; without fabrication and testing, do not describe CAD or a process diagram as fabricated or measured.

[MIT 6.152J](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-152j-micro-nano-processing-technology-fall-2005/)
emphasizes diffusion, oxidation, lithography, CVD, and institutional
fabrication/testing. 6.777J moves from specification through device, readout,
and package. Pick one parameter—beam thickness, gap, or cavity pressure—and
follow how it changes mechanics/thermal/fluid behavior, input-referred noise,
package constraints, and the final observable. The hard and valuable part of
6.777J is making all those changes belong to the same device rather than to
several unrelated model plots.
