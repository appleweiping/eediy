## First Confirm That You Want a Laboratory Course

The operative verbs in MIT 6.101 are design, construction, and debugging. The
Spring 2007 offering used 6 labs in the first half and 7 weeks for an analog
product in the second. Its subjects run from diodes, BJTs, JFETs, and op-amps
to audio and RF. No video sequence turns the bench into “watch and finish,”
and SPICE screenshots do not reproduce component spread, wiring, or
measurement.

The official syllabus treats passable performance in 6.002 or 6.071 as
sufficient background. For independent study, the following is an EEDIY
editorial self-check, not a new institutional prerequisite: try biasing a
common-emitter stage and estimating its small-signal gain, reading a corner
from a Bode plot, explaining op-amp saturation/slew rate/closed-loop bandwidth,
and measuring an RC network with a current-limited supply, multimeter,
oscilloscope, and function generator. Review the minimum background behind
whichever step stalls. There is no universal passing score. Anyone new to the
instruments should first learn probe grounding, range selection, and power-up
order on a supervised low-energy bench.

## Decide Lab by Lab: Perform, Adapt, or Omit

The official [Labs page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-101-introductory-analog-electronics-laboratory-spring-2007/pages/labs/)
publishes all 6 handouts. The table keeps the original task, present-day scope,
principal hazard, and check-off together. Every adaptation is an EEDIY choice,
not an update to MIT requirements.

| Lab | Original work | Independent-study treatment | Principal hazard | What the check-off must answer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Tuned circuits, Q, bandwidth, RF transmit/receive, and AM envelope detection | Use SPICE only, or measure through a dummy load or shielded wired attenuated path; no antenna or free-space transmission at home | Unauthorized radiation, wrong probe grounding, and resonant overvoltage | Do predicted and measured \(f_0,Q,BW\) agree; when does the envelope distort; is the connection non-radiating? |
| 2 | Log amplifier, rectification, regulation, and diode/BJT/FET curves on Tektronix 575/577-177 tracers | Use a low-voltage current-limited sweep and sense resistor, or a modern curve tracer in a compliant laboratory | High-voltage terminals on old instruments, reversed devices, and excessive sweep current | Preserve raw IV data, range, and resolution; report model parameters and residuals, not only a plot |
| 3 | Bias, low-frequency cutoff, a hand-wound inductor, and a FET analog switch | Simulate first, then measure at small signal with current limiting; do not reproduce a coil drive with unknown stored energy | Inductive kick, wrong bias, and device heating | Predict the operating point before power; measure cutoff, switch on/off error, and inductor parameters |
| 4 | LM741/LF356 gain, bandwidth, slew rate, and saturation; adder, Schmitt trigger, integrator, and differentiator | A current op-amp may be used, but recalculate supply, pinout, common mode, output swing, and stability for every replacement | Supply/pin mistakes, output oscillation, and probe loading | Compare calculation, SPICE, and waveform for one input; explain which non-ideality limits the output first |
| 5 | Op-amp, push-pull stage, differential pair, current sources, 555 VCO, and a power amplifier | Start into a resistive dummy load, then use a small speaker; do not reproduce a 400 W project | Output short, DC offset, transistor thermal runaway, and sound pressure | Record no-load/loaded DC offset, supply current, load power, temperature rise, and continuous-run drift |
| 6 | Series-pass regulator, low-battery indicator, 3-input mixer, and gain control | Measure line/load regulation by block before low-amplitude audio; do not connect a mains rectifier | Supply short, pass-transistor heating, and audio feedback | Sweep input and load; identify where dropout, thermal limitation, crosstalk, and clipping begin |

Labs 1, 5, and 6 originally had staff check-offs, and MIT estimated about
10 hours per week for the first half. The public archive cannot reproduce
those live questions. An independent record should retain a node-labelled
schematic, measured component values, pre-power prediction, supply current
limit, raw waveform/CSV, calculation-versus-simulation-versus-measurement
comparison, and one genuine debug trail.

The WaveForms 3.24.4 [official manual](https://files.digilent.com/manuals/WaveForms/3.24.4/start10.html)
documents the AD3’s two adjustable supplies as 0.5–5 V and -0.5–-5 V, with a
shared 0.6–15 W total-power limit, but does not document a user-set per-output
CC control. This tier is therefore designed around 5 V circuits and must not
be represented as a CV/CC bench supply. It uses a calculated series resistor
to bound worst-case short current plus a suitable fuse/polyfuse. The Current
and Power Adapter measures current and power; it is not protection. The
laboratory-tier DPS3340 provides CV/CC and overcurrent settings, with outputs
including 1–5 V / 10 mA–3 A, -1–-15 V / -10 mA–-500 mA, and 1–15 V /
10 mA–500 mA. Its current limit must still be set within the circuit’s own
ratings.

## A Purchasable Device Is Not a Drop-In Replacement

The 2007 handouts use LM741, LF356, 2N2219, 2N2905, and 555 devices and assume
the Building 38 stockroom and benches. The current Analog Parts Kit lists
AD8542, ADTL082, OP27/OP37/OP97/OP482/OP484, 2N3904/2N3906, TIP31/TIP32,
IRF510, ZVN2110A, 1N914/1N4001/1N4735, an 8 Ω speaker, and a 6.2 Ω 10 W power
resistor. These are available practice parts, not pin-compatible or
behavior-equivalent replacements for the historical devices.

For every substitution, recheck the datasheet supply range, pinout, input
common mode, output swing, gain-bandwidth, slew rate, noise, output current,
package, and thermal dissipation. In particular, do not preserve the old bias
or load when replacing 2N2219 with 2N3904 or LF356 with ADTL082. The supplier
also says kit contents may change for lead time, cost, or MOQ and that the web
list may lag. Build the actual BOM from the received markings and datasheets.

## 5–12 V Is This Guide’s Working Scope, Not a Safety Threshold

The official [Kit warnings](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-101-introductory-analog-electronics-laboratory-spring-2007/resources/kit_warnings/)
mention historical supplies of ±15 V/500 mA, ±12 V/1 A, and even laboratory
±18 V/6 A. EEDIY limits an independent adaptation to isolated DC, normally
5 V and never above 12 V, to narrow the design and fault space. That range is
not a declaration of safety: high current, stored energy, or a short at low
voltage can still burn devices and wiring or start a fire. Perform these checks
before power:

1. Rewire with power off and perform a continuity/short test. If the supply has
   adjustable CC, set voltage first and put its current limit below the weakest
   wire, connector, device, or load. The home AD3 tier lacks that control, so
   use \(R_{\mathrm{series}}\ge V_{\mathrm{supply}}/I_{\mathrm{fault,max}}\)
   to bound worst-case fault current, then select a fuse/polyfuse from allowable
   current, trip behavior, and fault energy. A fuse is not a precision limiter.
2. Write down \(E_C=\tfrac12CV^2\), \(E_L=\tfrac12LI^2\), and \(P=VI\) for
   critical parts. Discharge through a resistor and verify with a meter; never
   short a capacitor with fingers or a screwdriver. Provide a suitable clamp
   for inductive current.
3. Estimate resistor/transistor/IC temperature with datasheet derating, then
   measure it with a thermocouple or infrared instrument, not a finger. Cut
   power immediately if temperature keeps rising, odor appears, or current
   departs from prediction.
4. Test a power amplifier first into the 6.2 Ω 10 W dummy load. Check output
   DC offset, quiescent current, and shorts before connecting the 8 Ω speaker
   at low volume. Do not use headphones on the prototype; mute before rewiring
   and stop on feedback, obvious distortion, or heating.
5. At home, Lab 1 is limited to SPICE, a dummy load, or a shielded wired path:
   no antenna and no free-space RF transmission. Controlled radiation is
   considered only with a compliant laboratory, qualified supervision, and
   applicable local spectrum authorization.

Mains rectification, exposed high-voltage terminals, a 400 W amplifier, and
high-current supplies are outside unsupervised study. Entering a professional
lab does not itself make them safe; follow the site SOP, probe category,
grounding rules, and the responsible supervisor’s decision.

## Start the Seven-Week Project from Measurable Specifications

The official Projects page asks a 3-person team to define an idea as a product, state minimum
specifications first, and compare performance, usability, part count, and
cost. Digital logic and single-chip function solutions are constrained because
the subject is analog design with op-amps, FET switches, current mirrors, and
discrete devices. The historical allowance of about $100 per team is not a
2026 procurement budget.

A low-energy independent project might be a band-limited audio preamplifier,
light-measurement front end, or low-voltage function generator. In week 1,
define gain, bandwidth, noise, distortion, load, power, and stop conditions.
Test the least certain subcircuits separately in weeks 2–3; integrate and sweep
component spread, supply, and temperature in weeks 4–5; have another person
repeat the written test in week 6; organize the notebook, raw data, debug log,
and demonstration in week 7. That timing is an EEDIY proposal, not new MIT
coursework. At the end, explain how every specification was measured, which
discrepancy changed the design, and which original experiments were omitted
because of equipment or safety limits.
