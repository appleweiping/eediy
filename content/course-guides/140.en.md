## Keep “Biomedical” Inside the Supplied Data

MIT HST.582J is a Spring 2007 graduate course in Biomedical Signal and Image
Processing. The official
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/hst-582j-biomedical-signal-and-image-processing-spring-2007/pages/syllabus/)
places digital signal processing in cardiology, speech, and medical-imaging
contexts, with five MATLAB laboratory projects at the center. Lab 1 really
does ask students to filter ECG and construct a ventricular-arrhythmia
detector; Labs 3 and 4 use clinical brain MRI and medical-image registration.
Those phrases describe 2007 teaching exercises. They do not authorize
diagnosis or establish clinical performance.

This guide recommends offline computation only: use the packages already
provided by OCW, attach no electrodes, recruit no volunteers, process no new
patient records, and do not present an assignment score as clinical
sensitivity or specificity. The course data can show whether an algorithm
behaves on the assigned inputs; it cannot establish safe operation in a
hospital, monitor, or target population. Human measurement, identifiable
health data, or clinical decisions require institutional ethics, privacy,
medical, and professional oversight outside this public-study route.

## Translate the Course Numbers into Mathematics the Labs Actually Use

The named prerequisites are 6.003 Signals and Systems, 2.004 Dynamics and
Control II, 16.004 Unified Engineering IV, or 18.085 Computational Science and
Engineering I. Beyond the course numbers, work through the paper calculations
that Labs 1 and 3 will call: explain sampling and aliasing; classify a
difference equation as FIR or IIR and assess stability; explain DFT leakage
and windows; derive a two-class decision boundary with Bayes’ rule; and
compute covariance/eigenvectors for a small 2-D set and interpret PCA. Review
signals/DSP if the first three are weak. Fill probability
and linear-algebra gaps before classification, segmentation, and blind source
separation if the last two are weak.

The
[lecture-notes page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/hst-582j-biomedical-signal-and-image-processing-spring-2007/pages/lecture-notes/)
covers acquisition, filtering, ECG, DTFT/DFT, sampling, speech, 2-D image
processing, PDFs/classification, density estimation, segmentation and
registration, random signals, and blind source separation. Not all 24 lecture
positions include notes: MRI, surgical applications, random signals III, and
the wrap-up have no corresponding public notes. Some early chapters derive
from 1999–2001 materials. This is a dated archive, not a 2026 standard for
medical-image or physiological-signal practice.

## Reorder the Twenty-Four Lectures Around the Labs

The
[calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/hst-582j-biomedical-signal-and-image-processing-spring-2007/pages/calendar/)
interleaves lectures with a four-hour weekly laboratory. Independent study is
clearer when the
[labs page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/hst-582j-biomedical-signal-and-image-processing-spring-2007/pages/labs/)
defines the inputs and outputs:

1. **Optional Lab 0, one week:** use the MATLAB tutorial and self-test for
   arrays, plotting, scripts, and file loading.
2. **Lab 1, ECG, three weeks:** pair Lectures 1–6 on acquisition, filtering,
   DTFT/DFT, and sampling with 12 `.txt`, 11 `.mat`, and 1 `.m` file.
3. **Lab 2, speech coding, three weeks:** pair Lectures 7–8 with source-filter
   analysis/synthesis, pitch detection, vocoding, and linear prediction; the
   package contains 2 `.mat` and 13 `.m` files.
4. **Lab 3, MRI segmentation, two weeks:** pair Lectures 9–14 with noise
   reduction, tissue labeling, contours, and 3-D visualization; the package
   contains supporting data, 9 `.m` files, and 2 `.gz` files.
5. **Lab 4, registration, two weeks:** pair Lectures 15–17 with optimization
   of a 2-D slice-registration objective; the package contains 1 `.mat` and
   22 `.m` files.
6. **Lab 5, blind source separation, two weeks:** pair Lectures 18–20 with
   Wiener filtering, PCA, ICA, and higher-order methods for maternal/fetal
   ECG; the package contains 10 `.dat` and 7 `.m` files.

The course grades five numbered Problem Sets 1–5. The
[assignments page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/hst-582j-biomedical-signal-and-image-processing-spring-2007/pages/assignments/)
also lists supplemental Sets X and Y, while the syllabus counts only five
sets. Two quizzes existed, but OCW does not expose their papers or answers.
The problem-set page likewise gives no separate solutions for PS1–PS5. Treat
that as a feedback gap; “the questions download” is not the same as “complete
solutions are available.”

## Five Reports Matter More Than Five Notebooks That Run

The official [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/hst-582j-biomedical-signal-and-image-processing-spring-2007/pages/syllabus/)
made lab reports 60% of the original grade, compared with 10% for problem
sets. An independent submission therefore needs more than executed starter
code. Each report needs a result that can be recalculated. Lab 1 should name
the ECG records used to tune a detector threshold and those used to check it,
then show pre/post-filter spectra and counts at the fixed threshold. Lab 2
should connect codec parameters to reconstructed waveforms, spectrograms, and
audible artifacts. Lab 3 should give tissue-label rules, region counts, and
overlays on fixed MRI slices. Lab 4 should report the registration transform,
objective trace, and overlays from different initializations. Lab 5 should
place mixture and separated traces together and explain scale, sign, and
permutation ambiguity.

Repeatedly tuning a threshold on the same records does not produce a
generalization estimate; when data are split, identify the parameter-selection
and final-check groups. A lower registration objective does not by itself mean
anatomical alignment. A BSS component should not be called “maternal” or
“fetal” without discussing ambiguity. These reports answer signal-processing
questions on supplied teaching data, not medical-device validation.

## A MATLAB Migration Must Preserve Data Meaning

The 2007 course ran MATLAB on an MIT Server in an electronic classroom.
Outside readers receive instructions, starter `.m` files, and data, but not
that server, its staff, or the supervised lab. Commercial MATLAB may require a
license. Python/NumPy/SciPy or GNU Octave can reduce software cost; either is
an EEDIY migration rather than the official supported environment.

Preserve the archive and third-party attribution before migration. If files
are renamed, preserve subject and condition identifiers in a manifest. Do not
redistribute permission-based medical images without checking their terms.
Then verify array orientation, MATLAB’s 1-based indexing, filter initial
state, FFT normalization, image-coordinate convention, optimizer tolerance,
and random seed. Old `.mat` files, compressed images, and custom `.m`
functions may require different adapters. Successfully opening a file does
not prove numerical equivalence.

## Keep the Five Numerical Interpretations in Their Own Reports

Archive the five numbered problem-set attempts separately. Keep each Lab 1–5
codebase with its own report, and place data attribution and license notes
with the lab that uses them. Lab 0 remains optional MATLAB practice and is not a fifth
formal experiment. Across the five reports, compare the stationarity or
independence assumptions in ECG/BSS, the Gaussian assumptions in segmentation,
and registration sensitivity to interpolation and initialization. Those
cannot be collapsed into one “overall accuracy.”

The course remains five different interpretations of ECG, speech,
segmentation, registration, and BSS rather than one modern deep classifier.
No acquisition hardware is needed. “Realism” is not a reason to connect a
person or use any teaching-data output for diagnosis.
