## This Course Works through Written Derivation, Not Video

Cornell
[ECE 3150 Microelectronics](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3150-microelectronics/)
is a Spring 2016 junior-level course. It begins with carriers,
drift-diffusion, recombination, and PN junctions, then reaches MOS capacitors,
MOSFETs/BJTs, single- and multi-stage amplifiers, differential pairs,
high-frequency models, feedback, RF mixers, CMOS, subthreshold operation,
FinFETs, and quantum transport. The site names no required text and supplies no
lecture video. Its unusual value is continuity in writing: 27 derivation-heavy
handouts, 12 homework sets with a solution for each, a midterm and final with
solutions, and laboratory handouts.

The official page says junior undergraduate but publishes no chain of
prerequisite course numbers. An EEDIY editorial self-check is to explain drift
and diffusion from continuity, draw bands and carriers in forward- and
reverse-biased PN junctions, classify NMOS/PMOS regions, form a small-signal
model from a bias point, and read complex gain, poles, and phase margin. This
is not a Cornell enrollment rule, and there is no universal pass count. Each
unsuccessful attempt points only to a specific review—charge conservation,
basic circuits, device equations, or frequency analysis. After review, attempt
the first worked problem in the relevant handout instead of optimizing a
separate “prerequisite test.”
These checks interact. Correct node equations cannot rescue an unjustified
transistor region; a correct DC point with confused phase instead suggests a
frequency-analysis gap. Locate the broken inference, then address that
prerequisite rather than relearning an entire foundations course.

## Connect Handout, Homework, and Lab

The official [Lecture Notes and Handouts](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3150-microelectronics/ece-3150-lecture-notes-and-handouts/)
run from 1–27. [Homework and Exams](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3150-microelectronics/ece-3150-homework-and-exams/)
publishes all 12 homework sets, the midterm, the final, and every solution. The
table is an EEDIY grouping by topic span, not a Cornell calendar; no claim is
made that a homework set belongs to only one handout.

| Reading segment | Principal model | Written work immediately after it | Laboratory interface | Pause before the next segment to check |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Handouts 1–10 | Semiconductor statistics, transport, recombination, PN junctions, LEDs/solar cells, MOS capacitors, and MOSFET large/small signal | Attempt matching topics in HW 1–5 before the corresponding solution | Lab 1: diode IV, small-signal parameters, exponential amplifier, and FFT | Can parameters be inferred from data, and can you locate where series resistance, temperature, or high injection breaks the exponential model? |
| Handouts 11–18 | CS/CG/CD, active loads, current mirrors, cascodes, multistage and differential amplifiers | Attempt HW 6–10 and label every device operating region | Lab 2: CD4007C NFET/PFET and CMOS inverter; Lab 3: ALD1105 cascade, mirror, and cascode | Do DC bias, small-signal gain, and input/output resistance follow from the same parameter set? |
| Handouts 19–27 | Miller effect, frequency response, stability/compensation, folded cascodes, mixers/converters, CMOS, subthreshold, and FinFETs | Complete HW 11–12, then sample the midterm/final closed-book | Lab 4: open-ended work, although the public brief calls it optional bonus | Are the sources of high-frequency poles and approximations explicit, and where does the model stop being credible in voltage, frequency, or scale? |

These pause points are an editorial inference from the topic transitions across
27 handouts, not survey evidence that “learners usually fail here.” Solutions
come last: preserve the first attempt, classify corrections as model choice,
sign, algebra, or unit, and repeat only missed work several days later.
Recognizing a solution is not building the approximation from a blank page.
Keep an assumption ledger for each segment: thermal-equilibrium nodes, devices
neglecting channel-length modulation, and capacitors treated as open or short
at the stated frequency. Recheck it before carrying a result forward; the
device-to-amplifier transition should test old approximations, not merely
replace formulas.

## The Published Labs Are Asymmetric

The official [Projects and Labs](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3150-microelectronics/ece-3150-projects-and-labs/)
page lists Lab 1–4. The Lab 1 diode-IV sweep uses 1 Keithley 2400 SMU and
Labtracer 2.0; the handout also says that each bench has 2 SMUs. It acquires
1N914A IV, infers large- and small-signal parameters, then
compares exponential-amplifier harmonics with a function generator,
oscilloscope FFT, and MATLAB `besseli`. Lab 2 uses 2 SMUs to measure NFETs,
PFETs, and a CMOS transfer curve in the CD4007C. Lab 3 uses ALD1105 devices for
a common-source/common-drain cascade, a current mirror, and a cascode,
measuring DC bias and voltage gain at 1 kHz small signal. Its \(R_L\) sweep
characterizes loaded gain/output behavior. The lab goals mention input/output
resistance, but the public handout does not provide a complete standalone
input-resistance measurement procedure.

Keep the version seam visible. The course home says Spring 2016, while the
public Lab 1 PDF says Spring 2015; the other lab material is from Spring 2016.
Labs 1–3 have solutions and Lab 4 does not. The home description also mentions
4 labs and 1 relatively open design project, but the published
[Lab 4 brief](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/files/2017/03/ece3150_lab4-1cyoaq6.pdf)
explicitly calls the work optional bonus. It allows at most 2 people, requires
instructor approval of a schematic and proposed measurements, ends in an
in-person demo, requires no write-up, and warns that TA support is limited.
The public index exposes no separate project brief, Lab 4 solution, sample
grading, or instructor feedback. A fully published mandatory project should
not be inferred.

## Map Official Instruments to Modern Substitutes and Errors

With only a laptop, one can practice models, parameter-extraction algorithms,
and circuit analysis from the handouts; one cannot call that “rebuilding Labs
1–3.” As of 2026-07-29, the public index and linked lab files expose neither a
downloadable raw IV dataset nor the original manufacturer SPICE model package.
Without personal measurement data, use only a versioned datasheet curve or a
clearly labelled synthetic model to demonstrate the method. Such results
cannot be compared numerically with Cornell’s original measurements.

| Original laboratory object | Modern treatment | New error or difference that must remain visible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Keithley 2400 SMU + Labtracer 2.0 single sweep (2 SMUs are installed per bench) | A current SMU, or a current-limited source + precision shunt + DMM/DAQ | Compliance, burden voltage, lead resistance, current floor, NPLC/integration time, and acquisition order |
| 2 synchronized SMUs controlling gate/drain or source/sense | 2 coordinated source-measure channels; if swept sequentially, record settling time and temperature | Unsynchronized gate/drain bias, self-heating, hysteresis, and leakage can alter IV |
| 50 Ω function generator with a specified DC offset | An AWG with declared source/load termination; verify actual offset and amplitude with a DMM | Switching between high impedance and 50 Ω can create a 2× amplitude error; offset and clipping alter bias |
| Oscilloscope FFT + Tek OpenChoice export | Export a time trace from a current DSO and process it with a version-pinned FFT script | Window (Lab 1 specifies Flattop), record length, sample rate, noise floor, averaging, and amplitude calibration |
| MATLAB `besseli(n,a)` | Record the MATLAB version, or pin a version using `scipy.special.iv(n,a)` | Library version, dtype, overflow/scaling convention, and dB normalization |
| Physical CD4007C, ALD1105, and 1N914A | Record lot/package for genuinely matching marked parts; otherwise use only a versioned datasheet or synthetic model | Threshold, \(\lambda\), matching, body tie, temperature, package parasitics, and layout may all differ |

Every netlist must record the model file, download or transcription source,
version/date, exact parameters, and local edits. “Generic NMOS” is insufficient
for another person to recompute the result. If a manufacturer model becomes
available later, retain the old synthetic run rather than silently replacing
the model while preserving its plots.
Separate acquired from derived data. Keep captures read-only; script cleaning,
unit conversion, smoothing, and fitting so every plotted trace points to an
input and commit. Never call a smoothed trace raw. For datasheet digitization,
record the page, axes, and extraction method.

## Practice the Methods of Labs 1, 2, and 3 Separately

The point of Lab 1 is not a smooth diode curve. Fit \(I_S\) and ideality factor
from IV, inspect error on a held-out region, then obtain differential
resistance at the bias point. If the data came from datasheet digitization or
a synthetic model, mark both the plot title and CSV; the report may say only
that the extraction method ran. For harmonics, preserve the time trace, FFT
window, sample rate, and amplitude of each order, then explain normalization
between the Bessel prediction and numerical FFT.

Lab 2 should connect \(I_D\!-\!V_{GS}\), families of
\(I_D\!-\!V_{DS}\), \(g_m\), \(g_o\), and the inverter transfer curve. One
parameter set must face all those curves; do not retune the model for every
plot. If NFET, PFET, and inverter are simulated in sequence, place the bulk
connection, sign convention, and sweep direction beside the netlist.

The public Lab 3 handout measures DC node voltages and 1 kHz small-signal gain.
Its \(R_L\) sweep directly shows loaded gain versus load and supports discussion
of output behavior. Input resistance needs a separate excitation and
current/voltage-drop measurement, for which the public handout does not give a
complete procedure; it cannot be claimed from the same \(R_L\) data. The
handout also does not require a full frequency-response sweep. EEDIY may add an
AC sweep, pole, and bandwidth study after checking the DC regions and 1 kHz
result, but it must be labelled directly as an “EEDIY extension.” Do not write
the extension back into Cornell’s original Lab 3 objectives.
Do not force theory/simulation/measurement columns to appear complete. With no
bench data, mark measurement absent. Theory and simulation using the same
synthetic parameters can check equations and implementation, but cannot
validate a real device. Discuss instrument error, part spread, and temperature
drift only after measurements exist.

## When ECE 3150 Is the Right Choice

This course suits someone willing to move repeatedly among device equations,
transistor stages, and measurement. It is a poor first encounter for someone
who depends on video. A compliant-laboratory route uses isolated low voltage,
current limiting, power-off rewiring, and an instrument-competent supervisor
for first power-up. A software-only route concentrates on model and algorithm
while explicitly lacking actual devices, instrument noise, and layout effects.
Completion is not page count. Given an unfamiliar curve, explain the physical
model, parameter source, validation subset, residual structure, and next
measurement that separates competing causes. If that chain is unclear, adding
a more elaborate amplifier will not compensate for weak device understanding.

A concluding EEDIY technical record can join a diode/MOS parameter extraction with a
cascode or differential stage: include the source of measured or synthetic
data, derivation, netlist with an exact model-file hash, parameter script, DC/AC/transient results,
PVT sensitivity, residuals, and model failure conditions. This is an EEDIY
integration exercise, not a new Cornell project. The scarce feature of ECE
3150 is not its number of topics; every device approximation must bear a
consequence again in homework, an exam, or a lab.
