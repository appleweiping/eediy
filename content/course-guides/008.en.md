## Course fit

MIT 6.436J/15.085J Fall 2018 is a graduate probability course, not an extended
18.05. Its official
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-436j-fundamentals-of-probability-fall-2018/pages/syllabus/)
starts from probability measures, measurability, and abstract integration,
then reaches conditional expectation, modes of convergence, LLN/CLT, and
Markov processes. It is excellent preparation for stochastic processes,
communications, information theory, or statistical-learning theory. If sets,
quantifiers, sup/inf, and short proofs from definitions are still
painful, rigorous introductory probability and real analysis should come
first.

## Read the Notes as Definition–Hypothesis–Counterexample

The [lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-436j-fundamentals-of-probability-fall-2018/pages/lecture-notes/)
are the public textbook. For each lecture, record the quantifiers in each
definition, the hypotheses of each theorem, and a counterexample. Name the
sigma-algebra for a conditional expectation, and put almost-sure, in
probability, in distribution, and \(L^p\) convergence on an implication map.
This keeps the notation from hiding the logic.

The [assignment page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-436j-fundamentals-of-probability-fall-2018/pages/assignments/)
has 12 sets, with Set 12 optional, but OCW does not publish the classroom
solutions. Check normalization and boundary cases in calculations and audit
every invoked theorem in proofs. Ideally, ask someone who knows probability
to inspect the logic without supplying an answer. Explicitly retaining an
unresolved problem is more useful than mixing in an unattributed solution.

## The Exams Test Proof Density

The [exam archive](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-436j-fundamentals-of-probability-fall-2018/pages/exams/)
publishes the Fall 2018 midterm and final, also without solutions. After a
closed-book attempt, compress the longest proof into a half-page lemma chain
and rebuild it from blank paper several days later. If you cannot name the
conditions behind an interchange of limit and integral, or the probability
space for an almost-sure claim, the central standard of the course has not yet
been met.
