Choose Cornell for breadth or 6.701 for the quantum-transport transition, borrowing only selected phonon or transport chapters from the other.

The courses may share a concept index, but not an answer chain. Cornell solutions can check band, statistics, and scattering derivations; the 6.701 text carries a state-count argument into terminal current. ## DOS, occupation, and transmission separately control states, carriers, and current

Wavefunctions, eigenvalues, lattices, and thermal statistics from [physics](../physics/index.md); linear algebra, differential equations, and Fourier representations from [engineering mathematics](../mathematics/index.md); and terminal voltage, charge, current, and small-signal quantities from [circuit analysis](../circuits/index.md) play different roles. The formal 6.701 prerequisite is 6.007 or 6.003, so field and energy modeling from [EE introduction](../ee-introduction/index.md) or system representation from [signals and systems](../signals-systems/index.md) should already be usable.

Normalize a one-dimensional infinite-well state, derive the energy dependence of 1D, 2D, and 3D density of states, and interpret effective mass from the curvature of \(E(k)\). DOS combines with Fermi occupation to produce carrier density. Current additionally requires contact chemical potentials, scattering, or transmission. State units, degeneracy, temperature, and boundaries at every step. Similar-looking curves do not make available-state density, occupied population, and conductance interchangeable.

Plot the same parabolic band on three distinct vertical axes: states per energy, integrated carrier concentration, and current for stated contacts and transmission. When temperature or chemical potential changes, identify separately what changed in the DOS, occupation window, and terminal current. The separation checks units and locates each parameter in band structure, statistics, or transport.

## Check the state count of a one-dimensional chain before attaching Landauer current

Implement a finite-difference quantum well or nearest-neighbor tight-binding chain. Under closed boundaries, solve eigenvalues and eigenvectors and compare energies, normalization, and orthogonality with an analytic infinite well or known dispersion. Construct the DOS and integrate it to verify the state count. For transport, attach two contacts, calculate transmission and Landauer current, and test zero net current at zero bias together with symmetry under a left-right swap.

Compare at least three spatial grids or chain lengths. Before changing well width, temperature, or chemical potential, predict the direction from bands and occupation. Raw eigenvalue, wavefunction, DOS, transmission, and current arrays should regenerate the figures. When a nearby boundary, coarse energy grid, or incorrect contact coupling disturbs the result, identify whether state count, convergence, or current conservation changes first. The final I-V curve remains traceable through Hamiltonian, boundaries, DOS, and occupation.

An energy-grid check compares integrated DOS and current integrals, not only visual smoothness; a spatial-grid check compares low eigenvalues and wavefunction nodes. Align spectral broadening, transmission peaks, and the current window on one energy axis. Equal contact chemical potentials must cancel forward and reverse contributions, while a left-right swap tests sign conventions and self-energy wiring.

## MATLAB prompts remain model-level, and a cleanroom is not an outside extension

ECE 4070's public strength is its handout-assignment-exam chain; it has no video, laboratory, or code. 6.701 describes MATLAB simulation tasks but publishes no runnable official implementation. A learner-written solver states the spatial grid, Hamiltonian discretization, boundary conditions, contact or self-energy model, broadening, and convergence tolerance and separates new source from course material. Historical process scales, material parameters, and device cases are not current industry-node data.

Public access does not authorize cleanroom processing, chemical etching, implantation, vacuum work, high-field breakdown, or nanofabrication. These activities require chemical, radiation, voltage, and institutional controls. In the absence of a device sample, probe station, calibration, and raw I-V or C-V observations, a curve is a numerical model result. A strong semiconductor project need not imply fabrication; it makes state count, current conservation, numerical convergence, and identifiable course material agree.

Use model, simulation, or public-data comparison in titles and captions, and identify whether parameters came from a course example, literature, or a chosen sweep. That provenance makes the numerical result reproducible while leaving fabrication, contact resistance, and measurement uncertainty visibly outside scope.
