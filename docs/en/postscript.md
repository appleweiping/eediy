# Editorial Notes

EEDIY began with an ordinary frustration: electrical-engineering course links are plentiful, yet an independent learner may discover only after starting that solutions are missing, laboratories depend on campus instruments, the software license is unavailable, or two courses with similar titles serve different goals.

[CSDIY](https://csdiy.wiki/) already offers a useful reading model: browse by field, give each course its own guide, link the material directly, and let later learners add experience below. EEDIY keeps that structure and concentrates on the decisions particular to EE—whether devices and instruments are obtainable, whether the EDA environment is accessible, and how far an experiment can proceed safely.

## What this edition can honestly establish

The current course guides are based mainly on inspection of public material: course sites, syllabi, assignments, laboratories, examinations, code, and provider documentation. Those sources can establish what is public and what is missing. They cannot establish actual study time, the hardest migration step, or which explanation helped a learner through a particular obstacle.

As of July 31, 2026, one public, attributable account meets the site's `learner-reviewed` threshold: Steven J. Frank's completion of the first MITx 6.002x run in 2012. It concerns another run of the same course and documents the complete online-course experience and virtual-lab friction; it does not establish completion of the Spring 2007 physical labs on this page. The 6.111 guide also cites Andi Q. ’25's completion of MIT 6.2050 in Fall 2023. Because 6.2050 is a successor course, that account supplies context about FPGA system integration rather than completion evidence for 6.111; its 2023 labs cannot stand in for the Spring 2006 labs. Both accounts were found in published articles rather than submitted to EEDIY.

Only the 6.002 guide is therefore labeled `learner-reviewed`. The 6.111 guide and the other 139 courses remain public-material guides or catalogue entries; neither a successor course nor another run is allowed to endorse a specific offering silently. First-hand completion evidence is still the largest gap in this edition.

## The most useful additions

A learner report need not be an essay. State the units or assignments completed, environment and hardware used, actual effort, one concrete obstacle, and any code, report, or measurement record that can be inspected publicly. That is enough to add evidence a website review cannot supply.

An installation fix, a material change, or an explanation of why one measurement failed is useful too. Leave a short note below the relevant page; use the [contribution entry point](contributing.md) when an attributed report or evidence-backed change belongs in the article. EEDIY grants no credit or professional qualification and does not replace laboratory training. Its narrower job is to make the entry point, tradeoff, and attainable scope of a course clear.
