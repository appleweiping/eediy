# Licensing and Attribution

EEDIY treats site code, original editorial content, and third-party resources as separate layers. A link to freely accessible material does not place that material under this project’s license.

## License layers

| Material | Default license | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Configuration, scripts, styles, and site code | MIT License | Use, modify, and distribute while preserving copyright and license text |
| Original guides, selection notes, and route prose | [CC BY 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/) | Share and adapt with attribution, license link, and change notice |
| EEDIY name and brand mark | Project identifier | The licenses do not grant trademark rights or allow an unofficial derivative to appear official |
| Third-party courses, video, books, papers, code, data, and marks | Their owners’ terms | This project normally provides links and original summaries, not relicensing |
| Third-party excerpts in contributions | Source license and applicable quotation limits | State source, scope, and license; omit when permission is unclear |

## Citing this site

Include page title, contributors, license, page link, access date, and changes:

> EEDIY Contributors, “Page title,” CC BY 4.0, accessed YYYY-MM-DD, page link. Changes: translated and condensed.

When citing a course fact, also cite the official source. An EEDIY verification record says what contributors observed on a particular date; the official page remains authoritative for course identity, policy, and materials.

## What is and is not permitted

### Permitted

- Adapt an original route in a syllabus or article with CC BY 4.0 attribution.
- Reuse site code while preserving the MIT notice.
- Link to a specific page and explain where you adopt or reject its judgment.
- Contribute translations, corrections, and short properly sourced excerpts.

### Not permitted

- Copy an entire linked course, textbook, video collection, or solution set into this repository.
- Remove third-party copyright, license, authorship, or access limits.
- Present an EEDIY link as relicensing third-party material.
- Use the name, mark, and visual appearance to mislead readers that a derivative is official.
- Upload personal data, secrets, controlled technical information, or material that violates academic-integrity rules.

## Broken links and rights requests

For a broken link, misattribution, incompatible license, or material that should not appear, provide:

1. the exact page and passage;
2. your relationship to the material, when relevant;
3. original rights or license evidence;
4. the requested correction, replacement, or removal;
5. safety or legal urgency.

Maintainers should prevent further distribution of disputed material when appropriate, retain the minimum audit record, inspect the source, and document the resolution. A good-faith rights request does not require the claimant to write replacement copy.
