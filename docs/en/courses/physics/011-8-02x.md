## Which of the nine experiments can be migrated

### The low-voltage measurement work remains useful

The 9 experiments on the [Labs](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02x-physics-ii-electricity-magnetism-with-an-experimental-focus-spring-2005/pages/labs/) page are Meter Introduction, Low Voltage Power Supply, High Voltage Power Supply, Electrostatic Force, Voltage and Current, Electrical Breakdown, Magnetic Force, Amplifier, and Microwave. For an individual learner, the most transferable ideas come from meter use, low-voltage supply behavior, current-voltage measurement, and amplification: how range changes a reading, how input resistance disturbs a circuit, and how a schematic becomes a table with units and uncertainty.

Even the low-voltage portion should not copy the 2005 practice of beginning with a mains transformer. At home or in an ordinary makerspace, use a certified, enclosed, isolated, current-limited low-voltage adapter and never open the mains side. A magnetic-force activity should be redesigned for isolated, current-limited low voltage, with moving magnets and wires secured and power disconnected before adjustment. These are EEDIY restrictions for independent environments, not substitutes for MIT laboratory credit.

### The high-voltage experiments are not home reproductions

The old High Voltage Power Supply manual describes a device around 100–1000 V, and another safety document mentions operation up to about 1200 V. Its historical assurance based on low current, including language about contacting the output, is not modern permission to operate it. Do not build, touch, or demonstrate that supply. Electrostatic Force and Electrical Breakdown also depend on the high-voltage source and spark gap, so omit them from the independent physical route. Study the circuit and derive quantities on paper, but do not call a simulation or reading exercise an executed experiment.

Nominal current is not the only issue. High voltage charges parasitic capacitance, while wiring mistakes, damaged insulation, underrated probes, and unexpected discharge change the hazard. Without a qualified person physically responsible for the work, a controlled bench, suitable barriers and interlocks, a discharge procedure, and correctly rated instruments, there is no reasonable path to faithful reproduction.

### Microwave combines high voltage with radio compliance

The 2005 Microwave experiment reuses the spark gap and high-voltage supply. Its manual gives a typical setting around 800 V and observes damped bursts centered around 2.4 GHz, with energy possible at other frequencies. This is not an ordinary low-power Wi-Fi exercise: it intentionally creates broadband spark radiation while also presenting a high-voltage hazard. Omit it unless it is conducted within the supervision, shielding, and authorization of a qualified institution; this guide intentionally provides no home construction procedure.

Radio rules depend on jurisdiction and equipment status. A learner in the United States should at minimum consult the current [47 CFR Part 15](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-15); elsewhere, consult the local regulator. A United States citation does not establish legality in another country, and the historical availability of a manual does not authorize radiating electromagnetic energy today.

## The laboratory notebook is the course’s most valuable surviving output

The original course required notebooks and staff checkoffs, so the central evidence for the independent route should also be an auditable record. For every safe low-voltage experiment actually performed, retain the date and instrument models, schematic, measured component values, ranges, raw data table, units and uncertainty, processing equations, plots, anomalies, and failed attempts. Photographs show that something was assembled but do not replace measurements. For omitted high-voltage or radio activities, submit a risk analysis, theoretical derivation, and explicit statement of non-execution—never invented data.

For theory, preserve first attempts and corrections for PS1–PS12 and take the 4 Quizzes at the four stage boundaries. Because PS6 and PS12 lack public solutions, use dimensions, limiting cases, independent derivations, and numerical estimates as cross-checks, but do not claim official grading. The 10 unavailable Mastering Physics/Cybertutor tasks also cannot be silently replaced with new questions; extra practice is welcome only when labeled as such.

## An order that does not impersonate the campus laboratory

Following the 30 older notes and 9 manuals presented on the official Lecture Notes and Labs pages, study electrostatics, potential, circuits, magnetism, induction, and waves while using the 2005 PS1–PS12 and 4 Quizzes to set the pace. Read all the manuals to understand the intended experimental narrative, but physically perform only redesigned meter, low-voltage current-voltage, and selected amplifier or magnetic-force measurements. High Voltage Power Supply, Electrostatic Force, Electrical Breakdown, and Microwave default to paper analysis. Before every retained experiment, predict the order of magnitude and curve shape; afterward, explain discrepancies rather than merely seeking a plausible-looking plot.

This route loses Red Box consistency, partner work, 9 in-person checkoffs, staff notebook comments, and all 10 online assignments. It is therefore a safety-restricted self-study path based on public material, not distance-equivalent MIT 8.02X credit.
