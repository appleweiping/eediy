### Electromagnetism with Measurement at Its Center

[MIT 8.02X Spring 2005](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02x-physics-ii-electricity-magnetism-with-an-experimental-focus-spring-2005/)
is not an ordinary video course. Campus students completed 9 experiments over
12 weeks, with theory, instrument readings, and laboratory notebooks assessed
together. It suits learners who want to connect fields and circuits to actual
measurement; 8.02SC is a more direct choice for a conventional theory course.
OCW supplies neither the teaching boxes, live supervision, nor public lecture
videos, so transfer the experimental method rather than copying 2005 hardware.

The official
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02x-physics-ii-electricity-magnetism-with-an-experimental-focus-spring-2005/pages/syllabus/)
lists 12 problem sets, 9 experiments, and 4 quizzes. The course moves from
electrostatic fields, potential, and capacitance through circuits, magnetic
fields, induction, Maxwell's equations, and waves. Before starting, be able to
decompose vectors, perform elementary integrals, check units, and explain the
direction of a force or field. Pure formula substitution fails quickly when a
measurement needs interpretation.

### Pair Problems with Experiments

The [assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02x-physics-ii-electricity-magnetism-with-an-experimental-focus-spring-2005/pages/assignments/)
provide PS1–PS12; PS6 and PS12 lack solutions. The
[exam archive](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02x-physics-ii-electricity-magnetism-with-an-experimental-focus-spring-2005/pages/exams/)
preserves all 4 quizzes, solutions, and review material. Attempt each item
before checking it with a solution, dimensions, and limiting cases.

The [labs](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-02x-physics-ii-electricity-magnetism-with-an-experimental-focus-spring-2005/pages/labs/)
cover meters, low- and high-voltage supplies, electrostatic force, voltage and
current, breakdown, magnetic force, amplification, and microwave work. On a
personal bench, the useful subset is meter practice, isolated current-limited
low-voltage measurements, and amplification. Record range, meter impedance,
measured component values, raw data, and uncertainty, and observe how the
instrument changes the circuit.

### Omit High Voltage and Spark RF

The historical high-voltage, breakdown, and spark-gap microwave experiments
do not belong on a personal bench. Do not recreate their supplies from a
mains transformer. Personal work should use an enclosed, isolated,
current-limited low-voltage supply; high voltage, breakdown, and radiation
belong in an authorized, shielded, supervised institution.

### Finish with a Trustworthy Notebook

For a safe low-voltage experiment, preserve the schematic, instrument
ranges, raw data, processing equations, anomalies, and failed attempts.
Separate direct readings from model-derived values, and label high-voltage or
RF analysis as unperformed when it remains theoretical. The result should
connect charge and fields to electromagnetic waves while revealing whether a
discrepancy came from the instrument, wiring, component tolerance, or model.

For every inferred value, identify the readings and assumptions behind it.
Instrument resolution, range, and loading belong to measurement conditions;
fitting and theoretical equations belong to the model. Keeping those apart
makes a discrepancy actionable: change range, inspect wiring, or revise the
field-and-circuit model.
