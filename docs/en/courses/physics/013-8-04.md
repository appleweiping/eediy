### Course fit

MIT 8.04 Spring 2016 starts with wavefunctions, probability, and the
Schrödinger equation; proceeds through potentials in a single spatial dimension, the
harmonic oscillator, tunneling, and scattering; and finishes with angular
momentum, central potentials, and hydrogen. The official
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-04-quantum-physics-i-spring-2016/pages/syllabus/)
requires at least a C in 8.03. You should already know oscillations and waves,
Fourier representations, complex exponentials, and linear differential
equations. Choose it for physics foundations before semiconductor, photonic,
or quantum devices—not for a conceptual-only tour of “quantum.”

### Rebuild the Argument after Every Video

The 24 [video lectures](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-04-quantum-physics-i-spring-2016/pages/video-lectures/)
are divided into short topics. After each segment, close the recording and
reconstruct the result from the Hamiltonian, boundary conditions, and
normalization. Given a candidate wavefunction, be able to check probability
current, expectation values, and limiting behavior. Commercial textbooks add
exercise variety, but the public videos and notes carry the central sequence.

The [assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-04-quantum-physics-i-spring-2016/pages/assignments/)
contain 10 weekly sets, but that edition does not link solutions. For answer
feedback, use the separate [Spring 2013 archive of 10 solved problem sets](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-04-quantum-physics-i-spring-2013/pages/assignments/)
on the same foundations. Those are a historical parallel edition, not answers
to the 2016 assignments. Still triangulate with an analytic derivation, an
independent finite-difference calculation, and a known limit. A numerical
picture may clarify tunneling or a wavepacket, but it cannot replace
derivations of the spectrum and continuity conditions.

### A One-Dimensional Hamiltonian Is the Right Finish

The [exam archive](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/8-04-quantum-physics-i-spring-2016/pages/exams/)
provides course and practice material but no complete answer chain, so it
supports closed-book checks rather than full correction feedback. Then
discretize a single-coordinate Hamiltonian and compare low eigenvalues for an
infinite well, finite well, and harmonic oscillator. State the grid, boundary
condition, and convergence test, and validate against an analytic case first.
If you can explain the state, spectrum, time evolution, and observables from a
given Hamiltonian, 8.04 has become reusable preparation for device physics.
State how error changes under grid refinement; an apparently stable
eigenvalue may otherwise be only a discretization accident.
