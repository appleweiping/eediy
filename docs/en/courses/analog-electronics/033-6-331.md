### An Exercise Bank for a Mature Analog Designer

The official [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-331-advanced-circuit-techniques-spring-2002/pages/syllabus/)
for MIT 6.331 Spring 2002 requires 6.301 and 6.302 and says “no equivalents.”
The course assumes you can already analyze a transistor-level amplifier, loop
gain, stability, noise and offset, and transients before reaching
sample-and-hold circuits, ADCs and DACs, references, oscillators, PLLs, and
power converters. It is not a shortcut if small-signal models, Bode/Nyquist
reasoning, and bench measurements do not yet agree.

### Access and version notes

The [calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-331-advanced-circuit-techniques-spring-2002/pages/calendar/)
gives the topic order, but OCW has no lecture notes, lecture videos, or exams.
Public access also omits a uniform solution set and instructor feedback.
The [assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-331-advanced-circuit-techniques-spring-2002/pages/assignments/)
publish 6 problem sets and 3 design problems. A design was expected to be
detailed enough for a technician to build: named parts and values, tolerance,
bias, interfaces, worst cases, and a test method—not merely a block diagram.

For each design, map every requirement to a calculation, simulation, and bench
test. For an ADC or DAC, allocate an error budget across quantization,
reference, offset and gain, linearity, settling, noise, and clock jitter, then
choose the architecture from that budget rather than beginning with a familiar
part.

### Labs and projects

The [labs](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-331-advanced-circuit-techniques-spring-2002/pages/labs/)
publish 3 experiments. A modern migration must recheck supply, logic levels,
reference range, input protection, timing, and pinout and remain at isolated
low voltage with controlled stored energy. Debug by reference, switch or
buffer, conversion core, clock/control, and output reconstruction, preserving
the raw waveform.

Finish with a technician-ready design and its experiment. Mark every metric
as nominal, worst-case, or not yet verified, and challenge the result from
three directions: system tradeoffs, circuit headroom/stability, and
measurement bandwidth/grounding. This is excellent advanced practice, but it
needs authoritative theory from elsewhere and is not a complete course from
first principles.

Finally, push the reference, temperature, supply, or source impedance to
its stated boundary and try to break the design. If a result depends only on a
datasheet typical value, propose a measurement or fallback. “No problem
observed” is not a worst-case argument. Instrument accuracy, protection
limits, and calibration determine what the boundary test really covered; do
not draw untested temperature or load regions into the safe envelope.
