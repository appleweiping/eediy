### The Laboratory Bench Is the Center of This Course

MIT 6.101's [Spring 2007 syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-101-introductory-analog-electronics-laboratory-spring-2007/pages/syllabus/) defines the work as analog-circuit design, construction, and debugging: 6 experiments in the first half of term and a 7-week product project in the second. It suits learners who already know analog circuits and need practice building and diagnosing them. Choose a lecture-led course instead if theory exposition is the main goal.

MIT names passable performance in 6.002 or 6.071 as sufficient background. For anyone new to oscilloscopes and function generators, the usual gap is not another formula but bench habits such as probe grounding, current limits, and rewiring with power removed. Learn those first at a supervised bench.

### Six laboratory exercises

The [labs page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-101-introductory-analog-electronics-laboratory-spring-2007/pages/labs/) publishes Lab 1–6. Labs 1, 5, and 6 originally required staff check-offs, and MIT estimated about 10 hours of laboratory work per week. An outside learner cannot reproduce live questioning, but a schematic, measured component values, predictions, and raw traces preserve the important design judgment in each experiment.

| Lab | Original emphasis | Most useful phenomenon to observe independently |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Tuned circuits, Q/BW, RF links, AM detection | \(f_0,Q,BW\) and envelope distortion; simulate or use a shielded wired path without antenna transmission |
| 2 | Log amplifier, rectifier/Zener, diode/BJT/FET curves | Difference between a current-limited low-voltage IV sweep and the device model |
| 3 | Bias, low-frequency cutoff, hand-wound inductor, FET switch | Calculated, SPICE, and measured operating point, cutoff, and inductance |
| 4 | LM741/LF356, slew, saturation, Schmitt/integrator circuits | Which supply, common-mode, or stability limit appears first |
| 5 | Push-pull amplifier, differential pair, current sources, 555 VCO | DC offset, output power, and temperature on a dummy load |
| 6 | Series regulator, low-battery indicator, mixer | Line/load regulation, dropout, crosstalk, and clipping |

### Old Components and the Home-Bench Boundary

A practical minimum is an isolated, adjustable, current-limited DC supply, DMM, 2-channel oscilloscope, function generator, and properly rated probes and loads. MIT's [kit warnings](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-101-introductory-analog-electronics-laboratory-spring-2007/resources/kit_warnings/) describe the original kit rather than authorizing an unsupervised setup. RF transmission, mains, high voltage or current, and period high-power tasks belong in an approved supervised laboratory.

The LM741, LF356, and related parts reflect a 2007 bench. A substitute requires a fresh check of supply range, pinout, input common mode, output swing, gain-bandwidth, slew rate, output current, and dissipation. The label “similar op-amp” is insufficient. That version gap is itself a useful debugging exercise.

### The seven-week product project

The [projects page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-101-introductory-analog-electronics-laboratory-spring-2007/pages/projects/) asks a 3-person team to define an idea as a product and compare performance, usability, part count, and cost. An outside version can be a low-energy audio preamplifier, photodiode front end, or function generator. The important part is turning gain, bandwidth, noise, distortion, and load into measurable specifications and testing the most uncertain subcircuit first.

The most valuable project material is not a photograph of the finished device. It is the first substantial disagreement among calculation, SPICE, and measurement and the design change that followed. Explaining that debugging episode captures what distinguishes 6.101 from an ordinary analog-electronics lecture course.
