# MIT 6.011: Introduction to Communication, Control, and Signal Processing

## Course Overview

- **University:** MIT
- **Course code:** 6.011
- **Official prerequisites:** The MIT syllabus requires mastery of 6.003 and 6.041 or equivalents, plus the 18.03 material on solving first-order LTI differential systems with eigenvalues and eigenvectors
- **EEDIY preparation:** EEDIY's 6.003, 6.041SC, and 18.03SC pages provide one public preparation route, but the three EEDIY course instances are not themselves mandatory
- **Access:** Open without registration

### In 6.011, three subjects suddenly begin to explain one another

MIT [6.011 Spring 2010](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-011-introduction-to-communication-control-and-signal-processing-spring-2010/),
taught by Alan Oppenheim and George Verghese, contains 26 lectures. The
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-011-introduction-to-communication-control-and-signal-processing-spring-2010/pages/syllabus/)
requires mastery of 6.003 and 6.041 (or equivalents), plus the relevant 18.03
material on linear ODEs and eigenvalues/eigenvectors. It fits learners who have studied signals, probability, and control
separately but have not connected state estimation, projection, and
detection. Take a complete introduction first for any area that is still new.

### Course structure

The [calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-011-introduction-to-communication-control-and-signal-processing-spring-2010/pages/calendar/)
moves from Fourier/sampling, state space, and observer/feedback into
MMSE/LMMSE, WSS/PSD, and Wiener filtering, then PAM/QAM, hypothesis testing,
matched filters, spectral estimation, and information theory. As an independent comparison, use a
second-order state-space model and a noisy binary link: design an
observer in control, derive an LMMSE/Wiener estimate from covariance, and
reinterpret the same projection as a matched-filter statistic. The integration
is real when the inner product, randomness, and observable survive renamed
variables. These examples are not original MIT assignments.

### Assignments and feedback

OCW supplies the complete [open text](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-011-introduction-to-communication-control-and-signal-processing-spring-2010/resources/mit6_011s10_notes/),
but Problem Set 1–10 prompts and lecture videos are absent. The
[exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-011-introduction-to-communication-control-and-signal-processing-spring-2010/pages/exams/)
retain Quiz 1, Quiz 2, and final question booklets, answer booklets, and
solutions. An answer booklet may be only response paper, not a key. Take all
3 papers closed-book before classifying corrections by model choice,
conditioning, transform, or algebra.

### Two examples test whether the ideas really transfer

For those examples, compare observer poles,
estimation covariance, Wiener theory versus sample estimates, and PAM/QAM
detection at fixed `Eb/N0`. Label self-authored work as an independent exercise
and validate it with rank, the orthogonality principle, energy normalization,
or an analytic limit; it is not the original MIT problem sets. Finally start from
“orthogonal error” and explain the object, inner product, and observable in an
observer, LMMSE, and matched filter. A weak interface shows that the common
language is not yet established. Also identify where process noise, measurement
noise, and the symbol prior enter the two models, then vary one parameter and
explain its different effects in control, estimation, and detection. That
transfer across contexts is the most useful substitute for the missing weekly
problem sets.
