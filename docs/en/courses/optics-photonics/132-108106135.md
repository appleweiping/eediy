## Three entry problems test model changes, not formula recall

Problem 1: start from Maxwell boundary conditions, obtain the field-amplitude reflection and transmission relations at a normally illuminated dielectric interface, and check the \(n_1=n_2\) and high-index-contrast limits. Problem 2: derive the two-beam intensity expression containing mutual coherence and relate path difference, coherence length, and fringe visibility. Problem 3: convert a stated wavelength and optical power into photon energy and photon flux, then use quantum efficiency to obtain ideal photocurrent while closing the units W, J, s, and A.

Complete Problem 3 and at least 1 of the first 2 independently before Week 1. If interface conditions have collapsed into a memorized Fresnel coefficient, field amplitude is added as though it were optical power, or \(h\nu\) disappears from the photon flux, review electromagnetics, complex phasors, and elementary probability first. The provider only calls electromagnetics “preferred.” These problems are an EEDIY placement diagnostic, not an NPTEL enrollment gate and not a substitute for the course's treatment of statistical optics or semiconductor junctions.

## Turn the 10 lab demonstrations into observation records, not build instructions

The 10 official demonstrations cover diffraction and interference; the Michelson interferometer; fiber modes, NA, and MFD; light–matter interaction; EDFA characterization; a fiber laser; semiconductor sources; semiconductor detectors; intensity and polarization manipulation; and an EOM. They are useful for learning how an experimental argument is assembled. For each recording, identify the source, controlled variable, observable, calibration or reference, model prediction, uncertainty, and 1 plausible confounder. If the recording does not show a range, calibration, or raw data, write “not quantitatively reproducible” rather than inventing precise values from a screen image.

For example, the Michelson artifact is the relation between path change and fringe count plus its coherence assumption, not a decorative fringe image. EDFA notes should distinguish signal gain, ASE, and detector dynamic range. Semiconductor-detector notes should separate responsivity, dark current, bandwidth, and noise. The fiber-modes demonstration needs definitions and approximation limits for \(V\)-number, NA, and mode-field diameter. Watching a demonstration documents exposure to an observation chain; it is not evidence of completing a physical lab.

## EEDIY adds a model-to-synthetic-data loop

Build a computation-only photonics model notebook instead of purchasing optics. Implement at least 2 chains: generate an interferogram from source spectrum, path difference, and degree of coherence; and generate detector current and SNR from photon flux, quantum efficiency, and dark, shot, and thermal noise. Add 1 model chosen from three/four-level gain, laser rate equations, fiber normalized frequency, or a Malus/Pockels transfer function. Every model needs units, an operating domain, an analytical baseline, a parameter sweep, and 1 deliberately out-of-domain failure.

The notebook is an EEDIY supplement, not an NPTEL assignment, lab, exam, or certificate artifact. A plausible-looking curve is not acceptance evidence. The interferogram must close at zero path difference and in the short-coherence limit; the photodetector must show the correct noise trends at zero power and high photon flux; and the rate-equation model must determine and report whether its steady state is stable. A polarization model must track Jones-vector norm and relative optical power explicitly: a lossless retarder or ideal Pockels phase transformation should preserve the norm, whereas a polarizer should produce the expected \(\cos^2\theta\) change in squared norm and power under Malus's law. Submit raw arrays, an environment file, plotting sources, and a single rerun command with the report.

## Laser and bare-fiber boundaries outrank the schedule

This route defaults to viewing, derivation, and simulation only. Do not recreate the EDFA, fiber-laser, EOM, Michelson, or detector demonstrations at home. Do not energize an unknown-class or invisible-infrared laser; look into a beam, connector, or fiber end; or use a camera or telescope to hunt for a spot. “Low power” is not an eye-safety assessment. EDFA and telecom-fiber output may be invisible, so a blink response gives no protection, while broken fiber creates small, easily missed sharp fragments.

Any physical optical work belongs in an institutionally approved laser laboratory under a qualified laser supervisor designated by the institution. It requires wavelength, power, and classification assessment; eyewear matched by wavelength and optical density; enclosure, beam stop, interlock, key control, rated detector or power meter, verified de-energization, and labeled fiber-sharps disposal. Never bypass an interlock, search for a leak with a body part, connect or disconnect a fiber or connector while the optical source is energized, or improvise an EDFA pump/driver or an EOM high-voltage driver. A demonstration video is not operating authorization, and this page supplies no hardware procedure.
