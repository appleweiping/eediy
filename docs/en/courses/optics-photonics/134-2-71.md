## Draw Rays before Choosing This as an Entry to Optics

MIT 2.71 is the Spring 2014 undergraduate Optics course; 2.710 is its graduate version.
The official
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-71-optics-spring-2014/pages/syllabus/)
lists 8.02 Physics II, 18.03 Differential Equations, and 2.004 Dynamics and
Control II as prerequisites. 2.710 adds analytical work in HW4–HW6 and a final
project. Thin-lens exercises do not replace electrodynamics, differential
equations, and linear systems.

Describe one 2-lens system 3 ways: ray-transfer matrix for image plane and
magnification; phasor/Jones vector for a retarder; Fourier transform for a
finite pupil's resolution limit. Review paraxial matrices after the 1st
failure, Maxwell equations/complex amplitude/polarization after the 2nd, and
spatial frequency/convolution after the 3rd. This is EEDIY routing, not MIT's
grading threshold.

For video-led photonics vocabulary, NPTEL's
[Introduction to Photonics](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/108106135)
is the easier entry. Its official
[downloads API](https://nptel.ac.in/api/downloads/108106135) currently lists
48 course-download items mixing lectures, tutorials, and lab demonstrations,
but its `assignments` array is empty. Treat it as a 48-video entry, not a
source of solved assignments; recheck registration and current assessment at
enrollment. Choose 2.71 for written derivations, problem sets, historical
assessments, and one geometrical/wave/Fourier chain.

## Rewrite the Same Optical System in Three Languages

The official [calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-71-optics-spring-2014/pages/calendar/)
lists 25 sessions from Nature of Light to scalar/vector wave-optics review.
The [lecture-notes page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-71-optics-spring-2014/pages/lecture-notes/)
lists 12 PDFs: Sessions 7 and 25 are review handouts, leaving 10 substantive
groups. Not all 25 meetings have notes, and no complete public video series
exists. Track invariants across ray, wave, and spatial-frequency
representations.

### Rays: Fix Planes, Signs, and Apertures First

Sessions 2, 4, 7, and 10 cover geometrical optics, paraxial matrices, eikonal,
gradient-index media, and Hamiltonian optics; the calendar adds instruments,
microscopes, telescopes, apertures, and stops. Draw directed reference planes,
declare radius/distance/angle signs, then check determinant, afocal limit, and
chief/marginal rays. Reversed surface order remains a model error.

### Waves: The Phase Account Must Return to Intensity

Sessions 9, 11, and 22 connect the electromagnetic wave equation to
superposition, interference, interferometry, polarization, waveplates, and
birefringence. Declare basis and time convention, derive irradiance from the
complex field, then check zero path difference, orthogonal polarizations, and
vanished coherence—not only a plausible fringe plot.

### Fourier Optics: Put Propagation, Pupil, and Image on One Spectrum

Sessions 13, 14, 16, and 20 connect spatial Fourier transforms,
Fraunhofer and Fresnel diffraction, coherent filtering, depth of field, and
holography. The calendar adds incoherent imaging, resolution, and pupils in
Session 18, then Gaussian beams and laser principles in Session 21. Organize
source → propagation kernel → pupil/transfer function → detector. Give each
approximation a dimensionless condition; check energy normalization, sample
spacing, and field of view.

## A MATLAB Propagation Study Is an EEDIY Supplement, Not a Public Lab

The public directory has no reproducible physical lab. The syllabus describes
a 2.710 final project: teams of 2–3, mentors who suggest a few simple
simulations, and presentations in Sessions 23–24. OCW does not publish that
term's prompts, data, or graded examples.
A MATLAB, GNU Octave, or Python record is an EEDIY supplement, not an MIT
2.71 lab or 2.710 project.

Follow the 3 representations: trace rays with ABCD matrices, propagate a
complex field through an aperture, then compare point-spread function,
modulation transfer, and resolution under Fresnel/Fraunhofer conditions.
Preserve input field, wavelength, grid, normalization, padding, software
version, and seed. Halve spacing, enlarge field of view, and recheck focus,
energy, and conclusion; padding-sensitive results are not optical evidence.

Evidence includes corrected PS1–PS6 attempts, timed scripts for at least 2
solved assessments, a map of 12 note entries, and rerunnable propagation.
Mark project prompt, physical lab, and videos not public. Corrections should
name session, PS/exam item, Fourier convention, and a minimal numerical case.

## A Laser Table Is Not a Home Test of Fourier Optics

The public independent-study route is simulation-only. Calendar references to
a field trip, Gaussian beams, laser principles, waveplates, and holography do
not constitute an off-campus safety manual. Do not construct an exposed laser
path at home, modify a high-voltage laser supply, or align an unknown-class
source toward eyes, reflective objects, or a camera. Specular reflections,
invisible wavelengths, focused beams, and high-voltage supplies remain
hazardous even when the output appears dim.

Physical optics belongs in a lab with laser classification, controlled area,
beam enclosure/stops, eyewear, interlocks, training, and local supervision.
For this R0 route, explain one optical train as rays, fields, and spectra;
close the problem-set loop; reproduce propagation. That is bounded evidence,
not lab qualification or a reconstruction of the class.
