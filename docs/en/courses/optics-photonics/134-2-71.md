### Course fit

MIT **2.71 Optics, Spring 2014** is the undergraduate version; **2.710** is graduate. The [Syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-71-optics-spring-2014/pages/syllabus/) lists 8.02 Physics II, 18.03 Differential Equations, and 2.004 Dynamics and Control II as formal prerequisites. 2.710 adds work in HW4–HW6 and a final project. Semiconductor devices is not an official prerequisite.

Before entering, check readiness with a 2-lens example: use a ray-transfer matrix for the image plane, a phasor/Jones vector for a retarder, and a Fourier transform for the resolution limit of a finite pupil. Review paraxial optics, electromagnetic waves, or spatial frequency according to the missing form.

For a video-led introduction, begin with NPTEL [Introduction to Photonics](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/108106135). Its [Downloads API](https://nptel.ac.in/api/downloads/108106135) lists 48 lecture/tutorial/demonstration items, but an empty `assignments` array. The strength of 2.71 is derivation, problem sets, and historical exams.

### Make the 2-lens example agree as rays, waves, and Fourier optics

The [Calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-71-optics-spring-2014/pages/calendar/) has 25 sessions, while [Lecture Notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-71-optics-spring-2014/pages/lecture-notes/) has only 12 PDFs. Session 7 and 25 are reviews, leaving about 10 substantive groups. No complete lecture-video series is public.

The ray block covers geometrical/paraxial optics, eikonal methods, GRIN, instruments, and apertures; fix reference planes and signs. The wave block moves from the EM equation to interference, polarization, waveplates, and birefringence; complex fields must return to detector irradiance. The Fourier block puts propagation kernels, pupil/transfer functions, Fresnel/Fraunhofer diffraction, imaging, Gaussian beams, and detectors on one map, with sampling, field-of-view, and energy-normalization checks.

Each representation has distinct failure checks. A ray matrix needs surface order, determinant, afocal limit, and chief/marginal rays. A wave calculation should state polarization basis and time convention and return the expected limits for zero path difference, orthogonal polarization, and vanishing coherence. Fourier propagation must align sampling intervals for source, kernel, pupil, and detector. When padding or grid changes the result, repair the numerical representation before interpreting a new optical effect.

The [Exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/2-71-optics-spring-2014/pages/exams/) are not all solved. The 2004 and 2008 Quiz 1 and 2009 Quiz 2 are unsolved; 2012 Quiz 1/2 are solved. The 2013 final has practice solutions plus a Problem 4 solution; the 2014 Quiz 1/2 and final practice are solved. Use 2014 under time, 2012 as a second sample, and older unsolved papers for transfer.

### Access and version notes

Use MATLAB, GNU Octave, or Python for one 2-lens and aperture example: trace rays with ABCD matrices, propagate a complex field through an aperture, and compare PSF, MTF, and resolution under Fresnel/Fraunhofer conditions. Keep wavelength, grid, normalization, padding, software version, and seed. After halving spacing and enlarging field of view, focus, energy, and conclusions should remain stable.

State a dimensionless validity condition for every approximation and preserve a hand-calculable lens or aperture baseline. A diffraction discrepancy can then be traced to a session, problem, Fourier convention, and minimal case rather than blamed generically on MATLAB or Python. The propagation should also return to known results for an empty aperture, an infinite aperture, and the far-field limit, with Parseval or power flow used to check normalization.

The official Syllabus says the original 2.710 project used teams of 2–3,
mentor-suggested simulations, and presentations in Session 23–24, but OCW
publishes no prompt, data, or rubric. An outside route can cover PS1–PS6,
corrections from solved exams across years, a 12-note map, and a propagation
study generated by one command. The revealing check is whether PSF, MTF, and
energy remain consistent across the Fresnel/Fraunhofer transition; a physical
laser table and lab qualification are outside the public material.
