### Course fit and prerequisites

Cornell [ECE 5310 Quantum Optics for Photonics, Fall 2013](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-5310-quantum-optics-for-photonics/) was a graduate course meeting 3 times each week, with a midterm and final and no required textbook. The retained [Course Information](https://courses.cit.cornell.edu/ece531/Info/Info.htm) explicitly expects electrodynamics/optics and quantum mechanics. The opening lectures align notation; they do not reteach those prerequisites.

Be comfortable with a two-level Hamiltonian, density matrices, commutators, tensor products, trace, and partial trace. If pure and mixed states are not yet distinct, or it is unclear what evolves in the Schrödinger, Heisenberg, and interaction pictures, reviewing quantum mechanics first will save time.

### Use the two-level system to connect the first half

The [Lecture Notes and Handouts](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-5310-quantum-optics-for-photonics/ece-5310-lectures-notes-and-handouts/) begin with density matrices, Rabi oscillation, coherence, and EIT. They then quantize the electromagnetic field and develop number, coherent, and squeezed states, correlations, and homodyne/heterodyne detection before reaching cavity QED, amplifiers, lasers, optomechanics, and quantum information. The retained [Lecture Index](https://courses.cit.cornell.edu/ece531/Lectures/Lectures.htm) is useful for reconstructing sequence, but it is a 2013 archive rather than evidence of a current offering.

The difficult distinction is often not algebra but the difference between a quantity in the model and a quantity measured in the laboratory. Use a driven two-level system as the anchor: define field amplitude, Rabi frequency, population, coherence, and detector signal, then write the same problem in the Schrödinger and interaction pictures. Once normalization, units, and approximations are clear in that model, the cavity and laser sections become much easier to read.

### Homework 1–10 are worth completing

[Homework and Exams](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-5310-quantum-optics-for-photonics/ece-5310-homework-and-exams/) publishes Homework 1–10 with all solutions, an unusually complete set for an open quantum-optics course. Derive the main result independently, then use the solution to check signs, operator ordering, and normalization. Change an initial state, detuning, or decay afterward to see whether the method still works. Exam 1 has a solution and makes a good timed check. The final does not, so trace, probability, and analytical limits become the checks.

### One numerical extension

The public package has no classroom video, code, experimental data, or reproducible original project. For a numerical extension, choose either a driven two-level system or a lossy cavity, compare master-equation integration with one analytical limit, and sweep detuning or decay. Check trace and Hermiticity for the two-level model; state the Fock-space cutoff and make one convergence comparison for the cavity. This exercise adds model intuition, not Cornell optical alignment or detector calibration.
