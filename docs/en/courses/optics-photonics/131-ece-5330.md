## Use three diagnostics to enforce the strong prerequisites

Before Handout 1, attempt these three EEDIY diagnostics. They are not a Cornell admission test.

1. **Quantum mechanics:** for the one-dimensional infinite well \(0<x<L\), normalize the \(n=1,2,3\) eigenstates, give their energies, calculate \(\langle1|x|2\rangle\), and use parity about the well center to explain why \(\langle1|x|3\rangle=0\). If only the energy formula survives while wavefunctions, density of states, and occupation are blurred together, review quantum mechanics first.
2. **Electromagnetic radiation:** for normal incidence from a lossless medium \(n_1\) to \(n_2\), derive the field-amplitude reflection and transmission coefficients from tangential-field boundary conditions, use Poynting flux to verify \(R+T=1\), and explain why field amplitude cannot be treated as power. If you cannot close the boundary-condition derivation, phasor calculation, and flux balance, review material at roughly the ECE 3030 level.
3. **Semiconductor devices or solids:** draw an equilibrium and forward-biased heterojunction or PIN band diagram; label Fermi or quasi-Fermi levels, doping, and carrier-flow directions; then connect generation, recombination, and current density with units. If the band diagram and carrier statistics remain separate stories, review material at roughly the ECE 4570 or ECE 4070 level.

A clear failure in any one area should pause the course rather than be hidden by rereading notes. Retest with changed parameters, retain the first attempt, and identify whether the review concerned boundary conditions, statistics, device diagrams, or units. The diagnostics turn the provider's “strong prereqs” into observable work without pretending to grant an official waiver.

## Connect the 16 handouts with a carrier–photon–mode ledger

The official [lecture-notes index](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-5330-semiconductor-optoelectronics/ece-5330-lectures-notes-and-handouts/) contains 16 handouts. They should not be flattened into a mechanical 16-week checklist. Use a four-column ledger instead: **material and carrier state → transition, absorption/gain, and spontaneous emission → optical mode, confinement, and loss → terminal power, efficiency, bandwidth, threshold, or noise**. For every new device, identify the model in each column, the quantity that connects adjacent columns, and the location of each conservation check.

Handout 1 reviews semiconductor basics; 2 introduces heterostructures, quantum wells, and strain; 3 organizes light–matter interaction, gain, and spontaneous emission around Fermi's golden rule; and 4 treats III–V bandstructure and tight binding. Handout 5 moves to photodetectors and solar cells; 6 links dielectric response, free-carrier effects, and Kramers–Kronig; and 7 covers LEDs, radiative and nonradiative recombination, and lighting. At this point the first two ledger columns should connect band and carrier models to observable optoelectronic response.

Handout 8 develops waveguide modes; 9 explains SOAs through material gain, modal confinement, loss, and carrier/photon equations; 10 returns to quantum-well transitions; and 11 introduces laser rate equations and dynamics. Handout 12 covers cavities, S/T matrices, DBRs, and VCSELs; 13 extends the chain to gratings, DBR, and DFB structures; 14 treats chirp, phase noise, and linewidth; 15 addresses tunable lasers and carrier-induced index change; and 16 ends with plasmonics and plasmon lasers. The point of this half is not acronym collection. It is to explain why material gain is not automatically terminal gain, how cavity loss enters threshold, and how a phase perturbation becomes chirp or linewidth.

An archive study rhythm can use conceptual gates: Handouts 1–2 followed by HW1–2; 3–4 followed by HW3–4; 5–7 followed by HW5; 8–10 followed by HW6; and 11–16 followed by HW7–8. This is an EEDIY execution sequence, not the official Fall 2014 calendar. Treat a block as incomplete until its ledger connects definitions, equations, approximations, and observables.

## Let the 8 homework sets set the pace, not the solutions

The official [homework-and-exams page](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-5330-semiconductor-optoelectronics/ece-5330-homework-and-exams/) publishes HW1–8 and a solution for **every one of the 8 sets**. HW1 reviews semiconductors and quantum mechanics. HW2 covers heterojunctions, quantum wells, quaternary materials, and PIN structures. HW3 treats optical transients and gain; HW4 spontaneous emission, matrix elements, and gain versus temperature; and HW5 solar cells and thermodynamics. HW6 combines SOAs with a waveguide solver. HW7 treats an InGaAsP laser, transients, and rate equations. HW8 covers DBRs, VCSELs, S/T matrices, and DFB structures, and its prompt explicitly calls it a long two-week assignment that **counts twice as much as another homework**.

Retain two records for each set: a first attempt made without solution hints and a separate correction memo. A first attempt must reach the actual blocking step; “see solution” is not an ending. The memo should classify the failure as a material parameter, carrier statistic, transition model, mode normalization, unit, numerical method, or physical interpretation. After reading a solution, wait and redo the central derivation with changed parameters to distinguish transfer from memorized shape. Public solutions provide textual feedback; they are neither an automatic grader nor an original-course score.

HW3 and HW4 should each leave a numerical notebook. The HW3 notebook records transient or gain equations, initial conditions, steps or tolerances, and analytical limits.
