### An Information-Theory Notes Course for Proof Practice

MIT [6.441 Spring 2010](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-441-information-theory-spring-2010/)
moves from entropy, typicality, and source coding into capacity, Gaussian
channels, multiple access, broadcast, and side information. It fits learners
with probability, random processes, linear algebra, and proof experience who
want achievability/converse arguments and multiuser models. Pair it with a
more complete course if videos, per-problem solutions, or a programming
project must provide the main feedback.

### Course structure

The [lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-441-information-theory-spring-2010/pages/lecture-notes/)
provide 23 lectures. For each theorem, state the alphabet or channel model,
probabilistic assumptions, constraint, limit, and most fragile proof step.
Separate direct and converse coding arguments, state power and noise for
Gaussian results, and draw who knows what in multiuser results. Add a
`failure if removed` field: ask what changes without memorylessness,
stationarity, fixed variance, or decoder side information.

### Assignments and feedback

The [assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-441-information-theory-spring-2010/pages/assignments/)
provide Problem Set 1–9 without solutions. OCW also has no public videos or
complete exam set, and the original project prompt is incomplete. Preserve
`claim / proof / numerical check / unresolved` for each problem. Finite
alphabet enumeration or Blahut–Arimoto validates only a special case, not a
general proof. The useful output from an unresolved problem is a minimal lemma
that can be asked precisely, not a numerical observation promoted to theorem.

For the exit, build 3 synthesis notes on source coding, the single-user
channel, and Gaussian/multiuser models. Check the Huffman average-length
bound, symmetric-channel capacity, and a Gaussian MAC region, preserving pmf,
precision, and stopping rule. Label the programs as independent numerical
exercises. They are not official MIT projects. The final index distinguishes theorem,
example, and conjecture and records lecture/page plus outside proof sources.
For each multiuser result, draw the senders, receivers, messages, and side
information instead of carrying over a single-user capacity formula by
analogy. When a proof stalls, isolate the smallest missing lemma and test it
against an extreme distribution or a tiny alphabet. A numerical match can
narrow the doubt but cannot promote an unproved statement to a course theorem.
As a final check, pick one result at random and explain separately what its
achievability construction and converse bound depend on.

After 6.441, a capacity expression should immediately raise questions about
the alphabet, constraint, achievability, and converse. The formula is only the
middle of the argument.
