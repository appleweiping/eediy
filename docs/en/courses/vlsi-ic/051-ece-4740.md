### Course fit

Cornell's [ECE 4740 page](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/ece-4740-course-details)
preserves the Spring 2018 senior-level offering. It moves from transistors and
switching networks through combinational/sequential logic, clocks, PLAs, and
memories into floorplanning, CMOS scaling, timing, arithmetic, fixed-point
optimization, power, and performance. The focus is the trade among function,
area, speed, and complexity in a synchronous CMOS system. Choose another path
for an FPGA-only HDL course or analog-IC layout.

The required text is Weste and Harris, *CMOS VLSI Design*, fourth edition.
Before starting, be able to derive a complementary CMOS network from a Boolean
function, analyze setup/hold and a critical path, and read basic layout rules.
A commercial CAD interface will otherwise conceal rather than repair the
conceptual gap.

### Access and version notes

Read the [lecture notes and handouts](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/ece-4740-course-details/ece-4740-lecture-notes-and-handouts)
as device, gate, module, and system layers: locate capacitance and delay,
compare gate sizing and power, study adders, memories, and datapaths, then
consider floorplans, clocks, and architecture transforms. State process,
load, and clock assumptions so that a slide's empirical number does not become
a cross-process constant.

The overview describes 4–5 homework assignments, 3 preliminary exams, 4
extensive design labs, and a final project lasting about one quarter of the
term. The public
[homework and labs archive](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/ece-4740-course-details/ece-4740-homework-and-labs),
however, lists only 3 homework PDFs and Lab 1–5, without solutions, exams, or
a project brief. The Spring 2018 archive supports partial reconstruction, not
the complete campus course.

For each public lab, predict function, critical path, load, dominant area, and
power source before running the handout's simulation, synthesis, or layout
flow. Compare functional waveforms, timing reports, area or cell count, and
one constraint-violating case. Label a modern open-PDK or FPGA implementation
as a port; LUT counts are not standard-cell area.

### Labs and projects

Because the original final project is hidden, a learner may build a bounded
synchronous datapath such as a fixed-point FIR, iterative multiplier, or small
accelerator. This is an independent extension with no corresponding Cornell
prompt. Include a
specification, interface timing, golden model, random and boundary tests,
synthesis constraints, and a timing/area comparison of at least 2
architectures.

Close the design with two traces: one input from specification through test
vector, waveform, and synthesized netlist; and one critical path from clock
target through constraints, timing analysis, and structural change. Name the
device or process, tool versions, and unknowns. The public archive lacks
commercial licenses, the Cornell lab environment, prelims, and grading, so
stop at the independent small datapath rather than inventing a fourth homework
or Cornell's missing project.
