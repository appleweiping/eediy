### Course structure

Lectures 1–12 cover PMMC, electrodynamic and moving-iron instruments, damping, and galvanometers. Lectures 13–27 cover ammeters/voltmeters/ohmmeters, 4-terminal resistance, bridges, and wattmeters. Lectures 28–46 cover energy meters, potentiometers, AC bridges, CT/PT, and magnetic measurement. Lectures 47–70 cover counters, op-amps, frequency meters, DVMs, and ADC/DAC. Lectures 71–84 cover instrumentation amplifiers, generators, the 555 timer, and oscilloscopes.

Read each family through `measurand → conversion → loading/conditioning → indication → uncertainty`, recording input range, transfer relation, dominant error, calibration/check method, and hazard boundary. Lecture 23 connects absolute/relative/limiting error; Lectures 42–44 explain how CT/PT burden changes both ratio and phase; Lecture 58 turns gate time and quantization into frequency-counter resolution; Lectures 64–65 derive dual-slope rejection; and Lectures 80–81 separate probe loading, vertical path, time base, and trigger. Keep references, calibration dates, and traceability gaps separate from formula-derived error.

### Simulation tests instrument judgment

Useful studies are voltmeter loading, Wheatstone/Kelvin bridge tolerance Monte Carlo, a dual-slope DVM, and a frequency-counter gate-time model. Preserve equation/schematic, domain, units, seed, solver, and raw output, and check an analytical limit or old problem. A SPICE waveform is not calibrated measurement.

For a bridge, compare nominal behavior with a tolerance distribution and explain where source resistance, lead resistance, or detector sensitivity enters. For a dual-slope model, derive line-frequency rejection from integrator, reference, and clock. For a counter, report update rate along with the gate-time sweep. Returning these quantities to the same chain reveals that resolution gains usually trade against speed, loading, or noise. The uncertainty budget should distinguish random terms estimated from repeats, systematic terms supplied by specifications or calibration, and model-form terms that cannot yet be quantified; displayed digits cannot simply be added into a total error.

### Change One Range and Recompute Loading, Resolution, and Conversion Time

Choose a voltmeter, bridge, dual-slope DVM, or frequency counter and alter only
its range, input impedance, integration time, or gate time. Compare the
transfer relation, loading, resolution, and conversion time before and after
the change. If the prediction alters only the displayed digits, the measurement
chain is still incomplete. Agreement with an old problem checks only the
formula implementation within that case; it does not establish the accuracy,
calibration state, or safety rating of a physical instrument.

Mains, polyphase systems, energized CT/PT, and earth-referenced oscilloscopes
belong in controlled facilities. The public archive has no home lab, code
grader, or stable current certificate exam.
