### Move from Device Interior to Circuit Terminals

MIT 6.012 Fall 2009 connects semiconductor physics, pn junctions, MOS
capacitors, MOSFET/BJT models, amplifiers, logic, and frequency response. The
official [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-012-microelectronic-devices-and-circuits-fall-2009/pages/syllabus/)
has no lecture videos. Choose it if you are willing to draw energy bands and
analyze small-signal circuits. If your device knowledge ends at the ideal
diode, first review carrier statistics, drift and diffusion, and depletion.
If devices are familiar but KCL/KVL with dependent sources is not, review
circuits.

The [readings](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-012-microelectronic-devices-and-circuits-fall-2009/pages/readings/)
link Fonstad's open textbook alongside the course's assigned references. That
makes 6.012 unusually complete for independent study. Keep the Fall 2009 label,
however, and do not insert modern compact models or process parameters into the
historical course.

### Translate Every Device across Physical and Circuit Views

Build one table from physical structure and assumptions to terminal equations,
operating regions, and small-signal parameters, including temperature,
geometry, and bias dependence. Then place the model in an amplifier and check
operating point, headroom, gain, and dominant poles. This gives \(g_m\),
\(r_o\), and capacitance a physical origin instead of treating them as symbols
that appear on a circuit diagram.

The [assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-012-microelectronic-devices-and-circuits-fall-2009/pages/assignments/)
contain 10 problem sets and a take-home design begun after Lecture 19. The
sets lack a complete set of matching solutions. For the design, include the
specification, hand model, bias and power budgets, SPICE model source, corner
sweeps, and decision record. Passing nominal simulation does not verify every
requirement.

### Follow the Exams with a Model Migration

The [exam archive](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-012-microelectronic-devices-and-circuits-fall-2009/pages/exams/)
provides Fall 2009 Exam 1 and Exam 2 with solutions; final support is
incomplete. Take the first two under time and classify errors by device
assumption, operating region, bias, small-signal topology, or arithmetic.

A strong additional exercise is to extract one MOSFET or BJT model from safe measurements or public I–V
data. Predict a second bias condition with those fitted parameters, plot
residuals, and mark the model's invalid regions. Put those parameters into a
one-stage amplifier and observe how device error reaches gain, bandwidth, and
swing. Explaining the causal movement from device interior to terminal model
to circuit metric is the course's lasting result; plot the original fit
residuals and their units directly below the prediction.
