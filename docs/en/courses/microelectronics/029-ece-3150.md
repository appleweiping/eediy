### Course format

Cornell [ECE 3150 Microelectronics](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3150-microelectronics/) suits learners who enjoy derivations and device curves. This Spring 2016 upper-undergraduate course starts with carrier transport, recombination, PN junctions, and MOS capacitors, then reaches MOSFETs and BJTs, small-signal stages, differential pairs, frequency response, feedback, mixers, CMOS, subthreshold operation, and FinFETs. There is no lecture video and no assigned textbook. What survives is a strong package of 27 handouts, 12 homework sets with solutions, two solved exams, and device laboratories.

This is a poor course to “watch through” and a good course to work through. Its pleasure comes from seeing a device approximation reappear a few pages later as an amplifier's bias, gain, swing, or pole. Learners who like derivations and curves will find the archive unusually complete. For a first encounter with semiconductors that needs continuous explanation, take a device introduction first.

Try four small tasks before beginning: draw a PN-junction band diagram, identify a MOS operating region, turn a bias point into a small-signal model, and interpret complex gain, poles, and phase margin. The page says junior level but publishes no formal prerequisite number. A miss on one task points to a focused review of device physics, circuits, or frequency-domain analysis.

### Course structure

The [Lecture Notes and Handouts](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3150-microelectronics/ece-3150-lecture-notes-and-handouts/) begin with transport, junctions, MOS capacitors, and MOSFETs; move through CS/CG/CD stages, mirrors, cascodes, and differential amplifiers; then treat Miller effect, stability, mixers, CMOS, and short-channel topics. [Homework and Exams](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3150-microelectronics/ece-3150-homework-and-exams/) provides separate solutions for HW1–HW12 and answers for the midterm and final. Finish a derivation before opening its key, or an unusually useful feedback set becomes a transcription exercise.

Every approximation should carry its range forward: equilibrium, channel-length modulation, and why a capacitance is open or short at the frequency in question. Saturation current, ideality factor, or series resistance fitted from a diode IV curve should enter the next bias prediction. Threshold, \(g_m\), \(r_o\), and body-effect assumptions extracted from MOS curves should explain the next amplifier stage. Deliberately changing the operating point is the fastest way to expose a constant that only worked inside its fitting interval.

### Labs and feedback

On [Projects and Labs](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3150-microelectronics/ece-3150-projects-and-labs/), Lab 1 uses an SMU for diode IV sweeps and harmonic analysis; Lab 2 measures CD4007C NFET/PFET behavior and a CMOS transfer curve; Lab 3 builds ALD1105 cascades, mirrors, cascodes, and load sweeps. Those three have solutions. [Lab 4](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/files/2017/03/ece3150_lab4-1cyoaq6.pdf) is optional bonus work with no solution, grading example, or separate project brief. Another easy detail to miss: the course page says 2016, while the public Lab 1 PDF says 2015.

Without the SMU and original data, use an attributed datasheet curve or a device model explicitly labeled synthetic to practise extraction. Do not call that Cornell Lab 1. Script the cleaning, unit conversion, FFT, and fitting, and make each plotted line traceable to a file, device, and fitting interval. “Generic NMOS” is not enough information to reproduce a result.

### A good finish lets one device determine one amplifier

Choose either a diode or a MOS device and connect its extracted parameters to a cascode or differential stage. Fit one interval, test another, then change bias and see whether \(g_m\), \(r_o\), gain, swing, and output resistance still agree. A software-only path should simply leave measurement absent. In a suitable laboratory, add current limits, instrument uncertainty, and real curves.

The 27 PDFs have done their job when an unfamiliar IV or gain curve prompts a defensible physical model, a source and valid range for each parameter, and one next measurement that would separate several plausible explanations.
