### Access and version notes

Bernhard Boser's [EE105 course home](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~boser/courses/105/index.html)
is the Fall 2012 offering, not Berkeley's current entry. It moves from
semiconductors, diodes, BJTs, and bias through MOS devices, differential
pairs, op-amps, frequency response, and a two-part AM radio. The
[course information](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~boser/courses/105/information.html)
expects EE40-level KCL/KVL, node analysis, impedance, Bode plots, and basic
instrument use. Choose it to connect devices, amplifiers, and bench work. The
Razavi text is commercial, and old bSpace/Piazza paths are incomplete.

### Compare BJTs and MOSFETs through Bias, \(g_m\), and \(r_o\)

Move from charge or cross-section to terminal equations, from bias point to
small-signal model, and then to amplifier gain, input/output resistance,
headroom, and bandwidth. For common-emitter, common-source, and differential
stages, repeat DC bias, midband, pole/zero, and large-signal swing analysis.
That comparison separates effects of topology from effects of the device law.

The home page lists Homework 1–14, 2 midterms, and a final.
[Homework 1](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~boser/courses/105/assignments/H1.pdf)
is a direct entry. Solution coverage is uneven: a
[final solution](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~boser/courses/105/assignments/FINAL_solution.pdf)
survives, while most homework lacks equivalent support. Attempt the work first,
then use operating-region checks, KCL residuals, gain/headroom limits, and
SPICE for review.

### Organize the Simulations around a Two-Stage Amplifier

The archive includes an [LTspice guide](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~boser/courses/105/resources/LTSpice.pdf),
but many lab handouts and feedback paths lived in bSpace and cannot be fully
reconstructed. At low voltage, perform BJT/MOS characterization, a one-stage
amplifier, frequency response, and a differential pair, then build a
two-stage tuned/amplifier chain. Preserve bias, measured device values, model
source, raw waveforms, and one fault-localization episode. The final question
is how a device-parameter change passes through bias and small-signal
relations to system gain, bandwidth, or distortion.

After the nominal chain works, deliberately change a bias resistor, add a
load, or remove a bypass capacitor. Predict the affected node voltages, poles,
and output metrics before measuring. Tracing the symptom back to a device
region or small-signal path is closer to the experimental character of EE105
than one attractive AM output. Put the operating points before and after the
fault side by side. If the circuit merely starts working again and you cannot
name the changed bias or small-signal path, the diagnosis is unfinished.
