### The challenge in 6.013 is connecting fields to device ports

MIT [6.013 Spring 2009](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-013-electromagnetics-and-applications-spring-2009/pages/syllabus/) is David Staelin's undergraduate electromagnetics course. Its named preparation is 18.01/18.02 calculus or equivalent, 8.01/8.02 physics or equivalent, 6.002 circuits, and 6.00 programming; Fourier methods are also used. It does more than boundary-value problems: Maxwell equations, media, and waves lead into motors and generators, MEMS, transmission lines, resonators, waveguides, antennas, fibers, lasers, and acoustics. It best suits learners who know vector calculus and phasors but cannot yet make fields, energy, and terminal quantities check each other.

Begin with three sheets. Convert integral field laws to differential form with directions fixed. Analyze traveling waves, reflection, and initial and final values on a step-loaded transmission line. Derive the relation between \(Q\) and bandwidth from energy exchange in an RLC resonator. State reference planes, material parameters, units, and limiting cases on each page. Review the failed interface rather than using an aggregate readiness score.

### Course structure

On the [Calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-013-electromagnetics-and-applications-spring-2009/pages/calendar/), Lectures 1–4 establish Maxwell equations, boundaries, plane waves, and Poynting's theorem. Lectures 5–10 cover force, motor and MEMS models, Laplace problems, lossy media, and TE/TM waves. Lectures 11–16 treat transients, transmission lines, Smith charts, resonators, and waveguides. Lectures 17–24 proceed to antenna links, diffraction, fibers, lasers, and acoustics. In each block retain the control volume, constitutive relation, boundary condition, power balance, and observable port quantity.

The official [Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-013-electromagnetics-and-applications-spring-2009/pages/assignments/) page has Problem Sets 1–12, but public solutions only for 2–7, 9, 11, and 12. For an unsolved problem, require a pair of independent checks: energy conservation plus a limiting case, or a closed-form special case plus numerical convergence. Both quizzes and the final on [Exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-013-electromagnetics-and-applications-spring-2009/pages/exams/) have solutions and equation sheets. In corrections, draw the field picture, write the boundary, and identify where power enters, is stored, and is dissipated.

Three small problems show whether those ideas really meet. For a step-loaded line, place \(\Gamma\), traveling waves, terminal voltage and current, and reflected energy on a shared time axis. For a cavity or lumped resonator, derive \(Q\) from stored energy and loss and cross-check it against bandwidth. For a simple antenna link, complete a power budget using pattern, polarization, distance, and effective aperture. Vary a boundary or material parameter in each and demand agreement between field and energy predictions at the port.

A numerical picture does not replace a boundary-condition audit. Label a field map with mesh, domain truncation, excitation, reference plane, and normalization and report a mesh refinement. At conductor and dielectric interfaces, check tangential and normal component conditions explicitly. Put a passivity, reciprocity, or energy residual beside each S-parameter result. If a result holds only for a particular grid or reference impedance, keep that limitation with the figure instead of losing it during export.

### An open tool can check modern port data

The unofficial [scikit-rf](https://github.com/scikit-rf/scikit-rf) project is an active BSD-3-Clause Python RF and microwave library with networks, S-parameters, transmission lines, calibration, and instrument-data tools. It is not an MIT lab and does not replace a Maxwell derivation. After analyzing fields and lines, use versioned Touchstone or synthetic networks to check reference impedance, de-embedding, cascade operations, and power-wave conventions.

The course's 15 MATLAB movies are computational demonstrations. They do not authorize home reconstruction of lasers, radar, plasma, motors, or high-power RF. A public self-study route can remain theoretical and simulated.

### Choose one device and derive its port from the field

Choose a transmission line, resonator, or antenna link. Derive a terminal quantity from Maxwell equations and boundary conditions, then check it with Poynting's theorem or an energy balance. Vary a material, length, or load in a numerical model and verify that the conclusion survives time-step or mesh refinement. Include the field picture, reference planes, port parameters, instantaneous or average power, stored and dissipated energy, and error sources.

If a Smith chart can be operated but reflected energy cannot be explained, or Poynting's theorem cannot be connected to circuit power, the representations remain separate. A reader who has understood 6.013 can describe one physical process consistently as a field distribution, energy flow, and measurable port quantity, and predict either view from another.
