## Today’s ECE3030 Is Not This Public Offering

Current Cornell ECE3030 is a four-credit undergraduate electromagnetics
course. Its formal prerequisites are PHYS 2213, MATH 2930, and ECE 2100 or
ENGRD 2100. Outcomes include using vector calculus in multiple coordinate
systems to solve static and dynamic Maxwell equations, analyzing waves in
materials, transmission lines and waveguides, impedance matching, and wire,
array, and dish antennas. The complete public material, however, is Farhan
Rana’s Fall 2007 archive, not a 2025 course snapshot.

The [current Cornell catalogue](https://courses.cornell.edu/courses/ece/ece.pdf)
tells us what the course expects today; the
[2007 course page](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3030-electromagnetic-fields-and-waves-2/)
contains the material we can actually study. The archive describes three lectures and one section
per week, no required text, and no lecture video. Its strength is a continuous
set of instructor notes, problems, and solutions rather than a modern platform
or project sequence.

## If One of These Four Is Rusty, Stop and Review

Before the 36 handouts, a learner should be able to:

1. compute gradient, divergence, and curl in Cartesian, cylindrical, and
   spherical coordinates and use divergence and Stokes’ theorems;
2. solve constant-coefficient differential equations and interpret
   time-harmonic fields through phasors;
3. use complex magnitude/phase, power, and impedance and explain basic
   transmission-line reflection;
4. connect charge, current, field, energy, and boundary conditions from
   circuits and introductory electromagnetics.

Weakness in the first item shows up immediately in electrostatic boundary-value
problems, waveguides, and antenna integrals. Weak second and third items reduce
waves and transmission lines to
formula matching. A weak fourth item turns Maxwell equations into unrelated
symbols. Review those topics before H1–3 rather than waiting for Smith charts
or TE/TM modes.

## The 36 Handouts Form a Six-Part Route

[Lecture Notes and Handouts](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3030-electromagnetic-fields-and-waves-2/ece-3030-lectures-notes-and-handouts-2/)
divide naturally by physical problem:

1. **H1–12: static fields.** Maxwell equations, electrostatics and
   magnetostatics, Poisson/Laplace, boundaries, conductors and dielectrics,
   images, polarization, energy, and force.
2. **H13–19: waves and media.** Wave equation, harmonic fields, polarization,
   isotropic/anisotropic/dispersive media, reflection, and refraction.
3. **H20–24: transmission and matching.** Impedance transformation, Smith
   chart, stub and quarter-wave matching, multilayers, and transients.
4. **H25–27: guided structures.** Parallel plate, dielectric slab,
   rectangular metal waveguide, TE/TM modes, and cutoff.
5. **H28–33: radiation and antennas.** Retarded potentials, Hertzian, wire,
   and loop dipoles, transmitting/receiving antennas, effective area, arrays.
6. **H34–36: scattering and apertures.** Radar equation, aperture diffraction,
   and reflector/parabolic dishes close the sequence.

This runs from electrostatic boundary problems to antennas and diffraction; it
is not a lightweight survey. After each block, explain the result from Maxwell
equations, boundary conditions, and energy or power—not only a memorized final
formula.

## The Twelve Solved Homeworks Matter More Than Video

[Homework and Exams](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3030-electromagnetic-fields-and-waves-2/ece-3030-homework-and-exams-2/)
publishes twelve homework sets with solutions. For H20–24, solve the
transmission-line and matching problems first, then separate Smith-chart
reading, phasor-sign, impedance-normalization, and boundary-condition errors.
Redo the missed problem the next day. That diagnosis is more useful than
calling every miss “algebra.”

The same index lists Exam 1, 2, 3, and Final with solutions; the open final is
labelled Cornell ECE 303, Fall 2007. The course page instead says two prelims
and a final. That archive inconsistency means the four files should be used as
topic-spanning self-tests, not asserted to be three prelims plus final from one
offering without stronger syllabus evidence.

There is no need to invent a capstone. Choose one problem from each block and
keep the coordinate choice, boundary conditions, derivation, dimensional or
power check, and correction. Finish with one map connecting static fields,
waves, transmission lines, waveguides, and antennas. Six fully worked problems
say more than a folder containing all 36 handouts.

### This Public Offering Has No Labs or Projects

The course, lecture, and homework pages state that Projects and Labs are not
available. An isolated placeholder with empty “Project 1–6” and “Lab 1–6”
headings contains no files and is not evidence of a project sequence. For
independent study, use ECE3030 as a theory course built around notes and solved
problems, not as a lab-based or project-based course.

An optional EEDIY numerical check can be added with clear attribution: plot a
transmission-line reflection coefficient or Smith-chart path in a reproducible
script, or compare rectangular-waveguide cutoff with an open field solver.
State equations, boundaries, mesh/numeric error, and analytic cross-check. Do
not call it a Cornell lab; physical RF transmission, microwave power, and
measurement are also outside this public archive.

If you want a bench laboratory, this archive is the wrong entry point. Its
strength is exactly the 36 connected handouts and 12 solved assignments.
Numerical experiments can be useful, but they do not turn it into a Cornell
lab course that was never published.
