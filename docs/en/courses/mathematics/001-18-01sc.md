## Test Function Language, Trigonometry, and Units at Entry

Do not diagnose readiness by counting memorized formulas. Without references,
perform three checks:

1. For \(f(x)=\sin(2x)/(1+x^2)\), state the domain, describe and sketch how
   its magnitude behaves for large \(|x|\), and explain one limit both
   algebraically and graphically.
   If domain and limit collapse into one idea, review functions and
   inequalities first.
2. Derive \(\sin(a+b)\) from the unit circle, then rewrite
   \(A\cos \omega t+B\sin \omega t\) in amplitude-phase form. If you can only
   recall the identity, revisit its derivation; the same gap will resurface in
   periodic motion, AC quantities, and introductory Fourier analysis.
3. If \(i(t)\) has units A, state the units and meaning of
   \(\int i(t)\,dt\), then check the units of \(i'(t)\). Losing units under an
   operation turns a formally correct expression into a bad engineering
   model.

This is EEDIY routing, not an official MIT entrance test. Failure on item 1
calls for algebra and graph review; item 2 calls for trigonometric identities;
item 3 calls for a variable-and-unit table on every problem. More video time
will not address those specific gaps.

## Move Through Five Units as Local Change, Accumulation, and Approximation

The official [Unit 1](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/pages/1.-differentiation/)
asks learners to follow the displayed order. Units 1–2 move from limits,
derivative rules, implicit and inverse differentiation to linear
approximation, related rates, Newton's method, and optimization. Units 3–4
move from the definite integral and the fundamental theorem to area, average,
work, integration techniques, and improper integrals. The final
[Unit 5](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-01sc-single-variable-calculus-fall-2010/pages/unit-5-exploring-the-infinite/)
covers sequences, series, Taylor expansions, and error. These are not five
formula sheets but three changes of object: how a function changes at a
point, how local change accumulates, and how a finite polynomial approximates
a function with controlled error.

For EE, the useful interfaces follow the same three changes. A derivative is
the local slope of a device curve at an operating point, the mathematical
prototype of small-signal linearization. Integrating current yields charge;
integrating power yields energy; integrating the square of a periodic
quantity over one period is one step in computing RMS. A Taylor remainder
says when an approximation fails as the
operating point moves. 18.01SC itself does not teach diodes, transistors,
circuit laws, or spectra. Those are EEDIY transfer interpretations, not MIT
course content.

After each part, keep an approximation ledger: object, expansion point or
integration interval, assumptions, units, error source, and one limiting
case. Optimization requires endpoints and feasibility; an improper integral
requires an explicit limit; a series requires a convergence condition. That
ledger exposes operating-point violations, energy-unit errors, and missing
approximation conditions more effectively than another block of identical
differentiation drills.
