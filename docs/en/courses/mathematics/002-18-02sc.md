## The Hard Part Is Geometry, Not Merely Having Three Variables

The MIT [18.02SC course home](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-02sc-multivariable-calculus-fall-2010/)
identifies Denis Auroux's Fall 2010 OCW Scholar edition. It uses classroom
recordings from Auroux's Fall 2007 course and adds recitations, notes,
problem sets, exams, and Java Mathlets for independent study. The new
difficulty is not replacing \(x\) with \(x,y,z\). It is managing object,
coordinate, and orientation at once: scalar or vector field, curve or
surface, parameterized integration region, and chosen normal or boundary
direction.

The official [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-02sc-multivariable-calculus-fall-2010/pages/syllabus/)
lists 18.01 Single Variable Calculus as prerequisite and gives a provider
estimate of about 150 hours, roughly 1 hour or more per session. No textbook
is required for the public main path. The 150 hours are still a course
estimate, not a schedule to copy. Log drawing, derivation, blank-page work, and
correction time through the first 8 sessions of Unit 1 before deciding
whether to compress video.

The adjacent Fall 2010
[18.022 syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-022-calculus-of-several-variables-fall-2010/pages/syllabus/)
also requires 18.01, but uses Colley and places more emphasis in 34 meetings
on \(n\)-dimensional space, differentiability, moving frames, manifolds, and
differential forms. Its original class weights weekly assignments, hour
exams, and final at 25%, 45%, and 30%. Prefer 18.02SC when many visualizations,
segmented videos, and solved exercises are needed to build spatial judgment
independently. Choose 18.022 after the three major integral theorems are
computationally secure and a more conceptual generalization is the goal.

## Use Three Drawings, Not a Formula Quiz, at Entry

Perform three EEDIY diagnostics before opening answers. They are not an
official MIT entrance test:

1. Given two non-collinear vectors, compute a projection, cross product, and
   the area of their parallelogram, with a clear right-hand orientation. If
   the cross product is only a determinant gesture, begin with vectors and
   determinants.
2. For \(f(x,y)=x^2+xy+2y^2\), draw contours and give the gradient, one
   directional derivative, and tangent-plane approximation at a point. If
   the three answers are unrelated, review single-variable differentiation
   and linear approximation.
3. Rewrite \(\iint (x^2+y^2)\,dA\) over the unit disk in polar coordinates
   and explain the factor \(r\) in the area element. If
   \(r\,dr\,d\theta\) is only a substitution rule, do not proceed to
   spherical coordinates or surface integrals.

Item 1 diagnoses orientation, item 2 local linearization, and item 3 the
Jacobian. Use those same three labels on later errors so an arithmetic slip
does not conceal an incorrectly drawn object.

## Read the Four Units Through Objects, Coordinates, and Theorems

The official [Unit 1](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-02sc-multivariable-calculus-fall-2010/pages/1.-vectors-and-matrices/)
moves through vectors, determinants, planes, matrices, linear systems, and
parametric curves. Unit 2 joins multivariable functions to tangent
approximation, optimization, the chain rule, gradients, directional
derivatives, and Lagrange multipliers. Unit 3 covers double integrals, plane
vector fields, line integrals, and Green's theorem. Unit 4 adds triple
integrals, surface flux, the divergence theorem, and Stokes' theorem.

Do not discard the workspace after learning each new integral. For each
object, fill out a geometric contract: dimension of domain, dimension of
range, parameter, differential element, orientation convention, units, and
applicable theorem. A line integral must distinguish accumulation of a
scalar along arc length from work by a vector field. A surface integral must
distinguish \(dS\) from oriented \(\mathbf n\,dS\). A coordinate change must
transform region, boundary, and Jacobian together. The hard failures are
often a reversed normal, inconsistent boundary direction, or a parameter map
that covers the object twice—not integration technique.

For EE, gradients and Jacobians support local sensitivity and
multi-parameter linearization of device or circuit models. Double and triple
integrals accumulate surface charge, volume charge, or energy density.
Line/surface integrals and divergence/curl form the mathematical interface
between integral and differential field laws. 18.02SC does not teach
constitutive relations, Maxwell boundary conditions, or device models. Those
connections are EEDIY transfer interpretations, not an official lab.

## Bring in Maxwell Only After the Mathematical Chain Closes

The official Unit 4
[Physics Applications](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-02sc-multivariable-calculus-fall-2010/pages/4.-triple-integrals-and-surface-integrals-in-3-space/physics-applications/)
labels Session 97 “Curl and Physics” and Session 98 “Maxwell's Equations,”
using Gauss and Stokes to connect integral and differential forms. Use this
as a theorem audit, not a claim of completed electromagnetics. For every
expression, record region, boundary, orientation, smoothness assumption, and
units, then name the integral theorem that performs the conversion. If only
the symbols \(\nabla\cdot\) and \(\nabla\times\) remain, return to Green and
line integrals in Unit 3 instead of memorizing four Maxwell equations.

One low-risk artifact may be an **EEDIY field-audit notebook, not an official
MIT assignment**. For two analytic vector fields over simple rectangular or
cylindrical regions, calculate divergence/curl, boundary integrals, and
region integrals, then use numerical samples to test orientation and the
Jacobian. Preserve analytic derivations, grid convergence, an orientation
flip test, software versions, and raw output. Numerical agreement does not
replace theorem hypotheses.

## Attempt the Four One-Hour Exams Before Reading Solutions

The official [Exam 2](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-02sc-multivariable-calculus-fall-2010/pages/2.-partial-derivatives/exam-2/)
shows the Scholar feedback structure: each unit has a one-hour exam with
review, practice material, the paper, and a solution; problem sets and
session problems in each part are also solved. Draw the object and
orientation before starting algebra, complete the work closed-book and
timed, then classify discrepancies as object, coordinate, orientation,
theorem condition, or calculation. An opposite sign still needs to be traced
to the exact normal or boundary reversal; “careless” is not a diagnosis.

After Unit 4, the official
[Final Exam](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/18-02sc-multivariable-calculus-fall-2010/pages/final-exam-1/)
is a three-hour cumulative exam, in addition to the 4 one-hour unit exams. The
public edition provides papers and solutions but does not list an off-campus
automatic grader or MIT grade. If a Java Mathlet fails in a current browser,
mark it legacy optional and substitute hand-drawn cross-sections or local contour/vector
plots. A working plugin does not prove that you understand the integral.
