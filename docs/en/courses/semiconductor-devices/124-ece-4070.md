### Course fit

The public Cornell ECE 4070 *Physics of Semiconductors and Nanostructures* offering is Spring 2012. [Course details](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-4070-course-details) describes a senior undergraduate course with 3 lectures and 1 section per week, 2 prelims and a final, and no required text. It moves from free electrons, lattices, and band structure through statistics, phonons, transport, heterostructures, ballistic transport, optics, and thermoelectrics, with MOSFET and amplifier material inserted into the theoretical sequence.

This is not a fabrication, TCAD, or characterization lab; projects/labs are marked unavailable. Before entering, normalize a 1D infinite well, derive 1D/2D/3D density of states, and explain effective mass from `E(k)` curvature. Otherwise review quantum mechanics, state counting/Fermi–Dirac statistics, or reciprocal lattices as appropriate.

### Course structure

The [Handout index](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-4070-course-details/ece-4070-lectures-notes-handouts-2/) has Handouts 1–32 plus 3 advanced topics. Handouts 1–8 run from Drude, Sommerfeld, and the 3D/2D/1D Fermi gas to lattices, Bloch states, and LCAO. Handout 9 inserts MOSFETs, Handouts 10–12 use tight binding for polyacetylene, Ge, graphene, Si, and GaAs, and Handout 13 inserts amplifiers.

Handouts 14–24 return to statistics, effective mass, semiclassical dynamics, phonons, Boltzmann transport, drift-diffusion, and doping. Handouts 25–28 cover heterostructures, quantum wells/wires/dots, ballistic transport, and Landauer theory; Handouts 29–32 close with optical transitions, dielectric response, nanotubes, and thermoelectrics. Following this leave-and-return structure is more faithful than pretending the archive is a linear textbook.

### Use HW 1–11 and the two prelims for feedback

The [Homework and exams](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-4070-course-details/homework-and-exams/)
page provides HW 1–11 with solutions and Exam 1 and Exam 2 with papers and
solutions; the Final has only a paper. Attempt the homework independently,
then use the official answer to find the first physical divergence in
dispersion, DOS, carrier statistics, transport, or an approximation. The two
prelims calibrate the first and second halves. Treat the unsolved Final as
transfer work rather than manufacturing a score.

At every layer, trace `dispersion → DOS → carrier count → current`. Effective mass, temperature, degeneracy, and normalization should lead back to the band model. Preserve failures when grid refinement does not converge; smoothing cannot repair a normalization error.

A numerical artifact can implement a tight-binding chain or quantum-well
subband model. Fix the energy zero, lattice constant, states per unit cell,
and spin/valley degeneracy; compare 1D/2D/3D DOS and generate conductance
steps through the Landauer relation. After changing grid, domain, and boundary
conditions, the discrete state count should approach the continuum limit. A
band-edge singularity that drifts arbitrarily with the grid signals a
normalization or boundary error, not a new material effect. This numerical
extension is independent study, not an official Cornell project; the public
route also does not enter cleanroom, chemical-etch, vacuum, or high-field
experiments.

Choose Cornell for breadth across solid-state physics, phonons, transport, and optics; start with MIT for a gradual path from a quantum box to current.
