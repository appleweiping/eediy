### Course fit

MIT [6.823 Fall 2005](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-823-computer-system-architecture-fall-2005/)
studies constraints among ISAs, pipelines, memory hierarchies, protection, and
parallel execution. The official
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-823-computer-system-architecture-fall-2005/pages/syllabus/)
requires 6.004 or equivalent: you should read combinational and sequential
logic, trace assembly, exceptions, and caches, and follow a program into a
datapath. Hennessy and Patterson edition mappings appear in the
[readings](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-823-computer-system-architecture-fall-2005/pages/readings/).
Keep 2005 product figures separate from enduring mechanisms for dependencies,
ordering, and coherence.
Choose it after digital logic and computer organization when the goal is
microarchitectural tradeoff reasoning, not a first encounter with a CPU.

### Course structure

The [lecture notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-823-computer-system-architecture-fall-2005/pages/lecture-notes/)
move from ISAs and microprogramming through pipelines, virtual memory and
caches, protection and I/O, and then superscalar, VLIW, vector,
multithreaded, and SMP machines. For every topic, draw a structure, state
an invariant, and give a counterexample: which RAW hazards forwarding
removes and when a stall remains, how a store buffer preserves observable
order, or why a TLB miss differs from a page fault.

The [assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-823-computer-system-architecture-fall-2005/pages/assignments/)
train the move from specification to machine behavior. Follow dependency
order: carry an instruction from ISA into single- and multicycle control,
draw pipelines cycle by cycle, trace concrete addresses through CPU, TLB,
cache, page table, and memory, and only then study dynamic scheduling,
prediction, and multiprocessors. A small cache or branch trace simulator is
useful only after it agrees with hand traces.

### Assignments and feedback

The [exam archive](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-823-computer-system-architecture-fall-2005/pages/exams/)
provides 5 quizzes of 90 minutes with extensive solutions. First reconstruct
microarchitectural state without a clock, then repeat under closed-book time.
Classify corrections as specification, cycle boundary, address bits, ordering,
or arithmetic.

For each module, make an initial attempt, corrected version, and design
comparison stating performance, complexity, and correctness conditions for
the compared implementations. At the end, explain which workloads benefit from an
optimization, what simplicity it removes, and which mechanism restores
observable behavior. That judgment is much closer to 6.823 than reciting the
names of superscalar or cache-coherence mechanisms.

A useful stress test is a counterfactual: remove forwarding, shrink the reorder window,
or change memory latency. Predict the state trajectory and bottleneck before
checking with a simulator. If the direction is wrong, return to dependencies,
queue pressure, and ordering rules instead of merely revising the chart's
explanation.
