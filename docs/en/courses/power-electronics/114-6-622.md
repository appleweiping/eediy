### Prerequisites

MIT [6.622 Spring 2023](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/pages/syllabus/) is David Perreault's graduate power-electronics course. Before starting, write the switching states and apply periodic averaging, volt-second balance, and charge balance; interpret poles, zeros, and stability in a linearized model; and check device stress through voltage, current, loss, and temperature rise. For a first encounter with converter topologies, use a lighter Power Electronics 1 course. Choose 6.622 to study rectification, DC/DC, magnetics, semiconductor loss, inverters, 3-phase systems, control, EMI, soft switching, and resonant conversion together.

### Course structure

The [Lecture Videos](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/resources/lecture-videos/) can be reorganized around five questions: how power is converted; how magnetics and switches constrain a design; how a power stage becomes controllable; when parasitics, EMI, layout, and gate drive invalidate the ideal model; and how soft switching and resonance overcome hard-switching limits. For every topology, record operating states, steady-state balances, ripple, peak and RMS stress, loss, and control range instead of stopping at an ideal waveform.

The [Problem Sets](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/resources/problem-sets/) page completely publishes PS0–PS10 with solutions. It also displays “Problem Set 11” without an accessible resource. Of Assessments 1–10, only Assessment 9 lacks a public solution. Attempt each under the original independent-work and reference restrictions before opening an answer. Check Assessment 9 by simulation, an independent derivation, or peer review rather than an unattributed answer.

### What the Buck Design Project Must Deliver

The official [Design Project](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-622-power-electronics-spring-2023/pages/design-project/) requires a buck converter power stage, input and output filters, semiconductor and thermal choices, magnetics, capacitors, and stable feedback. The report should include input/output, ripple, transient, thermal, and protection specifications; duty and ripple derivations; peak and RMS stress and loss; rating margins and heat paths; an operating-point small-signal model, compensator, gain and phase margin, and load step; plus boundary cases, EMI and layout risks, and model limits. The 2023 attachments explain that design; current part numbers and availability still require new checks.

As an unofficial comparison, the University of Siegen IAS team's [Fundamentals of Power Electronics course](https://github.com/IAS-Uni-Siegen/PE_course) is CC BY 4.0, citable by DOI, and includes buildable notes, solved exercises, 2024–2025 exams, and a KiCad/SPICE buck example. It is an independent course resource. Use it for public simulation and different problems and to compare the depth at which both courses treat rectifiers, DC/DC conversion, and device stress.

### Access and version notes

6.622 does not publish an off-campus hardware lab. Without isolation, current limiting, differential probes, thermal design, discharge procedures, and qualified supervision, do not build mains-connected, offline-rectified, high-voltage, high-current, battery, or energy-storage converters. The project can close entirely through mathematical design and simulation.

The best iteration uses the same buck operating point and pushes input or load
to a boundary: do volt-second and charge balance, peak/RMS device stress, the
compensated loop, and the load-step waveform still tell one story? Mark
worst-case conditions, observation points, startup paths, and protection
thresholds beside the important waveforms. If a plot says only “steady state
runs” but cannot explain loss, margin, or a failure mode, return to the part
choice or compensator instead of adding another nominal waveform.
