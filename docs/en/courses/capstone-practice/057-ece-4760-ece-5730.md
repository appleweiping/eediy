# Cornell University ECE 4760 / ECE 5730: Digital Systems Design Using Microcontrollers

### Three Cumulative Labs before the Full Embedded Project

Choose Cornell [ECE 4760/5730](https://ece4760.github.io/) when you want fixed-point DSP, DMA, graphics, control, and electromechanical interfaces to accumulate into one system. It is too much work for a few unrelated peripheral demos. The 3 labs all feed system integration. The current board is the RP2350-based Pico 2, while the site still carries RP2040 videos from 2025 and 2022 and a PIC32 archive from 2021. Concepts transfer, but board targets, pin mappings, SDKs, and registers do not.

The [Policy](https://vanhunteradams.com/Pico/CourseMaterials/Policy.html) expects ECE 3140 or permission plus C, electronics construction, and mathematics. A current demo makes those prerequisites concrete: emit GPIO/PWM on a Pico 2 and save an oscilloscope trace with time and voltage scales. Difficulty here usually points to CMake, datasheet-reading, or measurement gaps.

### Labs and projects

Each lab occupies about 3 weeks, followed by a 4–5 week final project.

- [Birdsong](https://vanhunteradams.com/Pico/Birds/Birdsong.html) combines DDS, fixed point, an SPI DAC, keypad, and debounced state machine to synthesize bird calls around 2–7 kHz. Evidence should include spectrum, sweep timing, and interaction.
- [Digital Galton Board](https://vanhunteradams.com/Pico/Galton/Galton.html) simulates collisions and a binomial distribution on 60 fps VGA while DMA plays sounds. Report frame rate, throughput, and distribution error together.
- [1D Helicopter](https://vanhunteradams.com/Pico/Helicopter/Helicopter.html) integrates an I2C IMU, complementary filter, PWM, isolation, and PID. Verify signs and saturation against a plant model or recorded IMU data before touching the physical rig.

The first two labs establish fixed-point arithmetic, DMA, display, and observability. Skipping directly to closed-loop control makes a hazardous mechanism the first place software failures appear.

Before connecting a Pico 2, physical sensor, or actuator, the [sensor-sampler exercise (not an official Cornell lab)](https://github.com/appleweiping/eediy/tree/main/examples/sensor-sampler) shows how timeout and cancellation separate a state machine from its target adapter. It simulates fault paths on a host and does not validate the RP2350, a physical sensor, or a bus.

### Rebuild the helicopter only with a proper test rig

The [Motor Circuit](https://vanhunteradams.com/Pico/Helicopter/MotorCircuit.html) drives the MOSFET gate from an isolated 12 V side, powers the motor at 5–6 V, and keeps processor and motor supplies and grounds separate. Never carry motor current through a solderless breadboard. Verify the 4N35, MOSFET, and flyback-diode orientation, and provide current limiting, mechanical travel limits, guarding, and an accessible emergency stop. Confirm the PWM stop state, open-loop direction, and IMU sign first. Without an oscilloscope, current-limited supply, and safe fixture, limit the project to a simulated closed loop.

### Final project

The [Final Project](https://vanhunteradams.com/Pico/CourseMaterials/Final_Project.html) begins with a 2–5 page proposal and proceeds through weekly reports, a demo, and a reproducible project page; the official estimate is about 16–20 hours per week for the final 4–5 weeks. Pick one sensor and one actuator, make their interface explicit, and define core, stretch, and fallback scopes, BOM, cost, and risk. The final page should include schematics, versions, raw measurements, scope traces, speed or accuracy, failed attempts, and attribution.

The public site provides lab writeups, demo code, and past project pages, but not Canvas, course hardware, staff checkoffs, or in-person supervision. Fixed-point work from Birdsong, concurrency from the Galton Board, or a control limitation from the Helicopter should materially change the final project; otherwise the three labs remain unrelated demonstrations.
