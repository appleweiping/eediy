## Then Decide: This Is an RF Systems Course, Not an RFIC Course

The public Cornell ECE 4880, *Radio Frequency Systems*, archive is pinned to Fall 2016. ### What Real Evidence the Six-Step Lab Ladder Needs

The [official projects and labs page](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-4880-radio-frequency-systems/ece-4880-projects-and-labs) lists 6 lab PDFs and no lab solutions. Lab 1 covers RF instrumentation and spectrum exploration. Lab 2 uses a VNA for two-port S-parameters, de-embedding, and filter characterization. Lab 3 measures splitters and directional couplers. Lab 4 studies RF amplifiers. Lab 5 covers oscillators and mixers. Lab 6 is a 2–3 week free-lab proposal requiring goals, a block diagram, implementation, and a test plan. That last handout mentions 600 MHz–2 GHz, about 30 dBm, and even jammers among examples; it is not an invitation to reproduce such work at home or in open space.

### Measurement Gates Before Lab 1

1. In a 50 Ω system, convert both ways among dBm, W, and Vrms; explain that dB is a ratio while dBm has an absolute power reference; and catch the mistake of treating voltage gain directly as power gain.
2. Given a two-port S-parameter matrix, calculate input reflection, transducer gain, or mismatch and explain motion on a Smith chart; then state why moving the reference plane requires de-embedding.
3. For a receiver chain, estimate a link budget, cascaded noise figure, and IIP3/IM3 while identifying where an image, LO leakage, or blocker lands.

Following the official syllabus, notes, and homework/lab indexes cited above, if task 1 loses units, review sinusoids, power, and logarithmic units. If task 2 fails, return to ECE 3030, transmission lines, and network parameters. If task 3 merely adds gain without moving noise and nonlinearity reference planes, review Friis and intercept-point definitions. Then follow “review Lectures 1–5; attempt and correct HW 1–10; study Lectures 6–11 and Handouts 1–9; time Prelim 1/2; use the Fall 2015 Final as a transfer test.” Complete solutions do not erase the missing opening lectures or instrument feedback.

## Split Here into a Bench Path and a Simulation Path

Physical Lab 1–6 belongs only in an RF laboratory with authorized spectrum use, calibrated instruments, qualified supervision, and explicit power limits. Prefer a shielded enclosure, dummy load, attenuator, DC block, and instrument protection. Before power-up, check analyzer maximum input, amplifier stability, connector power rating, and emergency shutdown. Do not build an open-space jammer, unknown antenna, or high-power transmitter, and do not interfere with Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, navigation, broadcast, cellular service, or another person's measurements. The [current US FCC Part 15 text](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-47/chapter-I/subchapter-A/part-15) applies only in its jurisdiction; elsewhere, follow local regulation and institutional authorization.

Without a compliant laboratory, use a simulation-only route with synthetic signals, public or self-authored Touchstone models, controlled noise, and nonlinear blocks. Valid evidence includes a schematic or block diagram, simulation parameters, raw sweeps, unit tests, an error budget, and failed cases. It does not establish VNA calibration, spectrum-analyzer checkout, transmission from 600 MHz–2 GHz, or about 30 dBm power testing. The course also provides no public lab answers, equipment booking, staff checks, Simulink license, classroom critique, or grader. List each item in the completion statement.

Bench evidence cannot be instrument screenshots alone: record calibration plane, cabling and attenuators, range, sweep settings, environment, uncertainty, and anomalous runs. Simulation instead records model provenance, interpolation, tolerances, and failure ranges. These paths produce different evidence; one curve cannot impersonate the other.

### EEDIY Adds Only a Receiver Chain with No Transmit Path

The EEDIY supplement is an “auditable passive receiver-chain cascade model,” not official Cornell Lab 6. Represent gain, S-parameter mismatch, noise figure, IIP3, and phase noise in Python or Simulink, then sweep blocker power, source/load mismatch, and filter bandwidth. Independently check results with Friis and cascade equations. Deliver a block diagram with no transmit path, synthetic or clearly licensed Touchstone data, unit tests, raw sweeps, plots, and a validity statement. Acceptance requires swapping two stages and observing the theoretically expected noise/IIP3 change, rejecting non-passive or unit-invalid data, and rebuilding every conclusion from a clean environment with one command.
