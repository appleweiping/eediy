## All three threshold problems need coordinates and a reference sphere

Problem 1: starting with an electric or magnetic vector potential, derive the angular and radial dependence of \(E_\theta\) and \(H_\phi\) in the Hertzian-dipole far field and name the discarded near-field terms. Problem 2: given a normalized power pattern \(F_n=U/U_{\max}\), use \(\Omega_A=\int F_n\,d\Omega\) and \(D_0=4\pi/\Omega_A\) to obtain beam solid angle and directivity, and read the half-power beamwidth from the pattern. Explain why the absolute radiation intensity cannot be recovered without \(P_{\rm rad}\) or \(U_{\max}\), since \(P_{\rm rad}=\int U\,d\Omega\); then connect efficiency, gain, effective aperture, and the Friis equation. Problem 3: for a dipole, slot, or aperture, state the coordinate system, polarization, feed or reference plane, far-field condition, and boundary assumptions, and decide when transmission-line matching cannot replace full-wave analysis.

Complete at least Problems 1 and 2 independently before Lecture 1. If gain and directivity are treated as identical, a \(1/r\) field is confused with \(1/r^2\) power density, or polarization and reference planes remain undefined, review fields, transmission lines, and networks first. Retain a unit check, limiting case, reference-sphere radius, normal-vector sketch, and any polarization-basis transformation. These are EEDIY placement diagnostics, not NPTEL admission or certificate criteria.
