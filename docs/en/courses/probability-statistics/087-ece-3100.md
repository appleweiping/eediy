### Course fit and archive scope

Cornell ECE 3100 represents the Fall 2014 offering taught by Carl Poitras and is positioned at the 3rd-year undergraduate level. Its [course page](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3100-introduction-to-probability-and-inference-for-random-signals-and-systems/) gives an engineering-centered objective: model random phenomena, then use those models for estimation, inference, prediction, and decisions under uncertainty. The original class met for 3 lectures and 1 section each week, used the 2nd edition of Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis's *Introduction to Probability*, and had 2 prelims plus a final.

The public site does not cover the whole course. Cornell states that lecture notes, handouts, projects, and laboratories are unavailable. The surviving [homework and exam index](https://ocw.ece.cornell.edu/courses/ece-3100-introduction-to-probability-and-inference-for-random-signals-and-systems/ece-3100-homework-and-exams/) contains 9 homework sets, 2 exams, and 1 final.
