## This Is Not a Video-Only Circuits Course

MIT 6.002 begins with the lumped-circuit abstraction and moves from KVL, KCL,
node analysis, superposition, and Thévenin/Norton equivalents into MOS
switches and amplifiers, small-signal models, first- and second-order dynamics,
sinusoidal steady state, filters, op-amp feedback, power, and CMOS. That list
can make it look like a broad theory course. The official
[syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/pages/syllabus/),
however, includes measurement, construction, model comparison, and an
audio-playback system containing both analog and digital sections. Watching the
lectures is therefore not equivalent to completing the course.

The course shell is Spring 2007 with Anant Agarwal, while the notes,
demonstrations, and videos linked from the
[calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/pages/calendar/)
largely come from Fall 2000. Lecture 24 notes and video are unavailable, and
related resources are placed beside the Lecture 25 material. The sequence is
still useful, but the 2007
calendar and 2000 video numbering should not be forced into a false
one-to-one alignment.

### Choosing a neighboring course

6.002 connects circuit analysis, devices, dynamics, and bench work, so it is
broader and substantially heavier than
[Linear Circuits 1](022-linear-circuits-1.md) and
[Linear Circuits 2](023-linear-circuits-2.md). Choose
[MIT 6.091](../electronics-laboratory/025-6-091.md) when the immediate need is
basic bench and measurement practice. Choose 6.002 as preparation for later
analog electronics, signals, or integrated circuits.

## Prerequisites Need a Real Diagnostic

The official prerequisites are 8.02 Electricity and Magnetism and 18.03
Differential Equations. Before starting, check whether a learner can:

1. write KCL equations for a linear network containing a dependent source and
   check the sign convention in the power balance;
2. derive an RC or RL first-order response from its differential equation,
   initial value, final value, and time constant;
3. use complex numbers for phasors, impedance, and phase rather than only
   recalling series and parallel formulas.

A failure in the first check calls for node analysis and source
transformation; a failure in the second calls for first-order differential
equations; a failure in the third calls for complex numbers and phasors before
sinusoidal steady state. The electromagnetics prerequisite supplies physical meaning for
voltage, current, field, and energy, but circuit models become the working
language of the course.

## Do Not Treat Labs 0–4 as Appendices

The official
[labs](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/pages/labs/)
move from clean models toward real devices and instruments:

- **Lab 0** establishes bench and measurement habits so that instrument
  configuration is not mistaken for a circuit failure.
- **Lab 1** verifies Thévenin/Norton equivalents and builds logic gates,
  connecting an equivalent model to device behavior.
- **Lab 2** studies a MOSFET inverting amplifier and first-order circuits,
  comparing bias, small-signal prediction, and measured waveforms.
- **Lab 3** moves to second-order networks, requiring time-domain ringing,
  damping, and frequency response to tell the same story.
- **Lab 4**, prepared by the two-week Homework 11, builds an audio-playback
  system that joins analog filtering/amplification with digital control.

The syllabus labels its grade table an **initial weighting** and assigns labs
5%. It also says that failing to complete them lowers the course grade by a
full letter and that final letter grades undergo staff discussion. The 5% is
therefore not a guaranteed final formula; laboratory work is a completion
condition, not a minor bonus.

Without the original bench, reproduce each topology and limiting case in SPICE
before using a modern oscilloscope, multimeter, and signal source under
low-voltage, current-limited conditions. Those substitutions are an EEDIY
migration, not an MIT course fact. Power down before rewiring, set current
limits, and verify probe-ground reference; mains and high-energy storage do not
belong in this independent-study version.

## What Lab 2 Should Leave Behind

Do not stop when the inverter produces a waveform. Lab 2 should leave behind
four things: a hand prediction, a SPICE netlist, a current-limited measurement,
and an explanation of the difference. First predict bias, gain, and swing from
the operating point and small-signal model;
then use simulation to probe cutoff and saturation; finally measure the
waveform and explain deviations caused by device parameters, loading, probes,
or supply limits.

For a Lab 2 record, I would look for calculations with units and sign
conventions, a rerunnable SPICE netlist, a photo or diagram showing instrument
connections and settings, raw waveforms, and a
prediction–simulation–measurement comparison.
Lab 4 then adds module interfaces, a fault tree, and a short retrospective.
Without raw waveforms, a polished conclusion cannot show whether the model
worked or the result was accidental.

## Feedback, Exams, and Legacy Tools

[Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/pages/assignments/)
provides Homework 1–11 without solutions; Homework 11 is also the two-week
pre-lab design preparation for Lab 4.
[Exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/pages/exams/)
contains the two Spring 2007 quizzes, final, and several practice exams, again
without a uniform solution chain. Independent study should not pretend that an
official grader exists. Use dimensional analysis, KCL/KVL residuals, limiting
cases, SPICE, and measurement as cross-checks, and mark unresolved problems
instead of inventing an answer key.

The [course home](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-002-circuits-and-electronics-spring-2007/)
also points to WebSim, created in 2003. Its legacy HTTP entry and interactive
controls are unreliable in current browsers, so it is best treated as a
historical demonstration. If WebSim fails, restoring a 2003 browser environment
is not the course. Move to a modern SPICE tool that saves the netlist, model
version, and results, and record the substitution with the lab.
