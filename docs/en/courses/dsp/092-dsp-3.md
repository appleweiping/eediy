### Failed cases reveal how sampling works

[EPFL DSP 3](https://www.coursera.org/learn/dsp3) has 4 modules and 4
assignments covering sampling, aliasing, multirate processing, and A/D and
D/A conversion. The current page also names Fukushima, Rational Sampling Rate
Change, and One-Bit Music. It fits learners who know the DTFT, convolution,
and filter response and want to turn the sampling theorem into predictable
error. Review DSP 1/2 first if normalized and physical frequency still get
mixed.

### Pair Every Claim with a Counterexample

Track physical frequency, sampling rate, and normalized frequency together.
Demonstrate aliasing with distinct sinusoids that produce identical samples.
For rational resampling, expose `L/M`, the intermediate rate, low-pass cutoff,
and delay instead of merely calling `resample`. Separate ideal sinc
reconstruction from droop or images caused by a finite filter, window, and
D/A hold. One-Bit Music should report error power, spectrum, and overload
rather than rely on listening.

Apply undersampling, rational resampling, and low-bit
quantization to the same audio or sensor record. Preserve at least 2
failure-and-repair pairs: a tone crossing Nyquist into a low-frequency alias,
and an undersized resampling filter producing leakage. Predict each result
analytically, then change a single parameter to pass the metric.

### NumEx6/7 Belong to the Older Course

In the old [DSPNumex](https://github.com/LCAV/DSPNumex),
[NumEx6](https://github.com/LCAV/DSPNumex/tree/master/NumEx6) covers
interpolation and sampling, while
[NumEx7](https://github.com/LCAV/DSPNumex/tree/master/NumEx7) covers stochastic
processing and quantization. They help check notation and notebooks but are
not complete source files for the current 3 named labs. Coursera material,
grading, and feedback may be account-gated; state “no public counterpart”
when a mapping is absent. For rational resampling, put the physical-frequency
range and operation at every stage on one diagram. It quickly exposes a
frequency axis left at the old sampling rate, uncompensated filter delay, or
boundary samples compared at the wrong positions.
