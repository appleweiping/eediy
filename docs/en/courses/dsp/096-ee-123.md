### A Laboratory Course That Pushes DSP into a Radio Link

Berkeley [EE 123 Spring 2019](https://sites.google.com/berkeley.edu/ee123-sp19/)
was a 4-credit undergraduate course requiring EECS 120 or instructor
permission. It extends DTFT/z-transform, DFT/FFT, sampling, and filter design
into wavelets, 2D processing, and compressed sensing, then connects algorithms
to radio through RTL-SDR, Raspberry Pi, and AFSK/APRS. It fits learners with
stable theory and Python/Jupyter skills who can also manage hardware and
version drift. Choose a receive-only/offline route whenever transmit authority
is unclear.

### The Labs Expose the Link Layer by Layer

The [labs](https://sites.google.com/berkeley.edu/ee123-sp19/labs) move from
Lab 0 setup and sonar matched filtering through RTL-SDR/ADS-B,
spectrograms/FM demodulation, AFSK, and AX.25/APRS. The 2019 Drive snapshots,
OS image, Python packages, and SDR drivers may have drifted. Build from the
[Python page](https://sites.google.com/berkeley.edu/ee123-sp19/python) and
preserve versions plus compatibility diffs. Material with prerecorded data
can be reproduced offline, but it does not show that the full hardware labs
or original grader remain public.

### The 75-Second Project Tests Compression, Modulation, and Protocol Budgets

The [project](https://sites.google.com/berkeley.edu/ee123-sp19/project) asks
students to transmit the best possible video within 75 seconds using 2
handheld radios, while restricting direct use of ready-made compression and
communication implementations. The labs' AFSK1200 packet modem is the minimum
baseline. An outside artifact should freeze the test video, payload, noise and
loss model, and PSNR before comparing compression ratio, ECC overhead,
BER/packet loss, and synchronization cost. Explain whether the bottleneck is
compression, modulation, synchronization, or protocol rather than merely
showing that video arrived.

### Keep Transmission and Offline Reproduction Separate

The campus amateur-radio organization and device settings belong to the
Spring 2019 U.S. context and do not grant authority elsewhere. Without legal
equipment, a license, and a responsible site, use recorded IQ/audio and a
simulated channel for an offline reproduction, fixing sample rate, bandwidth,
symbol rate, frequency offset, random seed, and the 75-second net payload. In
a lawful setting, add power, antenna, callsign, and authorization records.
Every route needs a reproducible baseline and failed version; recheck local
rules by location, date, and band before buying hardware or transmitting.

Its most revealing constraint is fitting the complete source-to-packet chain
inside 75 seconds. An offline channel can still expose the conflict among
compression, synchronization, and protocol overhead.
