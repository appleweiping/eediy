### Learn to Move from a Specification to a Filter

[EPFL DSP 2](https://www.coursera.org/learn/dsp2) has 3 modules, 3 graded
assignments, and 5 Python/Jupyter labs, moving from digital filters and filter
design into stochastic and adaptive processing. It fits learners who can
already analyze LTI systems with convolution, the DTFT/DFT, and complex
frequency response and now need design judgment about passbands, stopbands,
delay, and computation. Take DSP 1 first if those foundations remain formulas
to memorize.

### Bring all 3 parts back to one filter specification

Begin with an audio specification covering the retained band, permitted
distortion, transition width, and delay. Compare windowed and
Parks–McClellan designs by order, ripple, group delay, and multiplication
count. For nonlinear processing, use a two-tone input to expose harmonics and
intermodulation rather than mistaking clipping for ordinary stopband leakage.
For echo cancellation, record step size, convergence, residual echo, and
recovery after a reference-path change. “Sounds better” cannot replace
target-versus-measured values.

Do not invent a separate project for every part. Fix the input,
amplitude scale, and boundary convention, then put the specification, 2
designs, automated metrics, a failed case, and the selection argument.
Recheck poles after IIR coefficient quantization; for an adaptive filter,
fix the random seed and show tracking after mismatch.

### Old NumEx Material Serves as a Numerical Reference

In the official old [DSPNumex](https://github.com/LCAV/DSPNumex),
[NumEx5](https://github.com/LCAV/DSPNumex/tree/master/NumEx5) supplements LTI
work and [NumEx7](https://github.com/LCAV/DSPNumex/tree/master/NumEx7)
supplements stochastic processing. They are not organized as the current 5
labs and provide neither the platform grader nor complete feedback. Label
Coursera labs, old NumEx material, and personal implementations separately;
access to current graded work depends on the account plan. Repeat the same
specification on short and long sequences and at different amplitudes so
boundary handling, startup transients, and numerical precision are not
mistaken for filter performance. Only then does a failed case point back to an
actual filter-design choice.
