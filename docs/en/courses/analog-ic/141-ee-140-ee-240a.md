# University of California, Berkeley EE 140 / EE 240A: Analog Integrated Circuits

### Access and version notes

Berkeley EE 140 / EE 240A Spring 2025 shares lectures, homework, labs, and project. Undergraduates use Razavi's 2nd edition and graduates use Gray/Hurst/Lewis/Meyer's 5th edition, both commercial books. The [catalog](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Courses/ELENG140/) requires EE 105 and spans transistor amplifiers, op-amps, feedback, frequency response, bias and references, ADCs, switched-capacitor filters, comparators, a hardware lab, and a design project.

An open SPICE build cannot move the course home intact, yet public lectures and problems expose substantial analog-IC reasoning. Campus Cadence, configured SKY130, GSI review, and team integration form the boundary. Accepting it makes the useful outside subset easier to choose.

Test readiness with one MOS common-source stage: derive \(g_m,r_o\), and gain from bias; estimate dominant pole and step response from capacitance; judge loop gain, phase margin, and swing after feedback. A miss points directly to device models, pole-zero analysis, or stability.

### Assignments and feedback

The 21 PDFs move from diode/BJT/MOS behavior, small signal, active loads and current mirrors, differential pairs, and 2-stage op-amps into compensation, RHP zeros, bias, cascodes, switched capacitors, ADC/PGA, StrongArm, gain boosting, and noise. Region, \(V_{OV}\), gain and poles, swing and common mode, and power belong on the same analysis page. Otherwise a sizing change can “fix” gain while quietly ruining swing or power.

The [Spring 2025 course directory](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pister/140sp25/) contains HW1–HW10 and both original and v2 versions of HW7. Direct current solutions exist only for HW8 and HW10, plus Spring 2025 Midterm 2. Many older solved exams are linked, but differences in term, topology, and process parameters mean they are not solutions to all ten current assignments.

When simulation and hand analysis disagree, separate model simplification, schematic mistakes, and PDK behavior before resizing a transistor. Hold the testbench, source and load, analysis type, and measurement expression fixed; compare operating point, small-signal parameters, dominant pole, output swing, common-mode range, and power before and after. That habit is more portable than one “best W/L.”

### Tools and environment

Spring 2023 [Lab 1](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pister/140sp23/labs/lab1.pdf) starts with hand analysis and LTspice, then requires breadboard work and TA initials. Old [Lab 3 Part 1](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pister/140sp23/labs/lab3_1.pdf), whose header says Spring 2020, studies a 2-stage BJT op-amp, feedback, poles, slew, and compensation. Its direct [BJTopamp.asc download](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pister/140sp23/labs/BJTopamp.asc) makes DC operating-point and sweep work a genuinely runnable public subset. Frequency and compensation work, breadboard measurements, and GSI initials are not contained in that one `.asc` file.

[Lab 2](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pister/140sp25/labs/lab2.pdf) instead logs into eda-1 through eda-4 and uses `/home/ff/ee140/sp25`, `/share/b/bin/cds6`, and `sky130_fd_pr_main`. Lab 4–8 continue in Cadence/SKY130 with parameter and reference work, a folded cascode, regulator, switched-capacitor PGA, and StrongArm/SAR ADC. PDFs remain readable; campus accounts, licenses, libraries, teams, and evaluation do not come inside them.

### Labs and projects

The [project specification](https://people.eecs.berkeley.edu/~pister/140sp25/labs/project.pdf) asks a 3-person analog team to integrate an 8-bit SAR ADC, switched-capacitor PGA, bandgap and temperature sensor, analog multiplexer, and analog and digital regulators. Battery voltage falls from 3.2 V toward 1.6 V, and temperature spans 0–70 °C.

The difficulty is not making one block work alone. Folded-cascode gain and bandwidth constrain the PGA and regulator; bandgap noise reaches the ADC; clocked switches and charge injection alter sampled values; battery droop squeezes headroom. After a local improvement, return to the system waveform and distinguish reference settling, PGA sampling, comparator decision, and digital handoff. Calling every anomaly “ADC noise” hides the interface.

### Outside Berkeley, choose one block and ask how much specification survives

Without Berkeley servers and configured Cadence/SKY130, choose a folded cascode, bandgap, PGA, or StrongArm and build an alternative testbench with a clearly licensed PDK, model, and simulator. It can teach the same `spec → hand analysis → schematic → corner/temperature → waveform → decision` reasoning. It does not inherit Berkeley's device models, corners, parasitics, or campus evaluation.

The tempting mistake is to turn “the netlist runs” into “the processes are equivalent.” A better question is how many original requirements survive a different device model, corner, parasitic, noise model, and interface, and why. Cornell ECE 3150 is a better first source of complete public feedback. EE 140/240A offers deeper mixed-signal interfaces after the Cadence boundary is accepted.

One successful waveform does not establish 1.6–3.2 V, 0–70 °C, and full-system integration. If the testbench conditions and measurement expression are unclear, even the quantity on the plot cannot be identified.
