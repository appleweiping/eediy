### Access and version notes

MIT OpenCourseWare's [6.691 Seminar in Electric Power Systems](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-691-seminar-in-electric-power-systems-spring-2006/) is a graduate seminar from Spring 2006 that met 2 times per week for 1.5 hours per meeting. It suits learners with undergraduate power-systems foundations who want assignments and a project to connect network analysis, dynamics, and dispatch. The [syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-691-seminar-in-electric-power-systems-spring-2006/pages/syllabus/) calls its plan a “straw man” that was expected to change. The structure remains useful, while market rules, prices, and event context from 2006 belong to a historical version.

The prerequisite is a power-systems background such as MIT 6.061. Strong electrical-network theory together with controls and electromagnetics is another stated route, and economics is helpful. This course suits project synthesis rather than replacing a first complete undergraduate power-systems course.

### Course structure

The opening sequence moves from complex power, polyphase systems, transmission lines, and 2-port representations to transformers, load flow, and Newton–Raphson. The middle sequence covers synchronous machines, dynamics, regulators, stabilizers, symmetrical components, faults, and protection. A steady-state load flow establishes the operating point; a dynamic model describes motion after a disturbance. The final sequence examines generation cost, economic dispatch, and optimal dispatch, offering practice with objectives, constraints, marginal cost, and network limits.

That range is the strength of the 20-lecture seminar, but the 2006 material alone cannot support a current conclusion about markets or regulation. Power-flow equations, sequence networks, and the swing equation can be exercised directly. An extension involving markets, protection practice, or event interpretation needs current primary material from an ISO/RTO, regulator, or utility.

### The Assignments Are More Complete Than the Notes

The [Assignments page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-691-seminar-in-electric-power-systems-spring-2006/pages/assignments/) publishes 7 problem sets and 7 solutions, drawing from the 1999 Bergen and Vittal text. Write the model and sign conventions before consulting a solution. The real advantage of the set is placing networks, machines, faults, and dispatch inside the same course.

The [Lecture Notes page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-691-seminar-in-electric-power-systems-spring-2006/pages/lecture-notes/) retains limited material rather than full notes for all 20 lectures, so a modern power-system analysis textbook should provide the narrative spine. The [Tools page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-691-seminar-in-electric-power-systems-spring-2006/pages/tools/) supplies 2 MATLAB files: a dynamic subroutine for `ode23` and a main script. Reproduce the response, then vary parameters to inspect damping, equilibrium, or instability rather than treating the small scripts as a modern grid simulator. The syllabus mentions that 2 quizzes might be used, but the public package contains no quiz papers.

### Use the Project to Answer an Open Seminar Question

The [Projects page](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-691-seminar-in-electric-power-systems-spring-2006/pages/projects/) asks an individual or a team of 2–3 to study a planning or operations problem and produce a conference-paper-size report and presentation. It retains 2 examples, “Ship to Shore Power” and “August 2003 Blackout.” A worthwhile reconstruction needs a bounded question, traceable data, and at least 1 sensitivity analysis.

A historical report should not merely receive a new title. A study of the
August 2003 blackout should separate what happened then from inferences under
today's network conditions; a shore-power study should expose load, network
constraints, emission factors, and economic assumptions separately. The
valuable question is which sensitivity changes the conclusion after the old
model receives a clearly stated new set of assumptions.
