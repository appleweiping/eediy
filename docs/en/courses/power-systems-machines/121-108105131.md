### Treat it as a second course in electrical machines

IIT Kharagpur / NPTEL [Electrical Machines II, course 108105131](https://nptel.ac.in/courses/108105131), taught by Tapas Kumar Bhattacharya, lists Basic Electrical Technology in its [official 12-week syllabus](https://archive.nptel.ac.in/content/syllabus_pdf/108105131.pdf): circuit analysis plus transformer operating principle and equivalent circuit. This is not a first machines course. Review circuits, magnetic circuits, and transformers if you cannot solve a loaded-transformer equivalent circuit, use complex power, and sketch the spatial MMF of a distributed winding.

Also fix polyphase sequence, line/phase conversion, RMS, and motor/generator sign convention before starting. Many factor, phase, and torque-sign errors originate there.

### Follow the field–circuit–torque–dynamics thread for 12 weeks

Weeks 1–4 move from transformers, machine construction, and torque to MMF, air gaps, distributed windings, electrical/mechanical angle, rotating fields, and induced voltage. Explain the rotating field with a winding-space diagram before using an equivalent circuit. Weeks 5–7 are the induction-motor core: slip, torque–slip behavior, speed control, and parameter estimation from no-load and locked-rotor tests. Validate a fit by predicting current, power factor, and torque at another operating point.

Week 8 covers single-phase motors, double-revolving-field theory, and capacitor starting. Weeks 9–10 turn to synchronous-machine EMF, reactance, synchronization, voltage regulation, power angle, and excitation. Week 11 introduces d/q analysis of salient-pole machines; Week 12 develops the swing equation, equal-area criterion, and stability. The final synthesis should connect rotating MMF, torque–speed behavior, a synchronous phasor, and the pre-fault operating point.

Classify them by induction, single-phase, synchronous, and stability topics; a file number is not a teaching week, and old deadlines or platform state are not current facts.

Solve by hand before checking ratings, slip sign, efficiency, and power balance. Parameter-estimation work should show conversion from polyphase line values to per-phase quantities, whether locked-rotor frequency equals rated frequency, and how copper, core, and mechanical losses are assigned. Name the synchronous-machine regulation approximation. The public layer has neither a complete modern problem bank nor a current grader; synthetic data validates only the calculation.

### The notebook covers models; machine tests stay in a laboratory

Build a spreadsheet or Python/MATLAB notebook that estimates parameters from no-load and locked-rotor data, plots torque–speed behavior, varies rotor resistance, voltage, and frequency, and replays a simple disturbance with the swing equation. Check torque as slip approaches 0, invariance of synchronous speed to rotor resistance, input-power balance, and energy direction before and after fault clearing. Put rated bases, parameter sources, and sign convention beside every plot.

This is neither an official coding lab nor a substitute for physical tests. Rotating machines involve mains voltage, starting current, mechanical inertia, exposed terminals, and guarding, so operate them only in a protected, supervised laboratory. Exit with independent solutions to the visible archive, a reproducible parameter notebook, and a synthesis diagram from rotating field to swing dynamics.

As a second machines course, its value is connecting rotating fields,
equivalent circuits, torque, and swing dynamics into a single dynamic story.
