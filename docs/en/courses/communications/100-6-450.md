### Course fit

MIT [6.450 Fall 2006](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-450-principles-of-digital-communications-i-fall-2006/pages/syllabus/) is a graduate introduction that assumes 6.011 plus probability and linear systems. Its real subject is an end-to-end chain: a source distribution determines a code or quantizer, the coded symbols become waveforms, the channel turns those waveforms into sufficient statistics, and a detector makes the decision. For a first hands-on encounter with modulation, physical links, and packets, 6.02 is the better starting point. 6.451, in turn, treats 6.450 as preparation for modern coding.

### How the notes and videos fit together

The [Lecture Notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-450-principles-of-digital-communications-i-fall-2006/pages/lecture-notes/) are a public 9-chapter manuscript, not per-session slides. They move from source coding and quantization through signal spaces, Nyquist criteria, PAM/QAM, WGN, and detection, then reach delay and Doppler spread, diversity, RAKE, and CDMA. The 24 [Video Lectures](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-450-principles-of-digital-communications-i-fall-2006/video_galleries/video-lectures/) come from 2003, while the text and assessment pages describe the 2006 offering. Writing both years at the top of the notes prevents a missing session from looking like a broken resource.

The signal-space detector is the derivation worth repeating. Take BPSK or QPSK, begin with an orthonormal-basis representation, keep \(E_s\), \(E_b\), noise spectral density, and decision regions in one normalization, and derive the error probability after projecting WGN. Many apparent 3 dB disagreements between simulation and theory come from complex-noise variance, bilateral spectral density, or bit-to-symbol conversion rather than from the communication model.

### Assignments and feedback

The syllabus names 14 problem sets, but public [Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-450-principles-of-digital-communications-i-fall-2006/pages/assignments/) retains only Assignments 1–10 and no solutions. The [Exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-450-principles-of-digital-communications-i-fall-2006/pages/exams/) page has 5 quiz/solution pairs but not the planned final. The problem sets work well alongside the manuscript and the quizzes make useful timed checks; the archive cannot reconstruct Assignments 11–14 or a complete course grade.

### One useful numerical experiment

With a small independent program or the unofficial Python library [Komm](https://github.com/rwnobrega/komm), compare analytic and Monte Carlo BER for BPSK/QPSK in AWGN, then put the same detector in a flat Rayleigh channel. Label the definition of \(E_b/N_0\), mapping, sample count, and confidence interval on the result. The point is not another familiar waterfall curve. It is being able to say which assumption changed between source rate, waveform energy, and the decision event, and why the curve moved.
