## IAP 2026 Builds One Radio in Four Weeks, Not Eight KiCad Tutorials

The [course home](https://pcb.mit.edu/) currently describes MIT IAP 2026,
listed as 6.S092/EC.S03. Its path runs from system parameters, SPICE, and
schematics through layout, manufacturing output, surface-mount and
through-hole assembly, and debugging. One FM radio board—with an ESP32,
numeric display, two audio-amplifier paths, USB or battery power, and an
antenna/receiver front end—persists through the four weeks. The labs are not
eight unrelated example boards.

The [syllabus](https://pcb.mit.edu/syllabus/), updated 2025-12-23, recommends
6.200, 2.678/2.679, or equivalent basic circuit analysis: R/L/C, op-amps, and
elementary power circuitry. “All skill levels” is not “no prerequisite.” The
most course-native starting point is
[Lab 02](https://pcb.mit.edu/labs/lab_02/): use an AD8592 to turn the radio
receiver's 80 mVrms maximum output with 10 kOhm source impedance into
200 mA peak through an 8 Ohm speaker, while covering 500 Hz–10 kHz. If supply,
output-current, and stability limits cannot yet be found for the 5 V op-amp,
or the coupling capacitor's low-frequency effect cannot be explained, review
op-amps, RC circuits, and SPICE first. That exercise predicts the next four
weeks better than a generic prerequisite quiz.

## Eight Labs Produce One Design-to-Manufacturing Chain

The [2026 schedule](https://pcb.mit.edu/schedule/) lists Lab 01–08. Learners
first create a block diagram and schematic in KiCad or Altium, design an audio
amplifier in LTspice, fill out power, RF, receiver, display, and interface
sections over two labs, finish layout, BOM, and manufacturing checks over two
more, then assemble and debug. Lectures move from system design, components,
and topologies to layout, high-power/high-speed concerns, and
fabrication/debugging. A snow day appears on January 26, and the page later
jumps to Lecture 10 and 11. Do not infer eleven continuously available
recordings from the labels.

The actual artifact chain is: requirements and block diagram; LTspice
simulation with measurable bandwidth and current; hierarchical schematic;
unique, matched symbols and footprints; layout rules, return paths, and
mechanical outline; orderable BOM, ERC/DRC results, Gerber and NC drill;
assembly checklist; and staged power-up plus a fault log. Every later output
consumes earlier decisions. A PCB-shaped drawing without manufacturability or
test evidence does not complete the exercise.

## The Lab 02 Amplifier Follows the Board into Hardware

Lab 02 should not end at a simulation screenshot. Carry its selected gain,
capacitors, and current budget into the `AudioAmplifier` schematic. During
layout, follow the small input signal, high-current output, and their return
paths. After assembly, measure gain and clipping with a sweep or points across
500 Hz–10 kHz. When hardware and LTspice differ, check the model, source
impedance, speaker equivalent load, and probe setup before replacing the
op-amp. One set of assumptions thus survives simulation, schematic, layout,
and bring-up, which is the point of the course sequence.

[Lab 06](https://pcb.mit.edu/labs/lab_06/) is where manufacturing output is
prepared. For relevant BOM entries, verify a real manufacturer part number
and package, then check applicable voltage, temperature, current, power, or
tolerance ratings. Sample connector and IC footprints against their
datasheet land patterns. Not every BOM row mechanically has both a “voltage”
and a “temperature” field. ERC/DRC green marks only mean that the configured
rules found no remaining report; they do not replace block-by-block schematic
review, return-path inspection, connector stress, test points, or manual
Gerber/NC-drill inspection.

I would select KiCad for an independent run because it is free,
cross-platform, and usable offline. Select Altium only with a legitimate
educational or work license and a Windows environment. LTspice supports the
specified audio-amplifier and RF small-signal checks. Do not copy Altium
screens while guessing equivalent KiCad commands. Choose one toolchain and
submit its version, library provenance, dimensional evidence for custom
footprints, and ERC/DRC configuration. Software can be free; parts, boards,
stencils, shipping, and rework are not. The public site does not publish one
current outside-learner total price.

## Public Study Forks at “My Layout” versus “The Course Assembly Board”

Registered students receive a lab seat, feedback, and project PCB materials.
More importantly, [Lab 07](https://pcb.mit.edu/labs/lab_07/) says that, to
manage a four-week IAP, staff designed and fabricated a board that is nearly
identical electrically to the student design and supplies that board and its
parts for assembly. Assembling the course board therefore does not prove that
the learner’s earlier layout was fabricated. Conversely, ordering a personal
Gerber does not provide the staff board’s BOM, stencil, or validated
firmware.

The two power arrangements must also remain distinct. In Lab 03, students
design an automatic TPS2115A power mux: VUSB and `5V_BATT` are its two inputs,
`5.0V` is the output, and the target current limit is about 2 A. The staff
board does not reproduce that block. Its three-pin J8 header uses a jumper to
connect `5V` to either `BATT` or `USB`. First power follows visual and
continuity checks, then a bench supply set to 5 V with a 0.5 A current limit.
Test the BATT and USB paths separately, power off before moving the jumper or
leads, and never connect both inputs at once.

An outside learner should make the fork explicit. The **design route** ends
with a manually reviewed schematic/layout, BOM, Gerber/NC drill, and fab
quote; an inexpensive low-voltage test coupon can validate footprints and
soldering. The **physical route** creates a new procurement, DFM, bring-up,
and firmware plan for the learner's own board and accepts that the first
fabrication may fail. Current [Lab 08](https://pcb.mit.edu/labs/lab_08/)
links ESP32 firmware on MIT's internal GitHub. An outside learner cannot
treat that code as public and must implement minimal power, display, and
receiver tests or record the access limit.

Carry one known staff-board rework into Lab 08: each of the five 5 V display
control lines needs a 10 kOhm pull-up to 5 V before diagnosing the shift
register or display path. This is an erratum for that course board, not a
universal design rule. A personal design using a push-pull level shifter must
follow the selected part's datasheet instead of copying the rework wires.

Video access also differs. The schedule’s slides, recitation notes, and lab
writeups are directly readable, but its Panopto recordings redirected to MIT
Touchstone login during review. Archives may have recordings from earlier
offerings, yet their year, project, and labs cannot replace the current
version without labels. Use 2026 slides and writeups as the spine and mark
recordings “restricted” instead of filling the current course with old video.

## A Bring-Up Log Should Reconstruct Every Power-On

The final directory should let another reader find requirements and a block
diagram; LTspice files and waveforms; hierarchical schematic and PCB sources;
library and footprint provenance; BOM and alternates; ERC/DRC records;
Gerber, drill, assembly drawing, and fab quote. For a physical build, record
each power selection, current limit, rail measurement, unexpected heating,
defect photograph, and rework result in time order. Functional checks cover
USB/battery exclusion, 3.3 and 5 V rails, audio bandwidth, four buttons,
display, and reception rather than only a final playback video.

Assembly is beyond ordinary desk work. The same Lab 07 page requires eye
protection, fume extraction, and hand washing after solder-paste handling and
warns about burns from preheaters and hot air, metal exposure from leaded or
lead-free paste, and sharp tweezers. Enrolled students also complete
Electrical Safety Awareness. Without training, extraction, a heat-safe area,
and supervision, skip hot-air reflow and submit manufacturing-ready files.
Keep any self-selected board at or below 5 V and in passive or small-signal
scope; do not copy high-power or mains examples. Price the PCB, components,
stencil, shipping, and one rework cycle before ordering. Open course
materials do not make the hardware free.
