### 6.241J Carries State Space into Structure and Robustness

Choose MIT [6.241J](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-241j-dynamic-systems-and-control-spring-2011/) after linear ODEs, matrices, and Laplace transforms when the goal is controllability, observability, and MIMO; choose an introductory feedback course for PID. The Spring 2011 archive also covers minimal realization, system norms, robust stability and performance, and optimal and robust control design. The [Syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-241j-dynamic-systems-and-control-spring-2011/pages/syllabus/) schedules 2 lectures of 90 minutes and 1 recitation of 1 hour each week.

You should already know linear ODEs, eigenvalues, rank, orthogonality, quadratic forms, convolution, and Laplace/Fourier methods. For a diagnostic, take 1 second-order ODE, write 2 realizations, check controllability/observability, and explain the distinction among a pole, mode, and input-output response.

### Use the Notes for Derivations and Assignments for Correction

The [Lecture Notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-241j-dynamic-systems-and-control-spring-2011/pages/lecture-notes/) and [open textbook](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-241j-dynamic-systems-and-control-spring-2011/resources/open-textbooks/) are the core. For every theorem, record assumptions, conclusion, conditions actually used in the proof, and a minimal counterexample. In particular, distinguish internal, input-output, and asymptotic stability, and distinguish whole-state from modal controllability and observability.

[Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-241j-dynamic-systems-and-control-spring-2011/pages/assignments/) contains 11 sets. PS1–PS10 publish solutions; PS11 explicitly has none. The first ten support line-by-line correction after an independent attempt, while the last requires checks against the notes, theorem conditions, and constructed counterexamples. Label state, input, output, dimensions, and conclusions invariant under coordinate change. Software may verify eigenvalues and simulations; it cannot replace existence, rank, or stability arguments.

The [Calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-241j-dynamic-systems-and-control-spring-2011/pages/calendar/) also shows that robustness is not decorative wording. Lectures 11–14 move from system norms and feedback performance to the small-gain theorem and MIMO stability robustness; Lectures 21–24 cover stabilizing-controller parameterization, \(H_2\), and \(H_\infty\) optimization.

### Use One Plant for Modeling, Structure, and Performance

Choose a mass–spring–damper or motor. Derive it from physics, then unify continuous/discrete solutions and realizations; analyze controllability, observability, decomposition, and minimality; design state feedback, an observer, or a least-squares estimator; finally test parameter uncertainty, system norms, a small-gain margin, and MIMO performance as robust-stability and performance questions.

At every layer retain a hand-calculated small matrix, numerical result, units, initial condition, solver, and discrepancy. Do not change the plant, sampling period, and metric every week; fixed conditions reveal whether changes come from theory or experiment. The official course has no lab, so this notebook is a self-designed validation tool.

Maintain an assumption–conclusion–counterexample card for each major theorem: the system class for a PBH test, why minimal realization requires both controllability and observability, and the spectral condition behind a positive Lyapunov-equation result. Add a counterexample after correcting each assignment. Reciting a rank condition without explaining how an uncontrollable mode affects response is still incomplete.

Design observer and controller separately on the continuing plant, then validate them together. Vary initial estimation error, measurement noise, and one plant parameter independently, plotting true/estimated state, control effort, and output error. The nominal separation-principle result does not replace performance checks under saturation, noise, and model mismatch.

### Assignments and feedback

[Exams](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-241j-dynamic-systems-and-control-spring-2011/pages/exams/) supports 2 passes: first untimed with all conditions explicit, then redo errors closed-book after a week. OCW has no grader, TA feedback, or lab checkout, so this cannot be converted into an MIT grade.

Without software, explain why one pole cannot be moved, why one output cannot see a mode, when least squares is nonunique, and when uncertainty crosses a small-gain stability margin. Then return to the continuing plant and see whether the numerical behavior actually reveals those structures. Once structure, robust stability, and performance fit one model, move to more specialized \(H_\infty\), MPC, or nonlinear control.
