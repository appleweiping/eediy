### Course fit

MIT [6.245](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-245-multivariable-control-systems-spring-2004/) is the Spring 2004 graduate course in multivariable control. It fits learners who have completed classical SISO control and linear systems and now want to understand robust control. The [Syllabus](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-245-multivariable-control-systems-spring-2004/pages/syllabus/) lists 6.302 and 6.241 (or 16.30) as prerequisites, then covers Youla/Q parameterization, LQ, \(H_\infty\), \(\mu\)-synthesis, LMIs, and model reduction.

The real step up is replacing single-loop gain and phase intuition with directional reasoning. A cross-coupled \(2\times2\) transfer matrix exposes the gap: its element-wise gains may look mild while the input direction associated with the largest singular value destabilizes 2 SISO loops when they are closed together. If controllability, observability, or internal stability still requires repeated definition checking, 6.241 is the better place to pause.

### Course materials

The [Lecture Notes](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-245-multivariable-control-systems-spring-2004/pages/lecture-notes/) form the main path, while the [Calendar](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-245-multivariable-control-systems-spring-2004/pages/calendar/) gives a clear sequence: MIMO representation and stability, parameterization and performance, LQ/\(H_\infty\), then uncertainty, \(\mu\), LMIs, and reduction. The notes matter for more than formulas; they repeatedly force a precise choice of plant, uncertainty, performance output, and controller class.

The [Assignments](https://ocw.mit.edu/courses/6-245-multivariable-control-systems-spring-2004/pages/assignments/) use the period's MATLAB/Simulink and Control System, LMI, and \(\mu\) toolboxes. Current MATLAB is closest to the original environment. Python or Octave can cover part of the computation, but old functions and \(\mu\) tools may not produce identical numbers. For self-study, keep the plant, weights, and performance question rather than every 2004 command.

### Coursework

The official grade consisted of homework at 50% and 2 quizzes at 25% each, with no final; OCW does not publish the quiz papers or solutions. A good thread through the course is one two-input, two-output plant: begin with 2 independent SISO loops, restore the coupling, compare \(H_\infty\) weights with a \(\mu\) uncertainty structure, and then see how reduction changes peak sensitivity, control effort, and tracking. The most instructive outcome is often a failed design because it reveals which weight, coupling direction, or reduction choice conflicts with the original specification.
