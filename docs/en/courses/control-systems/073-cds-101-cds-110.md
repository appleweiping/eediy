### Course fit and archive status

Åström and Murray’s [Feedback Systems 2e](https://fbswiki.org/wiki/index.php/FBS) provides the full 2020-07-24 text. Its 15 chapters move from feedback principles, modeling, and dynamics through state/output feedback, PID, frequency design, robust performance, fundamental limits, and architecture. An [archived snapshot of Caltech's Fall 2003 homework page](https://web.archive.org/web/20040820025958id_/http://www.cds.caltech.edu:80/~murray/courses/cds101/fa03/homework.html) preserves HW1–HW8, a midterm, final, and solutions as historical coursework with feedback alongside the 2e chapter exercises. ### Take One Physical Object from Model to Analysis to Design

For cruise control or a DC motor, write nonlinear equations and label input, state, output, disturbance, and units. Linearize at an operating point, then connect state space, transfer function, and simulation interfaces while naming saturation, delay, and unmodeled modes lost at each simplification. The [Chapter 1–4 exercises and Feedback Principles](https://fbswiki.org/wiki/index.php/Feedback_Principles) provide cruise-control and related examples for this stage.

In Chapter 5–10, cross-check every pole/zero conclusion with the characteristic equation, step/Bode/Nyquist plots, and a limiting case. The official [Python repository](https://github.com/murrayrm/fbs2e-python) provides BSD-3-Clause figure scripts. Pin an upstream commit, Python, and `python-control 0.10.1`; reproduce a figure with an analytical expectation, then vary a physical parameter, a disturbance, and a noise setting separately.

In Chapter 7–12, compare state/output feedback with PID/loop shaping on the same object. Specify tracking, disturbance band, overshoot, control effort, sensor noise, and actuator limits before choosing gains. Retain the controller equation, closed-loop poles or loop shape, identical test cases, and a failure that exposes each route's boundary.

### Robustness needs a failure boundary

For Chapter 13–15, build an envelope of parameter intervals, unmodeled high-frequency dynamics, sensor noise, and actuator saturation, then sweep the nominal controller. Separate inadequate tuning from structural limits such as a nonminimum-phase zero, delay, or Bode integral. The failure map lists perturbation, mechanism, metric, accept/reject threshold, and redesign. If every case succeeds, the envelope is probably too narrow.

The [archived solutions-manual notice](https://web.archive.org/web/20100616051415id_/http://www.cds.caltech.edu/~murray/amwiki/index.php/Solutions_manual) says it is restricted to instructors. Without public answers, cross-check with dimensions, limiting cases, independent derivation, and small simulations; do not seek restricted mirrors.
